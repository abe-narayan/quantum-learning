## Motivation

A superconducting qubit is, physically, a tiny electrical circuit: an
inductor and capacitor, cooled in a dilution refrigerator to
millikelvin temperatures (colder than deep space) so that thermal noise
doesn't wash out the delicate quantum states. The engineering challenge
this lesson opens with is that a plain LC circuit is a quantum harmonic
oscillator (Approximation Methods' oscillator, revisited here as real
hardware), and a harmonic oscillator's evenly-spaced levels make a
*terrible* qubit, since a "0→1" drive pulse would just as happily drive
"1→2," "2→3," and so on.

## Engineering Development

### The Josephson junction: breaking the harmonic spacing

A **Josephson junction**, a thin insulating barrier between two
superconductors, replaces the plain inductor with a genuinely
*nonlinear* circuit element. Physically, the qubit is this whole
circuit, capacitor and junction together, on a chip:

<HardwarePlatformSchematic variant="superconducting" ariaLabel="Superconducting transmon qubit: an LC circuit with a Josephson junction replacing the inductor" />

This nonlinearity makes the energy levels
*unevenly* spaced (anharmonic), so a microwave pulse tuned to the
0→1 gap no longer resonantly drives 1→2 as well. This is precisely
what makes an artificial "atom" out of a circuit, isolating a clean
two-level qubit ($|0\rangle,|1\rangle$) from what would otherwise be an
infinite oscillator ladder. The two ladders side by side make the
difference visible directly, not just in words:

(Illustrative spacing, chosen to show the *pattern* of shrinking gaps at
higher $n$, not a measured device's specific anharmonicity.) In the
plain harmonic case, every gap is identical, so a drive resonant with
0→1 is equally resonant with 1→2. In the transmon case, the 0→1 gap
(highlighted) is measurably different from 1→2, which is exactly what
lets a microwave pulse address only the qubit subspace.

### Coherence time and gate speed

Superconducting qubits' defining tradeoff is relatively short coherence
times (typically microseconds, with energy relaxation $T_1$ and dephasing
$T_2$ both in this range for current devices) but very fast gates
(single-qubit gates typically tens of nanoseconds). The *ratio* of gate
time to coherence time, not either number alone, is what determines
how many operations fit before decoherence dominates; the Noise,
Decoherence & Scaling course develops this tradeoff quantitatively.

### Gate time from the Rabi model

A single-qubit gate is implemented by driving the qubit at its resonance
frequency for a carefully timed pulse. In the idealized resonant-drive
model, the population transfer follows exactly the two-level Rabi
formula $P_1(t)=\sin^2(\Omega t)$. This platform's `exactTwoLevelTransitionProbability`,
evaluated with equal energies ($E_i=E_f$, exact resonance), reproduces
this closed form to machine precision (already verified in
Approximation Methods' time-dependent perturbation theory context, now
reused directly for real hardware). A full population transfer
("$\pi$-pulse," implementing an X gate) occurs at
$t=\pi/(2\Omega)$, a genuine, computable gate time from a given drive
strength $\Omega$ (the Rabi frequency), not a hardcoded number.

## Worked Example

**A superconducting qubit is driven at Rabi frequency
$\Omega=2\pi\times25\text{ MHz}$. How long is a $\pi$-pulse (X gate)?**

$$
t_\pi = \frac{\pi}{2\Omega} = \frac{\pi}{2(2\pi\times25\times10^6)} = \frac1{100\times10^6}\text{ s} = 10\text{ ns}
$$

Confirmed directly: `exactTwoLevelTransitionProbability(0,0,Omega,t)`
at $t=\pi/(2\Omega)$ gives $P_1\approx1.0$ to machine precision. This
platform's own engine, reused unchanged from a purely mathematical
context, correctly predicts a realistic superconducting-qubit gate
time (tens of nanoseconds, matching the "fast gates" claim above).

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking a plain LC circuit alone makes a good qubit.** Its evenly
spaced levels mean any drive resonant with 0→1 is equally resonant with
every other adjacent transition. The Josephson junction's
anharmonicity is what makes isolating a two-level subspace possible at
all.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Comparing platforms by coherence time alone.** A short coherence time
paired with proportionally fast gates can still allow many operations.
It's the ratio (roughly, gate count before decoherence) that matters,
not either number in isolation.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Superconducting qubits are engineered nonlinear LC circuits; the
  Josephson junction's anharmonicity isolates a usable two-level
  subspace from an otherwise evenly-spaced oscillator ladder.
- Typical regime: microsecond coherence times, nanosecond gate times,
  meaning fast gates relative to decoherence, a real engineering advantage.
- Gate time follows directly from the Rabi model already built and
  verified in Approximation Methods, reused here unchanged for a genuine
  hardware calculation.
