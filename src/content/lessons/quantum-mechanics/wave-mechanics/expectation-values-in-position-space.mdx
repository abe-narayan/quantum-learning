## Motivation

The last course defined $\langle A\rangle = \langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$ for
a finite-dimensional observable $A$, computed as a sum over basis states.
This lesson makes the exact same definition concrete for the position
observable, where the "sum over basis states" becomes an integral: the
same continuum-limit move as the last two lessons, now applied to a
specific, extremely useful observable.

## Intuition

If $|\psi(x)|^2$ is the probability density of finding the particle at
$x$, then the *average* position, over many identical measurements, is
just the probability-weighted average of $x$, precisely the same
"multiply each outcome by its probability and sum" recipe from the very
first probability lesson of the last course, with a sum turned into an
integral.

## Mathematical Development

### Deriving $\langle x\rangle$ from the abstract postulate

Starting from $\langle x\rangle = \langle\psi|\hat x|\psi\rangle$ (with
$\hat x$ the position operator, formally defined in the next lesson as
"multiply by $x$"), inserting the position-basis expansion
$|\psi\rangle=\int\psi(x)|x\rangle dx$ and using
$\langle x|x'\rangle=\delta(x-x')$ collapses the double integral to a
single one:

$$
\langle x\rangle = \int \psi(x)^*\,x\,\psi(x)\,dx = \int x\,|\psi(x)|^2\,dx
$$

which is exactly the probability-weighted average described above, now
derived rather than assumed. The same derivation with $x^2$ in place of
$x$ gives

$$
\langle x^2\rangle = \int x^2\,|\psi(x)|^2\,dx
$$

and, more generally, for *any* function $f(x)$ of position,

$$
\langle f(x)\rangle = \int f(x)\,|\psi(x)|^2\,dx
$$

This is exactly how the platform computes `expectationPotential(V)` (the
case $f = V$) and, via a discretized Riemann sum over the grid,
`expectationPosition()` (the case $f(x)=x$).

### Position variance

Exactly as in the finite-dimensional case,

$$
(\Delta x)^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2
$$

measures how spread out the position probability density is.
`Wavefunction1D.variancePosition()` computes precisely this. For the
Gaussian wave packets this course builds constantly, $\Delta x$ *is* the
packet's "width" parameter by construction (verified numerically in this
platform's own test suite: a Gaussian packet built with width $\sigma$ has
`variancePosition()` equal to $\sigma^2$).

## Physical Interpretation

$\langle x\rangle$ is where you'd expect to find the particle on average;
$\Delta x$ is how much any single measurement is likely to deviate from
that average. A tightly localized wavefunction (small $\Delta x$) predicts
measurements clustered near $\langle x\rangle$; a broad one predicts wide
scatter. That's the same interpretation as any probability distribution's
mean and standard deviation, because that is exactly what these are.

## Worked Example

**For the top-hat $\psi(x)=1/\sqrt L$ on $[0,L]$, find $\langle x\rangle$
and $\Delta x$.**

$$
\langle x\rangle = \int_0^L x \cdot \frac1L\,dx = \frac{L}{2}
$$

by symmetry, as expected: the midpoint of a uniform distribution.

$$
\langle x^2\rangle = \int_0^L x^2\cdot\frac1L\,dx = \frac{L^2}{3}
$$

$$
(\Delta x)^2 = \frac{L^2}{3} - \frac{L^2}{4} = \frac{L^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta x = \frac{L}{2\sqrt3}
$$

matching the well-known variance of a continuous uniform distribution,
here derived from the Born rule, not imported from classical statistics.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Computing $\langle x^2\rangle$ as $\langle x\rangle^2$.** These are
generally different (equal only for a state with zero variance, which no
normalizable wavefunction actually has). Squaring the average is not the
same as averaging the square.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Forgetting the weight $|\psi(x)|^2$ isn't $\psi(x)$.** The expectation
integral always carries the *probability density* as its weight, which
for complex $\psi$ means $\psi^*\psi$, not $\psi$ or $\psi^2$.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $\langle f(x)\rangle = \int f(x)|\psi(x)|^2\,dx$, derived directly from
  the abstract $\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle$ postulate via the position-basis
  expansion.
- $\langle x\rangle$ and $(\Delta x)^2=\langle x^2\rangle-\langle
  x\rangle^2$ are the mean and variance of the position probability
  density, exactly as for any probability distribution.
- The platform's `expectationPosition()`, `variancePosition()`, and
  `expectationPotential()` are direct, discretized implementations of
  these integrals.
