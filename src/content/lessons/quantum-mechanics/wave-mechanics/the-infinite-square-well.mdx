## Motivation

This is the first potential this course solves completely and exactly:
a particle strictly confined to $0 < x < L$ by walls of infinite
potential energy. It's the cleanest possible illustration of a deep
principle: confinement forces quantization, worked out with nothing
beyond the time-independent Schrödinger equation and two boundary
conditions.

## Intuition

Classically, a ball bouncing between two rigid walls can have *any*
energy: bounce it harder, it just goes faster. Quantum mechanically, the
wavefunction must fit the box the way a guitar string's vibration must
fit its fixed length. Only certain wavelengths, and hence only certain
energies, are geometrically possible.

## Mathematical Development

### Setting up the equation

The potential is $V(x)=0$ for $0<x<L$ and $V(x)=\infty$ outside. Inside
the well, the time-independent Schrödinger equation (from two lessons
ago) reduces to

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''(x) = E\,\psi(x) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \psi''(x) = -k^2\psi(x), \qquad k \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}
$$

with general solution $\psi(x) = A\sin(kx) + B\cos(kx)$.

### Boundary conditions force quantization

Outside the well $\psi=0$ (infinite potential energy makes the region
inaccessible), and $\psi$ must be continuous, so $\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0$.

$\psi(0)=0$: $A\sin(0)+B\cos(0) = B = 0$, eliminating the cosine term
entirely.

$\psi(L)=0$: with $B=0$, this requires $A\sin(kL)=0$. Since $A=0$ would
give the trivial (zero, unnormalizable) solution, we need
$\sin(kL)=0$, which holds only for

$$
kL = n\pi, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots
$$

($n=0$ is excluded: it gives $\psi\equiv0$ everywhere, not a valid
state). This is the entire mechanism. **The boundary conditions permit
only a discrete set of $k$ values**, and since $E=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$, only a
discrete set of energies.

### The energy levels

$$
k_n = \frac{n\pi}{L} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}}, \qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots
$$

exactly `infiniteSquareWellEnergyLevel(n, L, mass)`'s formula (natural
units drop the $\hbar^2$). Unlike the harmonic oscillator's *equally
spaced* ladder from the last course, these levels spread apart
quadratically ($E_n\propto n^2$), a genuinely different spectrum, from a
genuinely different confining mechanism. The unevenness is easier to see
than to read off an equation:

The gaps between rungs widen sharply as $n$ grows ($E_2-E_1\ne E_3-E_2$),
the visual signature of the $n^2$ scaling this section just derived.

### Normalized eigenstates

$$
\psi_n(x) = A\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \qquad 0<x<L
$$

Normalizing: $\int_0^L A^2\sin^2(n\pi x/L)\,dx = A^2\cdot\dfrac L2 = 1$
(using $\int_0^L\sin^2(\cdot)\,dx=L/2$, a standard half-period average),
giving $A=\sqrt{2/L}$:

$$
\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)
$$

matching `infiniteSquareWellEigenstate`'s formula exactly (shifted to a
well centered at $x=0$ rather than starting at $x=0$, a pure relabeling of
the origin that changes nothing physical, as the What Is a Wavefunction
lesson's continuum-limit discussion already established).

### Orthogonality

Distinct eigenstates satisfy $\int_0^L\psi_n(x)\psi_m(x)\,dx=0$ for
$n\ne m$, a direct consequence of $\hat H$'s Hermiticity (proven in
general in Mathematical Foundations: distinct eigenvalues of a Hermitian
operator have orthogonal eigenvectors), confirmed here numerically by
`overlapProbability` between different-$n$ eigenstates in the engine's
test suite.

## Physical Interpretation

$n$ counts the number of "humps" in $\psi_n$ (equivalently, $n-1$
internal nodes, points where $\psi_n=0$ inside the well). More nodes
means more curvature means more kinetic energy, exactly why $E_n$
increases with $n$, visible directly by comparing the Explorer's ground
and excited state presets.

Real semiconductor devices build this exact confinement on purpose. A
**quantum well** is a thin layer of one semiconductor sandwiched between
layers of another with a higher bandgap, trapping electrons in a
potential well only a few nanometers wide. The discrete $E_n$ ladder
this lesson derives is the literal operating principle behind quantum
well laser diodes, used in fiber-optic telecom transmitters and
Blu-ray players: shrinking the well's width raises $E_n$ (this lesson's
own $E_n\propto1/L^2$ scaling), which is exactly how engineers tune the
emitted wavelength by design, not by chance.

## Worked Example

**For $L=10$ (natural units, $\hbar=m=1$), find $E_1$ and $E_3$, and
confirm $E_3/E_1=9$.**

$$
E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{2L^2} = \frac{n^2\pi^2}{200}
$$

$$
E_1 = \frac{\pi^2}{200} \approx 0.0493, \qquad E_3 = \frac{9\pi^2}{200} \approx 0.444
$$

$$
\frac{E_3}{E_1} = \frac{9\pi^2/200}{\pi^2/200} = 9 = 3^2 \;\checkmark
$$

matching the $E_n\propto n^2$ scaling directly, and matching the
Wavefunction Explorer's live comparison panel for these exact preset
defaults.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Including the cosine term.** $\psi(0)=0$ eliminates $B\cos(kx)$
entirely. A common error is keeping both terms and applying boundary
conditions incorrectly, or forgetting $n=0$ must be excluded (it isn't a
valid state, not merely the "ground state with zero energy").
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming a literal infinite wall in a numerical simulation.** A
literal infinity can't be represented on a computer or evolved with the
periodic-boundary numerical method this course uses. The platform uses a
finite but very tall wall instead, an honest, standard, well-controlled
approximation (see the Numerically Evolving Quantum States lesson).
</Callout>

## Summary

- Boundary conditions $\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0$, applied to the general
  solution $A\sin(kx)+B\cos(kx)$, force $k_n=n\pi/L$ and hence quantized
  $E_n=n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)$.
- Normalized eigenstates are $\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$,
  mutually orthogonal by Hermiticity.
- These are exactly the formulas implemented in `potentials.ts` and
  visualized live in the Wavefunction Explorer's infinite-well presets.
