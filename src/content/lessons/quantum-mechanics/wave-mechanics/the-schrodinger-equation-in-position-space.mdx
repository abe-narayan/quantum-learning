## Intuition

Energy in classical mechanics splits into kinetic ($p^2/2m$) plus
potential ($V(x)$). The Hamiltonian operator does the same split, with
$p$ replaced by the operator $\hat p$ derived two lessons ago. Substituting
that operator's explicit differential form into the abstract time-evolution
equation is pure bookkeeping: no new physical input required, just
algebra applied to something already proven.

## Mathematical Development

### The position-space Hamiltonian

By direct analogy with the classical energy $E = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}+V(x)$,
promote each piece to its operator:

$$
\hat H = \frac{\hat p^2}{2m} + V(\hat x) = \frac{1}{2m}\left(-i\hbar\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{\!2} + V(x) = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + V(x)
$$

using $\hat p^2 = (-i\hbar\,d/dx)(-i\hbar\,d/dx) = -\hbar^2\,d^2/dx^2$
directly (apply the derivative twice; the two factors of $i$ give
$i^2=-1$, cancelling the earlier minus sign into a plus, which combines
with the overall minus to leave $-\hbar^2$).

### Substituting into the abstract equation

Take $i\hbar\,d|\psi\rangle/dt = \hat H|\psi\rangle$ from the last course
and apply $\langle x|\cdot\rangle$ to both sides (i.e., work in the
position representation, exactly as every quantity in this course has
been). The left side becomes $i\hbar\,\partial\psi(x,t)/\partial t$;
the right side, using the explicit $\hat H$ above, becomes
$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial^2\psi/\partial x^2+V(x)\psi(x,t)$. Together:

$$
\boxed{\;i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\psi(x,t)}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\psi(x,t)}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\,\psi(x,t)\;}
$$

This *is* the time-dependent Schrödinger equation, derived here as a
direct substitution into an equation already proven from Hermiticity, not
introduced as a fresh axiom.

### Stationary states, revisited

The last course's Stationary States lesson showed that energy
eigenstates evolve by a pure phase, $|\psi(t)\rangle =
e^{-iEt/\hbar}|\psi(0)\rangle$, with $|\psi(t)\rangle$'s *probabilities*
exactly constant in time. In position space, this means: if $\psi_n(x)$
solves the **time-independent** Schrödinger equation

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi_n''(x) + V(x)\psi_n(x) = E_n\,\psi_n(x)
$$

(an eigenvalue equation for the differential operator $\hat H$, exactly
analogous to $H|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle$ for a matrix), then
$\psi(x,t)=e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}\psi_n(x)$ solves the full time-dependent
equation, and $|\psi(x,t)|^2=|\psi_n(x)|^2$ for all $t$: the numerically
verified stationarity the Infinite Square Well and Harmonic Oscillator
lessons build on directly.

## Physical Interpretation

The kinetic term $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''$ measures curvature: a
sharply-curving wavefunction (large $|\psi''|$) carries large kinetic
energy, exactly matching the earlier lesson's observation that a tightly
localized wavefunction has broad, high-momentum Fourier content. The
potential term simply weights each position by its local potential
energy, position-space's most literal ingredient.

## Worked Example

**Verify that $\psi(x,t)=e^{-iEt/\hbar}\psi_0(x)$ satisfies the
time-dependent equation, given that $\psi_0$ solves the time-independent
one with energy $E$.**

$$
i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = i\hbar\left(\frac{-iE}{\hbar}\right)e^{-iEt/\hbar}\psi_0(x) = E\,e^{-iEt/\hbar}\psi_0(x)
$$

$$
\hat H\psi = e^{-iEt/\hbar}\,\hat H\psi_0(x) = e^{-iEt/\hbar}\,E\,\psi_0(x)
$$

(using the time-independent equation on $\psi_0$ in the last step). The
two sides match exactly, confirming the ansatz.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="note">
**Confusing the time-dependent and time-independent equations.** The
time-independent one is an eigenvalue equation for $\hat H$ with no time
dependence at all. It determines the *stationary states* $\psi_n(x)$ and
their energies, which then get dressed with a phase factor to build actual
time-dependent solutions.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Dropping the $\hbar^2$ or a sign in the kinetic term.** The double
derivative and the two factors of $-i\hbar$ combine to a **positive**
$\hbar^2/2m$ coefficient with an overall **minus** sign in front, a
common transcription slip.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $\hat H = -\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dfrac{d^2}{dx^2}+V(x)$, from directly
  substituting $\hat p=-i\hbar\,d/dx$ into the classical energy split.
- Substituting into $i\hbar\,d|\psi\rangle/dt=\hat H|\psi\rangle$ gives the
  time-dependent Schrödinger equation, a derivation, not a new axiom.
- Stationary states solve the time-independent (eigenvalue) version and
  evolve by a pure phase, with probability density exactly constant.
- The Wavefunction Explorer numerically integrates the time-dependent
  equation directly; the accuracy of that integration is checkable via the
  live $\langle H\rangle$ readout against known analytical energies.
