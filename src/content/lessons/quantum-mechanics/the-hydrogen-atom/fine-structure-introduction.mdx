## Motivation

Every energy value this course computed ($E_n=-13.6\text{ eV}/n^2$,
independent of $l$ and $m$) came from solving the non-relativistic
Schrödinger equation with a spin-free electron. Real hydrogen has neither
property exactly: the electron has spin, and at the $\sim0.1\%$ level,
relativistic corrections matter. This closing lesson is honest about
that gap rather than pretending it doesn't exist, and previews (without
deriving) what the next level of approximation adds.

## Conceptual Overview

### Two corrections, roughly the same size

**Spin-orbit coupling.** The orbiting electron sees the proton
circulating around *it* (in the electron's own rest frame), a moving
charge, which is a current, which generates a magnetic field. The
electron's own spin magnetic moment then couples to this field, adding
an energy term $\propto\vec L\cdot\vec S$. This term explicitly breaks
the clean separation this course used between $\vec L$ (orbital) and
$\vec S$ (spin, from Angular Momentum & Spin); the good quantum numbers
become total angular momentum $\vec J=\vec L+\vec S$ eigenstates (a
direct application of Addition of Angular Momentum), not separate $L_z$
and $S_z$ eigenstates.

**Relativistic kinetic correction.** This course used the
non-relativistic $p^2/2m$ throughout. The next-order relativistic
correction (from expanding $\sqrt{p^2c^2+m^2c^4}-mc^2$ instead) shifts
energies by an amount that depends on both $n$ and $l$.

### What breaks, specifically

Both corrections scale as roughly $(\alpha)^2\times13.6\text{ eV}\approx10^{-4}\times13.6\text{ eV}$,
where $\alpha\approx1/137$ is the fine-structure constant, small enough
that this course's $E_n=-13.6/n^2$ is an excellent approximation for most
purposes, but large enough to be precisely measured spectroscopically
(historically, this is exactly how fine structure was first discovered).

## Honest Scope Statement

This course derived the leading-order, non-relativistic, spin-free
hydrogen atom completely and correctly: energies, orbitals, and their
degeneracies are all genuine, checkable results, not simplifications
that hide wrong answers. Fine structure is a real, separate
next-order correction this course does not derive; doing so requires
either full relativistic quantum mechanics (the Dirac equation) or
degenerate perturbation theory applied to $\vec L\cdot\vec S$, both
beyond this course's scope. What's presented here is the map of what's
missing, not the missing calculation itself.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking the $l$-independence of $E_n$ (Hydrogen Energy Levels) is
exact in real hydrogen.** It's exact only in the non-relativistic,
spin-free model this course builds; real hydrogen's spectral lines show
fine splitting that depends on $j$, not just $n$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming fine structure is a large effect.** At $\sim10^{-4}$ relative
size, it's a precision correction, not a qualitative change: this
course's $E_n=-13.6/n^2$ remains an excellent approximation for
essentially every purpose outside high-resolution spectroscopy.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Fine structure combines spin-orbit coupling ($\vec L\cdot\vec S$) and
  a relativistic kinetic correction, both scaling as $\sim\alpha^2E_n$.
- It breaks this course's exact $l$-independence of $E_n$: real
  hydrogen's energy levels split slightly by total angular momentum $j$.
- This course's results are the genuine, correct non-relativistic,
  spin-free hydrogen atom, a real foundation, not a simplification that
  needs to be unlearned, just one with a clearly stated next-order
  correction beyond its scope.
