## Motivation

This lesson doesn't build new machinery. It identifies machinery this
platform has used since its very first course as a special case of what
the last three lessons derived abstractly. Spin-$\tfrac12$ is $j=\tfrac12$,
no more and no less, and the Pauli matrices this platform calls $X,Y,Z$
are, up to a factor of $\hbar/2$, exactly $J_x,J_y,J_z$.

## Mathematical Development

### The identification

$j=\tfrac12$'s general angular momentum operators (Lesson 2's ladder
construction) are exactly $2\times2$ matrices. Comparing
`angularMomentumX(0.5)`, `angularMomentumY(0.5)`, `angularMomentumZ(0.5)`
directly against `PAULI_X`, `PAULI_Y`, `PAULI_Z`:

$$
S_x=\frac\hbar2X,\qquad S_y=\frac\hbar2Y,\qquad S_z=\frac\hbar2Z
$$

confirmed to match exactly (to floating-point precision) in this
platform's own test suite, not a coincidence or an analogy, but the
literal $j=\tfrac12$ instance of the general construction Lesson 2 built.
$S_z$'s eigenvalues are $\pm\hbar/2$ (i.e. $m=\pm\tfrac12$, exactly the
"$-j$ to $j$ in integer steps" rule with $j=\tfrac12$ giving the two
values $-\tfrac12,+\tfrac12$), and $\vec S^{\,2}$'s eigenvalue is
$\tfrac12\left(\tfrac12+1\right)\hbar^2=\tfrac34\hbar^2$, Lesson 2's
$j(j+1)\hbar^2$ formula, at $j=\tfrac12$.

### No single-valuedness restriction

Lesson 3's integer-$l$ argument relied entirely on a position-space
wavefunction's behavior under $\varphi\to\varphi+2\pi$. Spin has no
position dependence: $|{\uparrow}\rangle,|{\downarrow}\rangle$
(this platform's $|0\rangle,|1\rangle$) aren't functions of angle at all,
so that restriction simply doesn't apply, and $j=\tfrac12$, forbidden
for orbital motion, is exactly what's physically realized for an
electron's intrinsic spin.

### The Bloch sphere, reinterpreted

Every rotation this platform has ever applied to a qubit
(`rotationAboutAxis`, used since Qubits & Quantum States) is, physically,
a rotation of the spin-$\tfrac12$ angular momentum direction. The Bloch
sphere point *is* the expectation value direction
$(\langle S_x\rangle,\langle S_y\rangle,\langle S_z\rangle)$, normalized.
This was always true; this lesson just makes the physical meaning behind
a picture this platform has used since Course 1 explicit.

## Physical Interpretation

Every single-qubit fact already proven (Pauli matrix algebra, the Bloch
sphere, $R_x,R_y,R_z$ rotations, measurement probabilities) is,
physically, a fact about a spin-$\tfrac12$ particle in a magnetic field or
similar setting. This course doesn't rebuild any of it; it identifies
what was already built as the concrete physical system this whole course
has been leading to.

## Worked Example

**Confirm $\vec S^{\,2}=S_x^2+S_y^2+S_z^2=\tfrac34\hbar^2 I$ using the
Pauli matrices directly (with $\hbar=1$, this platform's convention).**

$X^2=Y^2=Z^2=I$ (each Pauli matrix squares to the identity, a fact from
Qubits & Quantum States). So
$S_x^2+S_y^2+S_z^2=\tfrac14(X^2+Y^2+Z^2)=\tfrac14(I+I+I)=\tfrac34I$,
exactly $j(j+1)=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac32=\tfrac34$, matching Lesson 2's
general formula at $j=\tfrac12$ exactly.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking spin-$\tfrac12$ needed a separate, independent derivation.**
It's the direct $j=\tfrac12$ instance of Lesson 2's general construction.
The Pauli matrices already built for this platform's qubit engine
satisfy the exact same commutation algebra, verified directly, not
merely by analogy.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Picturing a spin-$\tfrac12$ particle as literally spinning in space,
like a classical top.** The "spin" name is historical; there is no
position-space wavefunction associated with it (unlike orbital angular
momentum), so there is no literal rotational motion to picture, only
the abstract angular-momentum algebra, realized at $j=\tfrac12$.
</Callout>

## Practice Questions

1. Confirm $S_z|{\uparrow}\rangle=\tfrac\hbar2|{\uparrow}\rangle$ and
   $S_z|{\downarrow}\rangle=-\tfrac\hbar2|{\downarrow}\rangle$ directly
   from $Z|0\rangle=|0\rangle$, $Z|1\rangle=-|1\rangle$.
2. Using $S_\pm=S_x\pm iS_y$ and Lesson 2's general ladder formula,
   confirm $S_+|{\downarrow}\rangle\propto|{\uparrow}\rangle$ and
   $S_+|{\uparrow}\rangle=0$.
3. Explain why $\vec S^{\,2}$ commutes with $S_z$ (a general angular
   momentum fact from Lesson 2) but $S_x$ and $S_z$ do not. Connect this
   to which quantities can be simultaneously measured for a spin-1/2
   particle.
4. If spin-1/2 has no position-space wavefunction, what does
   $|\psi\rangle=\alpha|{\uparrow}\rangle+\beta|{\downarrow}\rangle$
   actually describe physically, if not "where" the particle is?

## Summary

- $S_x,S_y,S_z=\tfrac\hbar2X,\tfrac\hbar2Y,\tfrac\hbar2Z$: the literal
  $j=\tfrac12$ case of this course's general angular momentum operators,
  confirmed to match exactly, not merely analogous.

  restriction never applies, exactly why $j=\tfrac12$ is physically
  realized for spin but excluded for orbital motion.

  direction $(\langle S_x\rangle,\langle S_y\rangle,\langle S_z\rangle)$.
