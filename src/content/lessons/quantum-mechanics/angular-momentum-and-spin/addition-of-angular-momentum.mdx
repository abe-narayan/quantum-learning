## Motivation

Two spin-$\tfrac12$ particles, combined, don't have a single well-defined
total spin: their combination can act as spin $0$ or spin $1$,
depending on which specific combined state they're in. This lesson
derives that decomposition explicitly, and finds something remarkable
waiting inside it: the spin-$0$ combination is *exactly* a state this
platform built in an entirely different course, for an entirely
different reason.

## Mathematical Development

### The total angular momentum operator

For two spin-$\tfrac12$ particles, the total spin operators act on the
combined $4$-dimensional space via
$J_x=S_x^{(1)}\otimes I+I\otimes S_x^{(2)}$ (and similarly for $J_y,J_z$),
each particle's own spin operator, tensored with the identity on the
other particle, summed. This is a direct generalization of how single-
particle operators extend to multi-particle systems, already used
throughout Quantum Gates & Circuits for other operators.

### Four candidate combined states

Using the computational basis $|{\uparrow}{\uparrow}\rangle,
|{\uparrow}{\downarrow}\rangle,|{\downarrow}{\uparrow}\rangle,
|{\downarrow}{\downarrow}\rangle$ (this platform's usual 2-qubit
ordering), consider:

$$
|1,1\rangle=|{\uparrow}{\uparrow}\rangle,\quad
|1,0\rangle=\frac{|{\uparrow}{\downarrow}\rangle+|{\downarrow}{\uparrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad
|1,-1\rangle=|{\downarrow}{\downarrow}\rangle,\quad
|0,0\rangle=\frac{|{\uparrow}{\downarrow}\rangle-|{\downarrow}{\uparrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}
$$

### Verified as exact eigenstates

Applying $\vec J^{\,2}$ and $J_z$ (built exactly as above, from this
platform's own $S_x,S_y,S_z$ for $j=\tfrac12$) to each of these four
states directly confirms, to floating-point-exact precision (not
approximately):

| State | $\vec J^{\,2}$ eigenvalue | $J_z$ eigenvalue | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| $|1,1\rangle$ | $2\hbar^2$ | $\hbar$ | Triplet, $m=1$ |
| $|1,0\rangle$ | $2\hbar^2$ | $0$ | Triplet, $m=0$ |
| $|1,-1\rangle$ | $2\hbar^2$ | $-\hbar$ | Triplet, $m=-1$ |
| $|0,0\rangle$ | $0$ | $0$ | Singlet |

$2\hbar^2=j(j+1)\hbar^2$ at $j=1$ (Lesson 2's formula); $0=j(j+1)\hbar^2$
at $j=0$: both consistent, both checked directly, not assumed by
symmetry.

### The classical vector-model picture

Textbooks almost always accompany that table with a picture: draw the
two spin-$\tfrac12$ vectors, each of fixed length $\sqrt3/2\,\hbar$
($\sqrt{s(s+1)}\hbar$ at $s=\tfrac12$), tip to tail, and look at the
resultant $\vec J=\vec S_1+\vec S_2$. For the triplet, $\vec J$ keeps a
fixed length $\sqrt2\,\hbar$ ($\sqrt{j(j+1)}\hbar$ at $j=1$) while its
vertical component runs through $+\hbar,0,-\hbar$ across the three
$m$ values, matching the $J_z$ column above exactly. For the singlet
the two vectors point opposite directions and cancel completely,
matching $\vec J=0$:

This picture is useful for intuition, but it's a cartoon: $\vec S_1$
and $\vec S_2$ are operators, not classical arrows with a definite
direction, so no measurement ever finds a spin literally pointing along
the drawn arrow. What *is* exact is the length and $z$-projection each
diagram frame encodes, both already verified directly from the
operators in the table above.

### The singlet is a Bell state

Compare $|0,0\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|{\uparrow}{\downarrow}\rangle-|{\downarrow}{\uparrow}\rangle)$
directly to Quantum Gates & Circuits' Bell state
$|\Psi^-\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)$: **identical**,
term for term, once $|{\uparrow}\rangle\leftrightarrow|0\rangle$,
$|{\downarrow}\rangle\leftrightarrow|1\rangle$. The maximally entangled
Bell state built for quantum computing and the total-spin-zero singlet
built for atomic physics are, mathematically, the exact same state, not
an analogy: the identical vector, verified via the identical
$\vec J^{\,2}=0$ eigenstate computation. (The triplet's $m=0$ state,
$|1,0\rangle$, is likewise identical to $|\Psi^+\rangle$.)

## Physical Interpretation

This is a genuinely deep connection, not a coincidence of notation: a
system with total angular momentum exactly zero necessarily has *no
preferred direction at all*: measuring either particle's spin along
*any* axis gives a perfectly random result, but the two particles'
outcomes are perfectly (anti-)correlated whichever axis is chosen. This
is precisely the same "correlated in every basis" property Bell States
and Entanglement proved for $|\Phi^+\rangle$ using Hadamards, here
derived instead from the physical requirement that a spin-zero system
carries no directional information whatsoever.

## Worked Example

**Verify $|1,0\rangle$'s $J_z$ eigenvalue is exactly $0$, directly from
the individual-particle operators.**

$J_z|1,0\rangle = \left(S_z^{(1)}\otimes I+I\otimes S_z^{(2)}\right)\frac{|{\uparrow}{\downarrow}\rangle+|{\downarrow}{\uparrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}$.
On $|{\uparrow}{\downarrow}\rangle$: $S_z^{(1)}$ gives $+\tfrac\hbar2$,
$S_z^{(2)}$ gives $-\tfrac\hbar2$, summing to $0$. On
$|{\downarrow}{\uparrow}\rangle$: $-\tfrac\hbar2+\tfrac\hbar2=0$ as well.
Both terms give eigenvalue $0$, so the whole superposition does too,
matching the table exactly.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming any two-spin-$\tfrac12$ state has a well-defined total spin.**
Only the four states above (or their linear combinations *within* the
same $j$) do: a generic superposition like $|{\uparrow}{\uparrow}\rangle+|{\downarrow}{\downarrow}\rangle$
(unnormalized) is *not* automatically a $\vec J^{\,2}$ eigenstate unless
it happens to match one of the four specific combinations derived here.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Treating the Bell-state/singlet connection as merely "similar" states.**
They are the identical vector, under the identified basis correspondence,
checked via the identical $\vec J^{\,2}=0$ eigenstate property, not an
approximate resemblance.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Two spin-$\tfrac12$ particles combine into a spin-$1$ triplet (3 states)
  and a spin-$0$ singlet (1 state), verified as exact
  $\vec J^{\,2}$/$J_z$ eigenstates, not assumed by symmetry.
- The singlet $|0,0\rangle$ is the identical vector to the Bell state
  $|\Psi^-\rangle$; the triplet's $m=0$ state matches $|\Psi^+\rangle$,
  a genuine mathematical identity, checked directly.
- Total spin zero forces perfectly random single-particle outcomes along
  any measurement axis, with perfect correlation between the two
  particles, the physical content behind Bell-state entanglement,
  arrived at from a completely different starting point.
