## Motivation

From Classical to Quantum, the harmonic oscillator's entire energy
spectrum was derived from the ladder operators $a,a^\dagger$ and the
single relation $[a,a^\dagger]=1$, no calculus, no wavefunctions. Angular
momentum's spectrum comes from exactly the same strategy, applied to a
different pair of ladder operators built from $L_x,L_y$ (or the general
$J_x,J_y$) instead.

## Mathematical Development

### Building the ladder operators

Define $J_\pm=J_x\pm iJ_y$. From last lesson's commutators:

$$
[J_z,J_\pm] = [J_z,J_x]\pm i[J_z,J_y] = i\hbar J_y \pm i(-i\hbar J_x) = \pm\hbar(J_x\pm iJ_y) = \pm\hbar J_\pm
$$

### J± raises/lowers Jz's eigenvalue by ħ

Suppose $J_z|j,m\rangle=m\hbar|j,m\rangle$ for some eigenstate. Then:

$$
J_z(J_\pm|j,m\rangle) = (J_\pm J_z + [J_z,J_\pm])|j,m\rangle = J_\pm(m\hbar)|j,m\rangle \pm\hbar J_\pm|j,m\rangle = (m\pm1)\hbar\,(J_\pm|j,m\rangle)
$$

So $J_\pm|j,m\rangle$ is itself a $J_z$ eigenstate, with eigenvalue
raised (or lowered) by exactly $\hbar$, precisely the harmonic
oscillator's $a^\dagger|n\rangle\propto|n+1\rangle$ argument, with $J_z$'s
spectrum playing the role $\hat N$'s did there.

### Boundedness forces a top and bottom rung

$\vec J^{\,2}-J_z^2=J_x^2+J_y^2$ is a sum of squares of Hermitian
operators, so its expectation value in *any* state is $\geq0$. Writing
$\vec J^{\,2}$'s eigenvalue as $j(j+1)\hbar^2$ (a labeling convention,
not yet a constraint) on a state with $J_z$ eigenvalue $m\hbar$:

$$
j(j+1)\hbar^2 - m^2\hbar^2 \geq 0 \implies m^2 \leq j(j+1)
$$

$m$ is bounded above and below. Since $J_+$ keeps raising $m$ by 1
indefinitely unless it eventually annihilates the state, there must be a
**top rung** $m=j$ where $J_+|j,j\rangle=0$, and by the same logic a
**bottom rung** $m=-j$ where $J_-|j,-j\rangle=0$; otherwise $m$ would
exceed its bound. Both terminations are needed simultaneously, and since
$J_+$ raises $m$ in steps of exactly $1$ (in units of $\hbar$), the
distance from the bottom rung to the top, $j-(-j)=2j$, must be a
**non-negative integer**, forcing

$$
\boxed{j = 0,\ \tfrac12,\ 1,\ \tfrac32,\ 2,\ \dots}
$$

exactly the half-integer-or-integer spectrum angular momentum is famous
for, derived here from pure algebra, not assumed.

### Verified directly

`angularMomentumRaising(j)` acting on the top state ($m=j$, index 0 in
this platform's basis convention) gives exactly the zero vector, for
every $j$ tested; `angularMomentumLowering(j)` similarly annihilates the
bottom state. Both are checked directly, confirming the boundedness
argument produces exactly the matrices this platform's engine
independently builds from the raw matrix-element formula.

## Physical Interpretation

Nothing about *space* or *rotation* entered this derivation. It's pure
consequence of the commutation algebra $[J_i,J_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$.
This is exactly why the same math applies unchanged to spin (which has
no classical rotational picture at all): spin-1/2's $j=\tfrac12$ isn't a
separate assumption, it's the *smallest nontrivial solution* this
algebra allows.

## Worked Example

**For $j=1$, list every allowed value of $m$ and confirm there are
exactly $2j+1=3$ of them.**

Starting at the top rung $m=j=1$ and stepping down by $1$ until reaching
the bottom rung $m=-j=-1$: $m=1,0,-1$, exactly $3=2(1)+1$ values,
matching `angularMomentumZ(1)`'s $3\times3$ matrix having exactly 3
diagonal entries, $1,0,-1$.

The ladder structure is easiest to see as an actual ladder: each rung is
one $m$ value, evenly spaced by exactly $\hbar$, with $J_+$ climbing up
and $J_-$ climbing down until each hits the corresponding end.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming $j$ can be any half-integer independently of the specific
system.** The *set* of allowed $j$ values is $0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\dots$
in general, but which $j$ a specific physical system actually has (spin-
$\tfrac12$ for an electron's intrinsic spin, integer $l$ for orbital
angular momentum specifically, shown next lesson) is additional physical
input, not derivable from the commutation algebra alone.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Confusing $\vec J^{\,2}$'s eigenvalue $j(j+1)\hbar^2$ with $(j\hbar)^2$.**
They're different numbers for any $j>0$: $j(j+1)>j^2$ always, reflecting
that $\vec J^{\,2}$ includes contributions from $J_x^2+J_y^2$, not just
$J_z^2$'s own eigenvalue squared.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $J_\pm=J_x\pm iJ_y$ satisfy $[J_z,J_\pm]=\pm\hbar J_\pm$, making them
  raise/lower $J_z$'s eigenvalue by exactly $\hbar$, the same strategy as
  the harmonic oscillator's ladder operators.
- Boundedness ($J_x^2+J_y^2\geq0$) forces a top and bottom rung, and the
  integer-step spacing between them forces $j=0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\dots$,
  derived, not assumed.
- Verified directly: `angularMomentumRaising`/`Lowering` annihilate the
  predicted top/bottom states exactly, for every $j$ tested.
