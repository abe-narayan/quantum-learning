<LessonHook eyebrow="Why this matters">
A perfectly isolated system evolving unitarily forever is an idealization nobody has ever built in a lab. Every real system eventually interacts with something outside itself: stray photons, a measuring apparatus, thermal vibrations in the mounting hardware.
</LessonHook>

## Motivation

Entanglement, Mixed States & Bell Tests built density matrices and gave
them a <Term id="unitary-operator">unitary evolution</Term> rule, $\rho\to U\rho U^\dagger$. That rule has a
property which turns out to be fatal for describing anything real: it
cannot change $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^2)$. A state that starts pure stays pure
for ever.

Real states do not. Left alone for long enough, every laboratory qubit
decoheres, and its purity is measurably lower afterwards than before. So
the rule is not merely incomplete; applied to an open system it is
wrong, and the correction is the subject of this lesson.

The plot below is the whole problem in one picture. All three curves
start from $|+\rangle$ and apply one step of evolution at a time: a
unitary rotation, an amplitude-damping channel, and a dephasing channel.

worth working out before reading on.

## Mathematical Development

### Why unitary evolution alone isn't enough

$\rho\to U\rho U^\dagger$ preserves <Term id="von-neumann-entropy-purity">purity</Term> exactly, and the one-line proof is
worth having: $\mathrm{Tr}\big((U\rho U^\dagger)^2\big) = \mathrm{Tr}(U\rho
U^\dagger U\rho U^\dagger) = \mathrm{Tr}(U\rho^2U^\dagger) =
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^2)$, using $U^\dagger U=I$ once and the cyclic property
of the trace once.

No choice of $U$ escapes that. So if purity drops in the laboratory, the
system's evolution is not being generated by any unitary acting on the
system alone. The resolution is not to abandon unitarity: the system plus
its environment does evolve unitarily. It is that discarding the
environment, by tracing it out, leaves behind a map on the system that
need not be unitary at all.

### The Kraus operator sum representation

Any physically valid (completely positive, trace-preserving) evolution
of an open system can be written as:

$$
\rho \to \mathcal E(\rho) = \sum_k K_k\,\rho\,K_k^\dagger
$$

for some set of **<Term id="kraus-operators-cptp-maps">Kraus operators</Term>** $\{K_k\}$ satisfying $\sum_kK_k^\dagger
K_k=I$, the **trace-preservation condition**, which guarantees
$\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal E(\rho))=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)$ so that total
probability stays $1$.

A single unitary is the one-operator case, $K_0=U$, where the condition
reduces to $U^\dagger U=I$ and is satisfied automatically. So the Kraus
form contains unitary evolution rather than competing with it, and the
interesting channels are the ones needing two or more operators. Purity
can only change when there is more than one term in the sum, which is
visible directly in the formula: with one term the map is a similarity
transformation, and with several it is a mixture.

### Verified directly against the trace-preservation condition

This platform's `isTracePreserving` checks $\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I$ entry
by entry. It confirms both channels this course builds, amplitude damping
and dephasing, and it also rejects a deliberately broken example: a
single Kraus operator scaled by $2$, whose sum comes out $4I$. That
second test matters more than the first, because a check that only ever
returns `true` proves nothing.

The condition has a geometric consequence you can watch. Every
intermediate state below is a legitimate density matrix, so its Bloch
vector stays within the sphere at every step, even while purity falls.

## Physical Interpretation

Each $K_k$ stands for one thing the environment could have done. For
amplitude damping, $K_1$ is "a photon was emitted" and $K_0$ is "no
photon was emitted"; nothing measures which, and no record is kept. The
sum $\sum_kK_k\rho K_k^\dagger$ is the system's state averaged over the
branches, weighted by their amplitudes.

That averaging is the whole source of purity change. A single branch
would be a unitary-like map and would preserve purity; a mixture over
branches nobody tracked is exactly what a mixed state is.

On hardware this is not a metaphor. A superconducting qubit couples to
its control lines and to phonons in the substrate; a trapped ion couples
to stray electric-field noise. In both cases the environment records
which branch occurred and the experimenter cannot read it, which is why
the $T_1$ and $T_2$ decay curves measured routinely on every processor
are these channels in another notation.

## Worked Example

**Confirm the amplitude damping channel, $K_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\end{pmatrix}$,
$K_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt\gamma\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, is trace-preserving.**

$$
K_0^\dagger K_0 = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1-\gamma\end{pmatrix}, \qquad K_1^\dagger K_1 = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&\gamma\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
K_0^\dagger K_0 + K_1^\dagger K_1 = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} = I
$$

This holds for every $\gamma\in[0,1]$, confirmed directly by this
platform's `isTracePreserving(amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma))`, which
returns `true` across the full range.

The same identity, seen rather than derived. Each preset below shows
$K_0$, $K_1$, and $K_0^\dagger K_0+K_1^\dagger K_1$ for a channel the
platform computes numerically, with the sum's diagonal highlighted. Note
how different the two channels' individual operators are, and how
identical their sums come out.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Assuming any set of matrices can serve as Kraus operators.** The
trace-preservation condition $\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I$ is a real
constraint. An arbitrarily chosen matrix set (like a single operator
scaled by 2, this lesson's counter-example) generally fails it and does
not describe a physical channel.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Thinking open-system evolution "violates" quantum mechanics' unitarity
postulate.** It doesn't. The FULL system-plus-environment evolves
unitarily; the Kraus representation is exactly what that global unitary
evolution looks like once the environment's degrees of freedom are
traced out (not tracked). Nothing about quantum mechanics' foundations
is being bent.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Open (environment-coupled) systems cannot, in general, evolve purely
  unitarily: real purity loss requires going beyond
  $\rho\to U\rho U^\dagger$.
- The Kraus representation $\rho\to\sum_kK_k\rho K_k^\dagger$, subject to
  $\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I$, is the general framework, a strict
  generalization of unitary evolution, not a replacement.
- Purity falling is a symptom of decoherence, not a definition of it:
  amplitude damping destroys a superposition while returning the qubit
  to a pure state.
- This platform's `isTracePreserving` verifies the condition directly,
  confirmed for amplitude damping and dephasing and rejected for a
  deliberately invalid counter-example.
