## Motivation

Entanglement, Mixed States & Bell Tests built density matrices and
covered their unitary evolution, but a perfectly *isolated* system
evolving unitarily forever is an idealization nobody has ever built in
a lab. Every real system eventually interacts with something outside
itself: stray photons, a measuring apparatus, thermal vibrations in the
mounting hardware. This lesson introduces the mathematical language for
that, **open quantum systems**, building directly on, and going beyond,
the density-matrix foundation Entanglement already established.

## Mathematical Development

### Why unitary evolution alone isn't enough

$\rho\to U\rho U^\dagger$ (Entanglement's evolution rule) always preserves
purity exactly: $\text{Tr}(\rho^2)$ never changes under it (a fact
already provable from $U$'s unitarity alone). But real decoherence
*does* change purity. A pure state actually becomes mixed over time
through environmental interaction. No unitary evolution of the system
*alone* can do this; the resolution is that the system's evolution,
once the environment is "traced out," need not be unitary at all.

### The Kraus operator sum representation

Any physically valid (completely positive, trace-preserving) evolution
of an open system can be written as:

$$
\rho \to \mathcal E(\rho) = \sum_k K_k\,\rho\,K_k^\dagger
$$

for some set of **Kraus operators** $\{K_k\}$ satisfying
$\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I$ (the **trace-preservation condition**: it
guarantees $\text{Tr}(\mathcal E(\rho))=\text{Tr}(\rho)$, so total
probability stays 1). A single unitary $U$ is the special case of exactly
one Kraus operator, $K_0=U$ (then $K_0^\dagger K_0=U^\dagger U=I$
automatically). Kraus operators *generalize* unitary evolution; they
don't replace it.

### Verified directly against the trace-preservation condition

This platform's `isTracePreserving` function checks $\sum_kK_k^\dagger
K_k=I$ directly, entry by entry, not just for the two decoherence
channels this course builds (amplitude damping, dephasing, both
confirmed trace-preserving), but also against a deliberately broken
example (a single Kraus operator scaled by 2, which fails the condition
outright) to confirm the check actually discriminates valid from invalid
channels, rather than trivially returning `true`.

Watch the trace-preservation condition play out physically, not just
algebraically. Every intermediate step below is a valid quantum state
(total probability exactly 1), even though it's visibly losing purity
along the way.

## Physical Interpretation

Physically, $\{K_k\}$ encodes *what could have happened* during the
system's interaction with its environment. Each $K_k$ corresponds to
one possible "environment outcome" (even though nothing is actually
measured or recorded), and the sum $\sum_kK_k\rho K_k^\dagger$ correctly
accounts for the system's state averaged over every possibility. That's
exactly why it changes purity: it's a statistical mixture over unknown
environmental branches, encoded directly in the operator sum. On real
hardware this is not an abstraction: a superconducting qubit's coupling
to its control lines and to phonons in the chip substrate, or a trapped
ion's coupling to stray electric-field noise, are physical instances of
exactly this "environment outcome we don't track" picture, which is why
$T_1$ and $T_2$ decay curves (measured routinely on every quantum
processor) are Kraus-channel purity decay in disguise.

## Worked Example

**Confirm the amplitude damping channel, $K_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\end{pmatrix}$,
$K_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt\gamma\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, is trace-preserving.**

$$
K_0^\dagger K_0 = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1-\gamma\end{pmatrix}, \qquad K_1^\dagger K_1 = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&\gamma\end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
K_0^\dagger K_0 + K_1^\dagger K_1 = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} = I
$$

This holds for every $\gamma\in[0,1]$, confirmed directly by this
platform's `isTracePreserving(amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma))`, which
returns `true` across the full range.

The same identity, seen rather than just algebraically derived: each
preset below shows $K_0$, $K_1$, and $K_0^\dagger K_0+K_1^\dagger K_1$
for a real channel this platform computes numerically
(`amplitudeDampingChannel(0.3)` and `dephasingChannel(0.3)`), with the
sum's diagonal highlighted to make "$=I$" visually immediate rather than
something to take on faith from the LaTeX above.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming any set of matrices can serve as Kraus operators.** The
trace-preservation condition $\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I$ is a real
constraint. An arbitrarily chosen matrix set (like a single operator
scaled by 2, this lesson's counter-example) generally fails it and does
not describe a physical channel.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking open-system evolution "violates" quantum mechanics' unitarity
postulate.** It doesn't. The FULL system-plus-environment evolves
unitarily; the Kraus representation is exactly what that global unitary
evolution looks like once the environment's degrees of freedom are
traced out (not tracked). Nothing about quantum mechanics' foundations
is being bent.
</Callout>

   occurs?

## Summary

- Open (environment-coupled) systems cannot, in general, evolve purely
  unitarily: real purity loss requires going beyond
  $\rho\to U\rho U^\dagger$.
- The Kraus representation $\rho\to\sum_kK_k\rho K_k^\dagger$, subject to
  $\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I$, is the general framework, a strict
  generalization of unitary evolution, not a replacement.
- This platform's `isTracePreserving` verifies the condition directly,
  confirmed both for physical channels (amplitude damping, dephasing) and
  against a deliberately invalid counter-example.
