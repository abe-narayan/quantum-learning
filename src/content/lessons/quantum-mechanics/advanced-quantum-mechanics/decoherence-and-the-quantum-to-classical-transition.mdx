consider applying the dephasing channel from the previous lesson
($\lambda=0.3$) over and over.

### What survives: the diagonal

Throughout this entire process, the diagonal entries ($P(0)=P(1)=0.5$
for $|+\rangle$) never change. Dephasing, true to its name, destroys
*phase* information while leaving *populations* untouched. What remains
after full decoherence is a state that looks exactly like a classical
50/50 probabilistic mixture, mathematically indistinguishable from "the
system was secretly in $|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$ all along, we just
didn't know which."

## Physical Interpretation

This is decoherence's actual physical content: environmental interaction
doesn't destroy superposition by some mysterious mechanism. It
*spreads* the coherence out into correlations with environmental degrees
of freedom nobody is tracking, which is mathematically identical to
tracing them out and watching the system's own density matrix lose its
off-diagonal terms. **What decoherence does NOT do**: it does not, by
itself, explain why a *single, definite* outcome is observed upon
measurement (the diagonal state after decoherence is still a
*probabilistic mixture*, not a single realized outcome). That remains an
open interpretational question this course states honestly rather than
claiming to resolve.

## Worked Example

**After how many dephasing applications ($\lambda=0.3$) does $|+\rangle$'s
off-diagonal magnitude first drop below 0.01?**

From the table above: at 10 applications, off-diagonal $\approx0.014$
(still above 0.01); at 20 applications, $\approx0.0004$ (well below).
Since each application multiplies the off-diagonal magnitude by exactly
$(1-\lambda)=0.7$ (a direct consequence of the dephasing Kraus operators'
structure), the threshold is crossed at
$n=\log(0.01/0.5)/\log(0.7)\approx10.9$, i.e. the 11th application,
directly checkable against `applyChannelRepeatedly`'s engine output.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Confusing decoherence with <Term id="wavefunction-collapse">wavefunction collapse</Term>.** Decoherence turns a
pure superposition into a mixed (probabilistic) state. It does NOT, by
itself, pick out one definite outcome. The state after full decoherence
is still "50% this, 50% that," not "definitely this." What actually
selects one outcome upon measurement remains an open question in the
foundations of quantum mechanics, not settled by decoherence alone.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Thinking decoherence only matters for exotic macroscopic superpositions.**
The same math applies to any qubit weakly coupled to any uncontrolled
environment. This is precisely why real quantum computers need error
correction and careful isolation (previewed here, developed fully in
Quantum Computing's Error Correction and Noise/Decoherence courses).
</Callout>

## Summary

- Repeated dephasing drives $|+\rangle$'s off-diagonal coherence toward 0
  and its purity toward 0.5, a direct numerical demonstration of
  decoherence, not an assertion.
- The diagonal (populations) are unaffected; only phase information is
  lost, so the resulting state looks exactly like a classical probabilistic
  mixture.
- Decoherence explains the *loss of quantum coherence*, but does not by
  itself explain *definite measurement outcomes*, an honest, explicitly
  stated scope limitation, not a resolved question.
