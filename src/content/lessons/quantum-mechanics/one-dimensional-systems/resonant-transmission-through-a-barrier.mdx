## Motivation

Tunneling and the Finite Barrier covered $E<V_0$ — a barrier taller than
the particle's energy, crossed only by the exponentially-suppressed
tunneling mechanism. This lesson covers the complementary regime,
$E>V_0$: the particle has enough energy to classically cross the
barrier, so *something* transmits — but exactly how much, as a function
of energy, turns out to have a genuinely surprising structure.

## Intuition

A barrier with $E>V_0$ has the *same* three-region structure as the
step-potential problem, just with a third region (transmitted wave)
past a *second* boundary — and, crucially, inside the barrier the
wavefunction still **oscillates** (not decays, since $E>V_0$ there
means $k$ is real inside too), unlike the tunneling case's exponential
decay. Two boundaries means two reflected waves can interfere with each
other — and interference, constructive or destructive, is exactly what
produces resonances.

## Mathematical Development

### The three-region setup

Barrier of height $V_0$, full width $L$, spanning $0$ to $L$; particle
energy $E>V_0$. Inside the barrier, $k_2=\sqrt{2(E-V_0)}$ (real,
oscillatory — the key difference from tunneling's $\kappa=\sqrt{2(V_0-E)}$).
Matching $\psi,\psi'$ at *both* $x=0$ and $x=L$ (four equations, the same
kind of boundary-matching used for the step, just doubled) gives, after
elimination of every amplitude except the incident and transmitted ones
— a longer algebraic exercise than the single-step case, not repeated in
full here — the standard closed-form result:

$$
\boxed{\;T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2\sin^2(k_2L)}{4E(E-V_0)}\right]^{-1}\;}
$$

This platform's `barrierScatteringTransmission` function implements
exactly this formula, tested against the $V_0\to0$ limit ($T\to1$, no
barrier) and the resonance condition below.

### The resonance condition

$T=1$ exactly whenever $\sin(k_2L)=0$, i.e.

$$
k_2L = n\pi, \qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots
$$

At these special energies, the barrier is **perfectly transparent** —
every incident particle transmits, none reflects, despite the barrier
being, in every other sense, a genuine obstacle.

## Physical Interpretation

This is the exact quantum-mechanical analogue of an anti-reflective
optical coating: a thin film reflects light at both of its surfaces, and
at specific wavelengths those two reflections destructively interfere,
cancelling the net reflection entirely. Here, the two boundaries of the
barrier play the role of the film's two surfaces, and the resonance
condition $k_2L=n\pi$ is exactly the same "round-trip phase is a
multiple of $2\pi$" condition that governs optical thin-film
interference — the same physics as light, showing up because both are
governed by a wave equation with boundary matching.

## Worked Example

**For $E=5$, $V_0=2$ (natural units), find the barrier width $L$ that
produces the first resonance, and confirm $T=1$ there.**

$$
k_2 = \sqrt{2(5-2)} = \sqrt6 \approx 2.4495
$$

First resonance ($n=1$): $L=\pi/k_2\approx1.2825$. Substituting into the
transmission formula, $\sin(k_2L)=\sin(\pi)=0$ exactly, so the
$\sin^2$ term vanishes and $T=1/[1+0]=1$ — confirmed directly, and
matched by this platform's own test suite for `barrierScatteringTransmission`.
For comparison, at a *non*-resonant width like $L=1$: $k_2L\approx2.449$
(not a multiple of $\pi$), giving $T\approx0.9736$ — still high (a
narrow barrier relative to the wavelength), but not exactly 1.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Confusing this formula with the tunneling ($E<V_0$) case.** The
tunneling regime's transmission falls off roughly as $e^{-2\kappa L}$
(Tunneling and the Finite Barrier); this regime's oscillates with $L$
via $\sin^2(k_2L)$ and can reach exactly $1$ — qualitatively different
behavior, because the wavefunction oscillates rather than decays inside
the barrier.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking resonance requires fine-tuning both $E$ and $L$
independently.** For a *fixed* barrier ($V_0,L$ given), resonances occur
at a discrete, predictable sequence of *energies* $E$ satisfying
$k_2(E)L=n\pi$ — an experimentally realistic, adjustable knob (scan the
incident particle's energy) rather than an impossible-to-achieve
coincidence.
</Callout>

## Summary

- For $E>V_0$, transmission through a barrier is
  $T=[1+V_0^2\sin^2(k_2L)/(4E(E-V_0))]^{-1}$, oscillating with barrier
  width rather than decaying exponentially (the tunneling regime's
  behavior).
- $T=1$ exactly whenever $k_2L=n\pi$ — perfect transmission at discrete
  resonant energies, directly analogous to anti-reflective optical
  coatings.
- This is the genuine complement to Wave Mechanics' tunneling treatment:
  together, the two regimes ($E<V_0$ and $E>V_0$) fully describe
  scattering off a rectangular barrier at any energy.
