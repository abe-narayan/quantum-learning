## Motivation

The Hydrogen Atom solved $H=p^2/2m-e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0r$ exactly. Almost
no other Hamiltonian of physical interest can be solved exactly: real
atoms have electron-electron repulsion, real oscillators are slightly
anharmonic, real spins sit in small stray magnetic fields. Perturbation
theory is the systematic answer. If $H=H_0+H'$ with $H_0$ exactly
solvable and $H'$ "small," you can build the true energies and states as
a correction series in $H'$, order by order.

## Mathematical Development

### Setup: H₀'s eigenbasis

Let $H_0|n^{(0)}\rangle=E_n^{(0)}|n^{(0)}\rangle$ be already solved (this
course's harmonic oscillator, or hydrogen, are natural choices for
$H_0$). Writing $H'$ as a matrix *in this same eigenbasis*, so that
$H'_{mn}=\langle m^{(0)}|H'|n^{(0)}\rangle$, is the essential setup step
every formula below assumes.

### First-order energy correction

$$
E_n^{(1)} = \langle n^{(0)}|H'|n^{(0)}\rangle = H'_{nn}
$$

This is literally just the diagonal entry of $H'$ in the $H_0$
eigenbasis: no sum, no derivation needed to *use* it, though the formula
itself follows from expanding $(H_0+H')|\psi_n\rangle=E_n|\psi_n\rangle$
in powers of $H'$ and collecting first-order terms.

### Second-order energy correction

$$
E_n^{(2)} = \sum_{m\neq n} \frac{|H'_{mn}|^2}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}}
$$

Every *other* state $m$ contributes here, weighted by how strongly $H'$
couples $n$ to $m$ ($|H'_{mn}|^2$) and how close their unperturbed
energies are (a small energy gap in the denominator makes a close-lying
state contribute disproportionately). This formula assumes $E_n^{(0)}$
is non-degenerate, a genuine restriction, stated honestly rather than
silently assumed (degenerate perturbation theory needs an extra
diagonalization step this course doesn't cover).

### First-order state correction

$$
|n^{(1)}\rangle = \sum_{m\neq n} \frac{H'_{mn}}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}}\,|m^{(0)}\rangle
$$

The perturbed state "mixes in" other unperturbed eigenstates here, with
coefficients built from the same matrix elements and energy gaps as
above. By convention, the $|n^{(0)}\rangle$ component of this correction
is taken to be exactly 0, fixing the overall normalization freedom at
this order.

## Physical Interpretation

Perturbation theory is only as good as "$H'$ small" actually is. The
worked example below applies it to a case with a fully independent exact
check, giving a genuine numerical confirmation, not just trust in the
formula.

## Worked Example

**The quartic anharmonic oscillator, $H=H_0+\lambda x^4$ ($H_0$ the
ordinary harmonic oscillator, $m=\omega=\hbar=1$). Find the first-order
ground-state energy shift.**

Using $x=\sqrt{1/2}(a+a^\dagger)$ (Harmonic Oscillator's ladder
operators), a closed-form identity for the harmonic oscillator gives
$\langle0|x^4|0\rangle=\frac34$ exactly, independent of perturbation
theory, a fact about $H_0$'s own ground state alone. So:

$$
E_0^{(1)} = \lambda\langle0|x^4|0\rangle = \frac{3\lambda}{4}
$$

This platform's engine confirms this two independent ways: building the
$x^4$ matrix from `positionOperator` (itself built from `annihilationOperator`/`creationOperator`)
and reading off `firstOrderEnergyCorrection(H0diag, Hprime, 0)` gives
$0.0075$ for $\lambda=0.01$, matching $\frac34(0.01)=0.0075$ exactly.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Using H′ in the wrong basis.** These formulas require $H'_{mn}$
computed in $H_0$'s *own* eigenbasis, not an arbitrary basis (like the
position basis directly): the matrix elements $\langle m^{(0)}|H'|n^{(0)}\rangle$
depend on this specific choice.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Applying second-order perturbation theory to a degenerate $E_n^{(0)}$.**
The $E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}$ denominator is exactly zero for a genuinely
degenerate pair, and the formula breaks (this platform's engine
deliberately throws rather than silently returning $\pm\infty$ or NaN).
</Callout>

## Practice Questions

1. Explain in words why $E_n^{(1)}=H'_{nn}$ requires no sum over other
   states, while $E_n^{(2)}$ does.
2. For the quartic oscillator worked example, is $E_0^{(2)}$ (this
   course's engine gives a small negative number) consistent with the
   general expectation that a repulsive perturbation ($\lambda x^4>0$
   everywhere) still gives a negative second-order correction? (Hint:
   second-order corrections to the *ground* state are always
   $\leq0$. Why?)
3. If $H'_{mn}=0$ for all $m\neq n$ at a particular $n$, what does this
   imply about $E_n^{(2)}$ and $|n^{(1)}\rangle$ at that $n$?
4. Why does the first-order state correction conventionally set its own
   $|n^{(0)}\rangle$ coefficient to exactly 0, rather than leaving it
   undetermined?

## Summary

- $H=H_0+H'$ with $H_0$ solvable: $E_n^{(1)}=H'_{nn}$,
  $E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{m\neq n}|H'_{mn}|^2/(E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)})$,
  $|n^{(1)}\rangle=\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{H'_{mn}}{E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)}}|m^{(0)}\rangle$.
- All three require $H'$ expressed in $H_0$'s eigenbasis, and
  non-degenerate $E_n^{(0)}$.
- The quartic anharmonic oscillator's first-order shift,
  $\frac34\lambda$, is checked here against an independent exact matrix
  element, a genuine numerical confirmation, not a circular check.
