## Motivation

Perturbation theory needs a small parameter; the variational method
needs a good guessed shape. WKB (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin) takes a
third, geometric route: treat the wavefunction as approximately a
classical wave with a slowly-varying local wavelength set by the local
classical momentum $p(x)=\sqrt{2m(E-V(x))}$, and demand that this wave
close consistently on itself between the classical turning points.

## Mathematical Development

### The classically allowed region

For energy $E$ and potential $V(x)$, the classically allowed region is
wherever $E>V(x)$ (a classical particle with that energy could physically
be there); its boundary points (where $E=V(x)$) are the **turning
points**, $x_1<x_2$. The local classical momentum is
$p(x)=\sqrt{2m(E-V(x))}$, real and positive only inside this region.

For the harmonic oscillator's $n=1$ level ($E=1.5$), the turning points
sit at $x=\pm\sqrt{2E}\approx\pm1.732$, found by solving $E=V(x)=x^2/2$
directly:

The shaded band is exactly where $E>V(x)$, the region the action
integral $\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p(x)dx$ sums over; outside it, $p(x)$ would be
imaginary, which is precisely why the integral stops at the turning
points rather than running over all $x$.

### The quantization condition

Demanding the semiclassical wave be single-valued and vanish smoothly at
both turning points (a technical boundary-matching argument this course
states rather than derives in full) gives:

$$
\int_{x_1}^{x_2} p(x)\,dx = \left(n+\frac12\right)\pi\hbar, \qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots
$$

This is the **Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition** (with the
$\frac12$ Maslov correction for two smooth/"soft" turning points, the
standard case for a smooth confining potential like the harmonic
oscillator). This platform's `wkbActionIntegral` computes the left side
directly, as a Riemann sum over the grid restricted to where $E>V(x)$;
`wkbQuantizedEnergy` bisects on $E$ until the integral matches the right
side for a given $n$, the same bisection strategy Wave Mechanics'
`finiteSquareWellGroundStateEnergy` already used for a different
transcendental equation.

## Physical Interpretation

WKB is a *semiclassical* approximation. It's built entirely from the
classical momentum $p(x)$, with quantum mechanics entering only through
the $\hbar$ in the quantization condition. It tends to work best when
the potential varies slowly on the scale of the local de Broglie
wavelength, physically the "large quantum number" or "correspondence
principle" regime where a quantum system starts behaving classically.
The harmonic oscillator is a special case where WKB happens to be exact
at *every* $n$, not just asymptotically for large $n$: a genuine,
checkable mathematical fact about this specific potential's shape, not a
general property of WKB.

## Worked Example

**Compute the WKB-quantized energies for the harmonic oscillator
($m=\omega=1$) at $n=0,1,2,3$, and compare to the exact
$E_n=(n+\frac12)\hbar\omega$.**

| $n$ | Exact | WKB (this engine) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.5000 | 0.5003 | 0.00026 |
| 1 | 1.5000 | 1.4999 | 0.00008 |
| 2 | 2.5000 | 2.5003 | 0.00031 |
| 3 | 3.5000 | 3.4996 | 0.00038 |

Every WKB value matches the exact answer to within $\sim0.04\%$ (the
small residual differences are pure grid-resolution numerical error in
the Riemann-sum action integral and bisection, not a limitation of WKB
itself: WKB is mathematically exact for the harmonic oscillator).

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking WKB is always this accurate.** The harmonic oscillator's
exactness is special (tied to its potential's specific quadratic shape).
For a generic potential, WKB is an *approximation* that improves as $n$
grows, and can be noticeably off for small $n$. This course doesn't
implement a second example to contrast against, but the distinction
matters for correctly interpreting the harmonic oscillator's near-perfect
result here.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Forgetting the $\frac12$ in $(n+\frac12)\pi\hbar$.** Omitting it
(using $n\pi\hbar$ instead) is a common error that shifts every
predicted energy level. The Maslov correction for two soft turning
points is not optional bookkeeping.
</Callout>

## Summary

- WKB quantizes energy via
  $\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p(x)dx=(n+\frac12)\pi\hbar$, using the classical
  local momentum and the potential's turning points.
- This platform computes the action integral as a Riemann sum over the
  classically allowed region, and bisects on $E$ to match the
  quantization condition, reusing the same numerical strategy as the
  finite-well ground-state calculation.
- For the harmonic oscillator, WKB reproduces the exact spectrum to
  within grid-resolution numerical error at every $n$ tested, a special
  exactness of this potential, not a general WKB guarantee.
