## Motivation

Early in this course, interference appeared as a fact about two physical
paths to a screen. By now, the tools exist to state it far more
generally, and more usefully: interference is a statement about
*measurement bases*, not specifically about paths — and the exact same
formula that produced the double-slit fringe pattern also produced the
Time Evolution lesson's precession result. This lesson makes that
unification explicit.

## Intuition

A superposition $|\psi\rangle = c_a|a\rangle+c_b|b\rangle$ has a relative
phase between $c_a$ and $c_b$. Measure in the $\{|a\rangle,|b\rangle\}$
basis itself, and that phase is completely invisible — $P(a)=|c_a|^2$
doesn't care what phase $c_a$ carries. Measure in a *different* basis
instead, and the phase suddenly matters enormously, continuously
reshaping the outcome probabilities. Interference is what happens when
you ask a question (a choice of measurement basis) that doesn't line up
with the way a state happens to be written.

## Mathematical Development

### The superposition principle

Postulate 1 says any normalized vector in the Hilbert space is a valid
physical state. So if $|a\rangle,|b\rangle$ are both valid states, any
normalized combination $|\psi\rangle=c_a|a\rangle+c_b|b\rangle$
($|c_a|^2+|c_b|^2=1$) is too — this is the entire content of the
**superposition principle**: nothing extra to postulate beyond the
vector-space structure already built.

### Measuring in the "natural" basis: no interference

If $\{|a\rangle,|b\rangle\}$ is itself an orthonormal eigenbasis of the
observable being measured, the Born rule gives $P(a)=|c_a|^2$,
$P(b)=|c_b|^2$ directly — no cross terms, no dependence on the relative
phase between $c_a$ and $c_b$ at all.

### Measuring in a different basis: interference appears

Take the concrete case $|\psi\rangle =
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|0\rangle+e^{i\varphi}|1\rangle\right)$, and
measure in the $\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}$ basis instead of
$\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$. Using
$|+\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$:

$$
\langle+|\psi\rangle = \frac12\left(\langle0|+\langle1|\right)\left(|0\rangle+e^{i\varphi}|1\rangle\right) = \frac12\left(1+e^{i\varphi}\right)
$$

$$
P(+) = |\langle+|\psi\rangle|^2 = \frac14\left|1+e^{i\varphi}\right|^2 = \frac14\left(1+e^{i\varphi}\right)\left(1+e^{-i\varphi}\right) = \frac14\left(2+e^{i\varphi}+e^{-i\varphi}\right) = \frac{1+\cos\varphi}{2}
$$

(using $e^{i\varphi}+e^{-i\varphi}=2\cos\varphi$, from Euler's formula).
This is a genuine interference pattern in $\varphi$: $P(+)$ ranges from
$1$ (at $\varphi=0$, fully constructive) down to $0$ (at $\varphi=\pi$,
fully destructive) — exactly the double-slit fringe shape, now derived
for a projective measurement in a rotated basis rather than for two
physical paths to a screen. **The relative phase that was completely
invisible when measuring in the $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$ basis is fully
visible here.**

### The same formula, twice

Compare $P(+)=\tfrac{1+\cos\varphi}{2}$ to the Time Evolution lesson's
result, $P(\text{still }|+\rangle\text{ at time }t) = \cos^2(\omega
t/2)$. The half-angle identity $\cos^2(\theta/2) =
\tfrac{1+\cos\theta}{2}$ shows these are the *same* formula, with
$\varphi \leftrightarrow \omega t$. Precession in time and interference
across a change of measurement basis are not two different phenomena
that happen to look similar — they're the identical mathematical
statement, $\tfrac{1+\cos(\text{phase})}{2}$, arising once from a state
evolving and once from a state being probed in a different basis.

## Physical Interpretation

This reframes what "which-path information destroys interference" (from
the earlier lesson) really means: detecting which path a particle took
is, mathematically, a measurement in the $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$-type
"path basis" — and as shown above, that basis is exactly the one in which
the relative phase becomes invisible. Choosing *not* to measure which
path, and instead measuring in a basis like $\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}$
that mixes the two paths together, is precisely what makes the phase-
dependent interference pattern appear. Nothing mysterious is added by
"observation" — it's the ordinary Born rule, evaluated in two different
bases.

## Worked Example

**For $\varphi=2\pi/3$, find $P(+)$ using the derived formula.**

$$
P(+) = \frac{1+\cos(2\pi/3)}{2} = \frac{1+(-\tfrac12)}{2} = \frac{1/2}{2}=\frac14
$$

Check against the direct calculation:
$\langle+|\psi\rangle=\tfrac12(1+e^{i2\pi/3})$, and
$e^{i2\pi/3}=-\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$, so
$1+e^{i2\pi/3}=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}i$, with squared magnitude
$\tfrac14+\tfrac34=1$; then $P(+)=\tfrac14\cdot1=\tfrac14$ ✓, matching.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming relative phase always shows up in probabilities, in every
basis.** It's invisible in the superposition's own basis by construction
— it only appears once you measure in a basis that mixes the original
terms together.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Treating interference and time evolution as unrelated topics that
happen to share notation.** As derived above, they produce the literal
same formula via the half-angle identity — not a coincidence, a genuine
mathematical identity between two physically different setups.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Superposition is a direct consequence of Postulate 1's vector-space
  structure — nothing extra to add.
- Measuring in a superposition's own basis hides relative phase
  completely; measuring in a different basis reveals it, via
  $P=\tfrac{1+\cos\varphi}{2}$, derived directly from the Born rule.
- This same formula (via a half-angle identity) is exactly the Time
  Evolution lesson's precession probability — interference-across-bases
  and precession-in-time are the same mathematics.
