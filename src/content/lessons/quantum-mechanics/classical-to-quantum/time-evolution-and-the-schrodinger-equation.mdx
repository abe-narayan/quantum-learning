## Motivation

Postulate 4 said evolution is unitary, without saying which unitary
operator, or how it depends on time. This lesson derives the answer: the
generator of continuous unitary evolution is forced to be Hermitian,
which leads directly to the Schrödinger equation, not as a separate
postulate to memorize, but as a consequence of unitarity plus ordinary
calculus.

## Intuition

Think of evolving a state for a very short time $dt$ as a tiny nudge,
$U(dt) \approx I + (\text{something}) \cdot dt$. Unitarity constrains what
that "something" is allowed to be, and the constraint turns out to force
it into exactly the same Hermitian-operator mold every observable already
lives in.

## Mathematical Development

### The generator must be Hermitian

Write the evolution operator for a short time step as
$U(dt) = I - \tfrac{i}{\hbar}H\,dt + O(dt^2)$ for some operator $H$ to be
determined ($\hbar$ and the factor of $i$ are conventional choices that
make $H$ come out with units of energy, confirmed below). Impose
unitarity, $U^\dagger(dt)U(dt)=I$, and keep only first-order terms in
$dt$:

$$
U^\dagger U = \left(I+\tfrac{i}{\hbar}H^\dagger dt\right)\left(I-\tfrac{i}{\hbar}H\,dt\right) = I + \tfrac{i}{\hbar}(H^\dagger - H)\,dt + O(dt^2)
$$

For this to equal $I$ at first order, the $dt$ term must vanish:
$H^\dagger = H$, so **the generator of unitary evolution must be
Hermitian.** This is exactly why the Postulates lesson could already
identify $H$ (once the physical identification below is made) with
energy: Hermitian operators are precisely what Postulate 2 reserves for
observables.

### From the generator to the Schrödinger equation

Chaining infinitesimal steps (or, equivalently, solving the differential
equation this construction implies) gives the finite-time evolution
operator:

$$
U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar}
$$

Differentiate both sides of $|\psi(t)\rangle = U(t)|\psi(0)\rangle$ with
respect to $t$, using $\frac{d}{dt}e^{-iHt/\hbar} =
-\tfrac{i}{\hbar}H\,e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ (ordinary calculus applied to the
operator exponential):

$$
\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle = -\frac{i}{\hbar}H\,U(t)|\psi(0)\rangle = -\frac{i}{\hbar}H|\psi(t)\rangle
$$

Rearranged, this is the **time-dependent Schrödinger equation**:

$$
\boxed{i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle = H|\psi(t)\rangle}
$$

$H$ is called the **Hamiltonian**, and it's physically identified with
the system's total energy (the same identification that makes the
$E=\hbar\omega$ relation from de Broglie/Planck-era physics consistent
with this framework, a connection made precise once this course's
successor, Wave Mechanics, introduces continuous position). This
identification is itself a further physical input, not something the
unitarity argument alone proves; it's confirmed by every subsequent
prediction matching experiment.

### A concrete two-level example: reusing existing machinery

For a two-level system, any traceless Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix can be
written $H = \tfrac{\hbar\omega}{2}(\hat n\cdot\vec\sigma)$ for a real
number $\omega$, a unit vector $\hat n$, and the Pauli matrices
$\vec\sigma=(X,Y,Z)$ (traceless is no real restriction: adding a
multiple of the identity only contributes an unobservable global phase,
as the Global Phase vs. Relative Phase lesson in the Quantum Computing
track shows directly). Then:

$$
U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar} = e^{-i(\omega t/2)(\hat n\cdot\vec\sigma)} = \cos\!\left(\frac{\omega t}{2}\right)I - i\sin\!\left(\frac{\omega t}{2}\right)(\hat n\cdot\vec\sigma)
$$

This is **exactly** the `rotationAboutAxis` function already built in
this platform's quantum engine for the Bloch Sphere Explorer, so no new
code is needed to compute it, only the substitution $\theta=\omega t$.
Time evolution under a two-level Hamiltonian *is* a rotation of the Bloch
sphere at constant angular velocity $\omega$ about the axis $\hat n$: if
you've used the Bloch Sphere Explorer's rotation gates, you've already
computed this exact evolution operator, one discrete step at a time.

## Physical Interpretation

A state that starts as an eigenstate of $H$ stays an eigenstate of $H$
for all time (the next lesson, Stationary States, proves this directly
from the formula for $U(t)$ above). A state that starts as a
superposition of two different-energy eigenstates instead **precesses**:
its measurement probabilities for other observables oscillate in time at
a frequency set by the energy difference between those eigenstates,
exactly the rotation picture just derived.

## Worked Example

**$H = \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}Z$ (so $\hat n = \hat z$). The system starts
in $|\psi(0)\rangle=|+\rangle$. Find $|\psi(t)\rangle$ and its
probability of measuring $|+\rangle$ again at time $t$.**

Using the rotation formula with $\hat n=\hat z$:

$$
U(t) = \cos\!\left(\frac{\omega t}{2}\right)I - i\sin\!\left(\frac{\omega t}{2}\right)Z
$$

Applied to $|+\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$, and using
$Z|0\rangle=|0\rangle$, $Z|1\rangle=-|1\rangle$:

$$
|\psi(t)\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left[\left(\cos\tfrac{\omega t}{2}-i\sin\tfrac{\omega t}{2}\right)|0\rangle + \left(\cos\tfrac{\omega t}{2}+i\sin\tfrac{\omega t}{2}\right)|1\rangle\right] = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(e^{-i\omega t/2}|0\rangle+e^{i\omega t/2}|1\rangle\right)
$$

The probability of measuring $|+\rangle$ again is
$|\langle+|\psi(t)\rangle|^2$. Computing $\langle+|\psi(t)\rangle =
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}e^{-i\omega t/2} +
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}e^{i\omega t/2}\right) = \tfrac12(e^{-i\omega
t/2}+e^{i\omega t/2}) = \cos(\omega t/2)$ (Euler's formula, again), the
probability is $\cos^2(\omega t/2)$: the state precesses around the
Bloch sphere's equator at angular frequency $\omega$, returning to
$|+\rangle$ exactly (probability $1$) at $t=0, \tfrac{2\pi}{\omega},
\tfrac{4\pi}{\omega},\ldots$

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Treating the Schrödinger equation as something separate from
unitarity, to be additionally postulated.** As derived above, it follows
directly from requiring the generator of unitary evolution to be
Hermitian: the two are the same statement in different forms.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Confusing $H$ (an operator) with $E$ (a number).** $H$'s *eigenvalues*
are energies; $H$ itself acts on the whole Hilbert space, not just on
one eigenstate.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Requiring $U(dt)=I-\tfrac{i}{\hbar}H\,dt$ to be unitary forces
  $H^\dagger=H$: the generator of time evolution must be Hermitian.
- This gives $U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}$, and differentiating it gives the
  Schrödinger equation $i\hbar\,d|\psi\rangle/dt = H|\psi\rangle$.
- For a two-level system, $U(t)$ is exactly the existing
  `rotationAboutAxis` function: time evolution under a qubit Hamiltonian
  is Bloch-sphere precession, reusing code rather than needing new math.
