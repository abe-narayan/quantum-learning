## Motivation

No solvable system in quantum mechanics gets used more than the harmonic
oscillator: a particle sitting in a potential well shaped like
$V(x)=\tfrac12 m\omega^2x^2$. Molecular vibrations, the electromagnetic
field's individual modes, and countless approximations all reduce to it.
The traditional route to its solution is a differential equation
(deferred to the Wave Mechanics course); this lesson takes a different,
fully rigorous route that needs nothing beyond the linear algebra already
built: **ladder operators**.

## Intuition

Instead of solving for energy eigenstates directly, define two operators,
one that steps a state *down* one energy level, one that steps it *up*,
and let the algebra determine which energies are possible at all. It is
the difference between climbing a ladder to inspect each rung and
proving, from how the ladder was welded together, that rungs can only sit
at integer heights.

The payoff is not just economy. The argument uses one commutator and
nothing about the potential's shape, so it transfers intact to every
other system with the same commutator: field modes, phonons, and the
angular-momentum ladders later in this pillar.

## Mathematical Development

### The ladder operators

Postulate two operators $a$ (**annihilation**) and $a^\dagger$
(**creation**), satisfying the single defining relation:

$$
[a, a^\dagger] = aa^\dagger - a^\dagger a = 1
$$

(the identity operator on the right: this is the harmonic oscillator's
version of the canonical commutation relation the Position and Momentum
lesson will connect to $[x,p]=i\hbar$ directly). Define the **number
operator** $N = a^\dagger a$. It's <Term id="hermitian-operator">Hermitian</Term> by direct check:
$N^\dagger = (a^\dagger a)^\dagger = a^\dagger a^{\dagger\dagger} =
a^\dagger a = N$, so by the Hermitian Operators lesson, $N$ has real
eigenvalues and an <Term id="orthonormal-basis">orthonormal eigenbasis</Term>, call it $\{|n\rangle\}$ with
$N|n\rangle = n|n\rangle$.

### a lowers n by 1; a† raises it by 1

From the defining relation, $[N,a] = [a^\dagger a, a] = a^\dagger[a,a] +
[a^\dagger,a]a = 0 + (-1)a = -a$ (using $[a,a]=0$ trivially and
$[a^\dagger,a]=-[a,a^\dagger]=-1$). So $Na = aN - a = a(N-I)$. Applying
both sides to $|n\rangle$:

$$
N(a|n\rangle) = a(N-I)|n\rangle = a(n-1)|n\rangle = (n-1)\,(a|n\rangle)
$$

So $a|n\rangle$ is an eigenvector of $N$ with eigenvalue $n-1$ (or the
zero vector): $a$ lowers the level by exactly one step. The same
computation with $[N,a^\dagger]=+a^\dagger$ gives $a^\dagger|n\rangle$
eigenvalue $n+1$.

Being an eigenvector fixes the direction but not the length, and the
length is needed to use these operators for anything numerical. Take the
norm of the raised state, converting $aa^\dagger$ into $a^\dagger a + 1$
with the defining relation:

$$
\|a^\dagger|n\rangle\|^2 = \langle n|a a^\dagger|n\rangle = \langle n|(a^\dagger a + 1)|n\rangle = n+1
$$

So $a^\dagger|n\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle$ once the phase is fixed
by convention, and the same calculation run on $a$ gives
$a|n\rangle=\sqrt{n}\,|n-1\rangle$. Those two square roots are the source
of every numerical coefficient in the rest of this lesson, and note that
the second one hands back the zero vector at $n=0$ without being told to.

### The spectrum is exactly the non-negative integers

Positive-definiteness of the <Term id="inner-product">inner product</Term> (Inner Products lesson) gives:

$$
n = \langle n|N|n\rangle = \langle n|a^\dagger a|n\rangle = \|a|n\rangle\|^2 \ge 0
$$

so every eigenvalue $n$ is non-negative. But repeatedly applying $a$
lowers $n$ by exactly $1$ each time: if $n$ weren't an integer, this
process would eventually produce a state with a **negative** eigenvalue,
contradicting the inequality just derived. The only way to avoid that is
for the lowering to terminate exactly at $n=0$, where $a|0\rangle$ is
the literal zero vector rather than a new eigenstate with a negative
label. Both requirements together force the spectrum to be exactly the
non-negative integers:

$$
n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots
$$

This is a complete derivation, not a stated fact: every step above uses
only the commutation relation and results already proven earlier in this
course.

### Energy levels

The harmonic oscillator's Hamiltonian is $H = \hbar\omega\left(N +
\tfrac12\right)$ (the connection between this $H$ and the physical
potential $\tfrac12m\omega^2x^2$ is made precise once continuous position
is developed, in the Position and Momentum lesson and beyond; for now,
take $H$'s form as the definition of "a quantum harmonic oscillator" at
this algebraic level). Its eigenvalues follow immediately:

$$
E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n+\tfrac12\right), \qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots
$$

Two features are worth naming. The levels are **equally spaced** by
$\hbar\omega$, so every transition in a pure oscillator happens at one
frequency. And the ground state has $E_0=\hbar\omega/2$ rather than zero:
the **zero-point energy**, a floor the system cannot be cooled below
because $n=0$ is where the ladder ends, not where the energy does.

Every rung sits $\hbar\omega$ above the one below it, all the way up.
Compare the hydrogen spectrum later in this pillar, where the gaps shrink
as $n$ grows and crowd together toward a limit. Even spacing is a
property of this potential, not a general rule, and it has a practical
consequence: because every gap is identical, you cannot address one
transition of a pure oscillator without driving all of them, which is
precisely why superconducting qubits are built from *anharmonic*
oscillators instead.

### A finite matrix representation

The true ladder is infinite-dimensional, but truncating to the lowest
`dimension` levels, representing $a,a^\dagger,N$ as `dimension`×`dimension`
matrices on the Fock basis $\{|0\rangle,\ldots,|{\rm dimension}-1\rangle\}$,
reproduces every identity above, with one exception: $a^\dagger$ sends the
top retained level to $0$, because the level it should raise into is not
in the basis. This is a standard approximation and a safe one as long as
the states you care about sit well below the cutoff.

That truncation is what this platform's `annihilationOperator`,
`creationOperator`, and `numberOperator` functions build, which is what
lets the ordinary finite-dimensional `Matrix` engine handle the harmonic
oscillator alongside every other operator on this site.

## Physical Interpretation

Zero-point energy is measurable rather than an artifact of the
derivation. Liquid helium refuses to freeze at atmospheric pressure no
matter how cold it gets, because the zero-point motion of its atoms is
large enough to keep the lattice from holding together; the Casimir
attraction between two uncharged plates comes from the zero-point energy
of the field modes between them.

The equal spacing shows up wherever a system approximates an oscillator.
Molecular bonds and electromagnetic field modes absorb and emit in sharp,
evenly spaced lines, each line one $n\to n\pm1$ step of a ladder like the
one derived above.

## Worked Example

**In a 5-level truncated representation ($\hbar\omega=1$), compute
$a^\dagger|2\rangle$ and the energy $E_2$.**

By the raising rule derived above, $a^\dagger|n\rangle =
\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle$, so $a^\dagger|2\rangle = \sqrt3\,|3\rangle
\approx 1.732\,|3\rangle$. Index $3$ sits below this truncation's top
index of $4$, so the truncated matrix gives the same answer the true
infinite ladder does. The energy is
$E_2=\hbar\omega(2+\tfrac12)=1\cdot2.5=2.5$.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Forgetting the zero-point energy.** $E_n=\hbar\omega n$ (without the
$+\tfrac12$) is a common slip: the ground state energy is
$\hbar\omega/2$, not $0$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Confusing $N$ (number operator, integer eigenvalues $0,1,2,\ldots$)
with $H$ (energy operator, eigenvalues $\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12)$).** $H$
is a specific linear function of $N$, not the same operator.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $a,a^\dagger$ satisfy $[a,a^\dagger]=1$; $N=a^\dagger a$ is Hermitian,
  with $a$ lowering and $a^\dagger$ raising $N$'s eigenvalue by exactly
  $1$.
- Positive-definiteness plus the integer-step lowering together force the
  spectrum of $N$ to be exactly $0,1,2,\ldots$, a complete algebraic
  derivation.
- $H=\hbar\omega(N+\tfrac12)$ gives $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12)$,
  equally spaced, with nonzero zero-point energy $E_0=\hbar\omega/2$.
- The platform's `annihilationOperator`/`creationOperator`/`numberOperator`
  functions are a finite, truncated, exact-except-at-the-boundary matrix
  representation of this same algebra.
