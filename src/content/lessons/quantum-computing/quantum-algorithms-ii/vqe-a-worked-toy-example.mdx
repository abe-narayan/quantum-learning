## Motivation

Proving an algorithm can't be wrong in one direction is one thing.
Proving it actually lands close to the right answer is another. The
last lesson showed VQE's cost function can never dip below the true
ground energy, a real guarantee, but not yet a demonstration that the
algorithm gets anywhere close to it. This lesson checks the other
direction directly: run the real optimizer on a specific Hamiltonian,
not a hand-picked success story, and see how close it actually gets.

## Mathematical Development

### The Hamiltonian and its exact answer

$$
H = 0.6\,Z + 0.8\,X
$$

Both coefficients chosen so $\sqrt{0.6^2+0.8^2}=1$ exactly: for any
Hamiltonian of the form $aX+bY+cZ$, the eigenvalues are exactly
$\pm\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$ (a standard fact about Pauli combinations,
consistent with this platform's `eigenvaluesHermitian2x2` giving the
closed-form quadratic solution directly). So $E_0=-1$ here, exactly,
computed independently via `exactGroundStateEnergy`, not asserted.

### Running the optimizer

`runVqe(H, 80)` starts at $(\theta,\varphi)=(\pi/2,0)$ (the state
$|+\rangle$) and allows *up to* 80 pattern-search steps, but 80 is only a
ceiling, not a count of what actually runs: the loop has a second, real
halting condition, stopping as soon as its step size shrinks to $10^{-6}$
or below. For this Hamiltonian that threshold is hit after just 28
iterations (`toyVqeResult.history.length - 1`), so the run exits fewer
than halfway to its 80-step budget without ever coming close to using it.
The cost history decreases monotonically (proven structurally: the
optimizer only ever accepts a strictly better candidate, checked directly
in this course's tests) and converges to an energy within $10^{-12}$ of
$-1$, far tighter than needed for any practical purpose — and it's exactly
that shrinking step size, not the iteration cap, that ends the run.

Hamiltonian, not a smoothed illustration:

Every step either strictly improves the cost or shrinks the search step;

variational principle from the last lesson guarantees.

### Reading the convergence

The optimizer's step size started at $0.5$ radians and halved every time
no candidate direction improved the cost, a direct, visible signal of
approaching a local (here, also global, since the ansatz is fully
expressive) minimum: a smooth cost function's gradient shrinks toward zero
near a minimum, so smaller and smaller steps are needed before any
direction shows further improvement.

## Physical Interpretation

This is the entire VQE idea, stripped to its smallest working case: a
quantum circuit measures an energy, a classical loop nudges the circuit's
parameters toward lower energy, and because of the variational
principle from the last lesson, every step of that process is
trustworthy in the sense that it's always searching from *above* the true
answer, never mistakenly below it.

## Worked Example

**Verify $H$'s eigenvalues directly via the quadratic formula, without
citing the general $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$ shortcut.**

$H=\begin{pmatrix}0.6&0.8\\0.8&-0.6\end{pmatrix}$ (since
$Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$).
Trace $=0$, determinant $=(0.6)(-0.6)-(0.8)(0.8)=-0.36-0.64=-1$.
Eigenvalues solve $\lambda^2-0\lambda-1=0$, i.e. $\lambda^2=1$, giving
$\lambda=\pm1$, exactly $E_0=-1$, matching both the shortcut formula and
`exactGroundStateEnergy`'s direct output.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Expecting VQE to converge in one or two iterations.** Even for this
simple, fully expressive single-qubit case, meaningful convergence took
dozens of pattern-search steps. Larger, more realistic ansätze typically
need many more, and are far more prone to getting stuck in poor local
regions of the cost landscape (an active, ongoing challenge in real VQE
research this toy example is too simple to exhibit).
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming the found parameters $(\theta,\varphi)$ are unique.** Multiple
parameter settings can give the same ground state (global phase, or
different $\varphi$ values when $\theta=0$ or $\pi$ make the state
insensitive to $\varphi$). The *energy* converges reliably; the exact
parameter values found can vary between runs with different starting
points.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $H=0.6Z+0.8X$ has exact ground energy $-1$, confirmed independently via
  both the quadratic formula and this platform's closed-form eigensolver.
- `runVqe` converges to within $10^{-12}$ of this exact value after just 28
  pattern-search iterations, halted early by its step-size shrinking below
  $10^{-6}$, well short of the 80-iteration budget it was allowed, with a
  monotonically decreasing cost history throughout.
- Shrinking step sizes are a direct signal of approaching a local (here,
  global) minimum of a smooth cost landscape.
