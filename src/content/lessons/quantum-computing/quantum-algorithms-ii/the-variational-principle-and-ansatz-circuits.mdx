## Motivation

Shor's algorithm is a fixed sequence of gates, run once, with a classical
post-processing step at the end. **<Term id="vqe">VQE (the variational quantum
eigensolver)</Term>** and **QAOA** work completely differently: a
parameterized circuit is run many times, with a *classical optimizer*
adjusting its parameters between runs to improve some measured quantity,
a genuinely hybrid loop, not a one-shot circuit. This lesson derives the
mathematical fact that makes that loop meaningful at all.

## Mathematical Development

### The variational principle, proved

Let $H$ be a Hermitian <Term id="hamiltonians-time-evolution">Hamiltonian</Term> with eigenvalues
$E_0\le E_1\le\cdots$ and corresponding orthonormal eigenstates
$|\phi_0\rangle,|\phi_1\rangle,\dots$ ($E_0$ the ground-state energy). Any
normalized state $|\psi\rangle$ can be written in this eigenbasis:
$|\psi\rangle=\sum_ic_i|\phi_i\rangle$, with $\sum_i|c_i|^2=1$. Then:

$$
\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle = \sum_i|c_i|^2E_i \geq \sum_i|c_i|^2E_0 = E_0\sum_i|c_i|^2 = E_0
$$

using $E_i\geq E_0$ for every $i$ in the inequality step. So
**$\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle\geq E_0$ for every normalized $|\psi\rangle$,
with equality exactly when $|\psi\rangle=|\phi_0\rangle$** (any weight on
a higher-energy eigenstate strictly increases the expectation value,
unless that eigenstate happens to be degenerate with $E_0$). This is the
**variational principle**: the ground energy is a genuine lower bound on
every state's energy expectation value, and minimizing that expectation
value over some family of states can only approach $E_0$ from above,
never overshoot below it.

### The ansatz and the hybrid loop

An **<Term id="ansatz">ansatz</Term>** is a parameterized circuit $|\psi(\vec\theta)\rangle$. This
platform's single-qubit VQE uses
$|\psi(\theta,\varphi)\rangle=R_z(\varphi)R_y(\theta)|0\rangle$, which
reaches *every* point on the Bloch sphere as $\theta,\varphi$ vary, so it
can represent the exact ground state of *any* single-qubit Hamiltonian,
not merely approximate it. The **hybrid loop**: measure
$\langle\psi(\vec\theta)|H|\psi(\vec\theta)\rangle$ on the quantum device
(via `costFunction`, reusing `expectationValue` directly), feed that
number to a classical optimizer, get updated parameters, repeat.
`runVqe` implements this with pattern search (try a step in each parameter
direction, keep whichever improves the cost, shrink the step when nothing
does), a genuine, if simple, optimizer, not a hardcoded answer.

### Why the variational principle matters for an imperfect optimizer

The variational principle guarantees `runVqe`'s output energy is *always*
at least $E_0$, no matter how badly the optimizer performs or how few
iterations it runs, a real safety property this course's tests check
directly (`result.energy >= exact - 1e-6` for every Hamiltonian tried).
The optimizer can fail to *reach* $E_0$; the variational principle
guarantees it can never report something *below* $E_0$, which would signal
a bug (in the ansatz, the cost function, or the optimizer) rather than a
better answer.

## Physical Interpretation

VQE and QAOA are built for a different regime than Shor's algorithm: they
tolerate imperfect answers (an approximate ground energy, a near-optimal
cut) in exchange for using much shallower circuits. This is the reason
this family of algorithms is the leading candidate for near-term ("<Term id="nisq">NISQ</Term>",
covered in the capstone) hardware that can't yet run Shor's deep,
long-coherence-time circuits reliably.

## Worked Example

**For $H=Z$ (eigenvalues $+1,-1$), confirm the variational principle holds
for the ansatz state at $\theta=\pi/2,\varphi=0$ (i.e. $|+\rangle$).**

$Z|+\rangle=|-\rangle$, and
$\langle+|Z|+\rangle=\langle+|-\rangle=0$ (an even, $50/50$ mix of the two
eigenvalues under this particular state). $E_0=-1$ for $H=Z$, and indeed
$0\geq-1$: the variational principle holds, though this particular
$\theta,\varphi$ is far from optimal (the true minimizer is
$\theta=\pi,\varphi=0$, giving $|1\rangle$, energy exactly $-1$). Placed
on the actual energy spectrum, the inequality is a picture, not just an
arithmetic check: the trial value has nowhere to go but at or above the
floor $E_0$ sets. That isn't special to $\theta=\pi/2$.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Believing a lower measured energy is always more trustworthy.** The
variational principle says the *true* minimum is a hard floor. If an
implementation ever reports an energy below the Hamiltonian's actual
smallest eigenvalue, that's evidence of a bug, not a better answer.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Assuming any ansatz can represent any Hamiltonian's ground state.**
This platform's specific $R_z R_y$ single-qubit ansatz can (it spans the
whole Bloch sphere), but a poorly chosen or insufficiently expressive
ansatz for a larger system can systematically fail to reach $E_0$ no
matter how well the classical optimizer runs, a real and active
limitation of VQE in practice.
</Callout>

## Summary

- The variational principle, $\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle\geq E_0$ for any
  normalized $|\psi\rangle$, follows directly from expanding $|\psi\rangle$
  in $H$'s eigenbasis.
- An ansatz circuit parameterizes a family of trial states; the hybrid
  loop alternates quantum expectation-value measurement with classical
  parameter updates.
- This platform's single-qubit ansatz is exactly expressive enough to
  reach any single-qubit ground state, verified directly, not merely
  argued.
- The variational principle guarantees the optimizer's output energy is
  never below the true ground energy, a genuine safety check built into
  this course's own tests.
