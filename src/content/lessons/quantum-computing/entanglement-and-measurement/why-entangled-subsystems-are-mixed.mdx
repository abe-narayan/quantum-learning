<LessonHook eyebrow="Why this matters">
Here is the fact the last lesson left on the table. The 2-qubit state |Φ+⟩ is pure: fully specified, with no uncertainty about it at all. Yet each of its two qubits, taken alone, is in the most uncertain single-qubit state there is.
</LessonHook>

## Motivation

The Bell state's <Term id="partial-trace">reduced density matrix</Term> works out to $\rho_A=I/2$. The whole
is perfectly well-defined; either half alone is maximally undefined. Read
slowly, that should sound like a contradiction: how can you know
everything about a pair and nothing about either member?

It is not a quirk of $|\Phi^+\rangle$'s 50/50 amplitudes. This lesson
proves the effect is forced for any 2-qubit pure state, and produces an
exact formula for how mixed a subsystem becomes as a function of how
entangled the whole state is. The formula turns out to be one line, and it
connects to the separability determinant this platform already computes.

## Mathematical Development

### Setting up the general 2-qubit case

Let $|\psi\rangle=a|00\rangle+b|01\rangle+c|10\rangle+d|11\rangle$ be any
normalized 2-qubit state. Quantum Gates & Circuits already established
(and this course's Concurrence lesson will lean on again) that
$|\psi\rangle$ is a product state exactly when $ad-bc=0$: that's
precisely `testSeparability`'s determinant test.

The partial trace (last lesson) gives qubit 0's reduced state:

$$
\rho_A = \begin{pmatrix} |a|^2+|b|^2 & ac^*+bd^* \\ (ac^*+bd^*)^* & |c|^2+|d|^2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Write $p=|a|^2+|b|^2$ (so $q=|c|^2+|d|^2=1-p$ by normalization) and
$z=ac^*+bd^*$ for the off-diagonal entry.

### The purity identity, derived

For any $2\times2$ density matrix $\begin{pmatrix}p&z\\z^*&1-p\end{pmatrix}$,
direct matrix multiplication gives
$\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)=p^2+(1-p)^2+2|z|^2$. So:

$$
1-\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2) = 1-p^2-(1-p)^2-2|z|^2 = 2p(1-p)-2|z|^2 = 2\big(pq-|z|^2\big)
$$

Now expand $pq-|z|^2$ directly in terms of $a,b,c,d$:

$$
pq = |a|^2|c|^2+|a|^2|d|^2+|b|^2|c|^2+|b|^2|d|^2
$$

$$
|z|^2 = |ac^*+bd^*|^2 = |a|^2|c|^2+|b|^2|d|^2 + 2\,\text{Re}(ab^*c^*d)
$$

$$
pq-|z|^2 = |a|^2|d|^2+|b|^2|c|^2-2\,\text{Re}(ab^*c^*d)
$$

Compare this to $|ad-bc|^2=(ad-bc)(a^*d^*-b^*c^*)=|a|^2|d|^2+|b|^2|c|^2-2\,\text{Re}(ad\,b^*c^*)$.
Since complex multiplication commutes, $ab^*c^*d=ad\,b^*c^*$ (the same
product, just reordered), so $pq-|z|^2=|ad-bc|^2$ exactly. Substituting
back:

$$
\boxed{1-\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2) = 2|ad-bc|^2}
$$

An exact algebraic identity, holding for *every* normalized 2-qubit pure
state, with no approximation anywhere in the derivation. It is verified
numerically against the engine (`purity` composed with
`reducedDensityMatrixQubit0`, compared to `testSeparability`'s
determinant) for a Bell state, a product state, a partially entangled
state, and an unstructured "random-looking" state; all four agree to
floating-point precision.

The mechanism behind the identity is visible in the partial trace itself.
The $|00\rangle\langle11|$ cross term of $\rho_{AB}$, of size $ad$, has
mismatched bra and ket indices on *both* qubits, so it never survives into
$\rho_A$ (or $\rho_B$). That excluded cross term, which grows with
$ad-bc$, is what $\rho_A$ loses track of.
For each state below, all three presets have the same amplitude pattern
($a|00\rangle+d|11\rangle$), so $\rho_A$ stays exactly diagonal
throughout; what changes is how far that diagonal sits from $1{:}0$:

### The theorem

Since $|ad-bc|^2\geq0$ always, the identity immediately gives
$\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)\leq1$, with equality **exactly when** $ad-bc=0$,
exactly `testSeparability`'s condition for a product state. This proves,
for 2-qubit pure states:

$$
\rho_A \text{ is pure} \iff |\psi\rangle_{AB} \text{ is a product state} \iff \rho_A \text{ is not mixed}
$$

Equivalently: **a 2-qubit pure state's reduced state is mixed if and only
if the state is entangled.** Not "mixed states are associated with
entanglement" as a slogan, but an if-and-only-if, with the amount of
mixedness ($1-\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)$) pinned to how far the separability
determinant sits from zero.

### Scope: this is a 2-qubit statement

This proof used the 2-qubit reduced-density-matrix formula explicitly.
It is not a general $n$-qubit argument. The underlying idea (a pure
global state has a mixed reduced state exactly when there's entanglement
across the cut) is true far more generally, provable with the <Term id="schmidt-decomposition">Schmidt
decomposition</Term> for any bipartite split of any dimension, but that
general proof needs a general eigendecomposition, which this platform does
not implement (see `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`). The boxed identity is airtight
for the case this course uses: two qubits, one cut.

## Physical Interpretation

A reduced state's mixedness is not uncertainty leaking in, the way it was
for the classical mixtures two lessons ago. The global state
$|\psi\rangle_{AB}$ is fully known. The mixedness of $\rho_A$ is instead a
signature of *correlation with something outside the subsystem being
described*.

This is the sharpest sense in which entanglement is not classical
correlation. Complete knowledge of the whole gives incomplete knowledge of
each part, whenever the parts are entangled. Nothing in classical physics
works that way: there, knowing the joint distribution exactly always
determines each marginal exactly, and the marginals stay as sharp as the
joint.

Seeing that phrase as a real correlation, rather than an algebraic
byproduct of the <Term id="von-neumann-entropy-purity">purity</Term> identity, is easiest in the maximally entangled case:
$|\Phi^+\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)$. Alice and Bob
each hold one qubit, and alone each holds the maximally mixed
$\rho_A=\rho_B=I/2$ derived above. The interactive below samples one real
joint measurement at a time, built with the platform's engine (`HADAMARD`
then `applyCNOT`, the construction the CHSH lesson uses) rather than a
hand-written coin flip. Watch both glyphs, held far apart, land on the
same outcome every time:

## Worked Example

**Use the boxed identity to find $\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)$ for
$|\psi\rangle=\sqrt{0.9}\,|00\rangle+\sqrt{0.1}\,|11\rangle$ without
computing the partial trace directly.**

Here $a=\sqrt{0.9}$, $b=c=0$, $d=\sqrt{0.1}$, so
$ad-bc=\sqrt{0.9}\sqrt{0.1}=\sqrt{0.09}=0.3$. By the identity,
$1-\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)=2(0.3)^2=0.18$, so $\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)=0.82$,
closer to 1 (less mixed) than the Bell state's 0.5, matching the
intuition that this state is less entangled than a maximally entangled
Bell pair (its amplitudes are $90\%/10\%$, not $50\%/50\%$).

$\rho_A=\text{diag}(0.9,0.1)$ is itself just a Z-basis mixture of
$|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ weighted 90/10, which is the kind of object
the density matrix explorer builds directly:

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="note">
**Thinking any nonzero purity loss means "maximal" entanglement.**
$1-\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)$ ranges continuously from 0 (product state) to
0.5 (maximally entangled, at $|ad-bc|=0.5$, the largest a normalized state
allows). It is a graded quantity, not a yes/no flag.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Assuming this proof generalizes automatically to 3+ qubits, or to
mixed global states.** It does not, without further work this platform
does not undertake (see the Scope note above). The 2-qubit proof uses the
2-qubit reduced-density-matrix formula by name, and that formula changes
the moment there is a third qubit.
</Callout>

## Summary

- For any normalized 2-qubit pure state $a|00\rangle+b|01\rangle+c|10\rangle+d|11\rangle$,
  $1-\text{Tr}(\rho_A^2)=2|ad-bc|^2$: an exact algebraic identity, proven
  by direct expansion.
- Consequently, $\rho_A$ is pure if and only if the global state is a
  product state: entanglement and reduced-state mixedness are
  mathematically equivalent for 2-qubit pure states.
- The mixedness of an entangled subsystem's reduced state reflects
  correlation with the rest of the system, not any uncertainty about the
  (perfectly well-defined) global state.
- This specific proof is a 2-qubit result; the broader idea generalizes
  via the Schmidt decomposition, which this platform does not implement
  in general.
