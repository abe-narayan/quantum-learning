## Mathematical Development

### The quantum correlation

For two single-qubit $\pm1$-valued <Term id="observable">observables</Term> $M_a,M_b$ (Hermitian,
eigenvalues exactly $\pm1$; see `spinObservableInXZPlane`, which builds
$M(\theta)=\cos\theta\,Z+\sin\theta\,X$, a spin measurement at angle
$\theta$ from $Z$ in the $X$-$Z$ plane, verified Hermitian with trace 0
and determinant $-1$ for any $\theta$), quantum mechanics predicts the
correlation as an expectation value of the combined observable:

$$
E(a,b) = \langle M_a\otimes M_b\rangle = \text{Tr}\big[\rho\,(M_a\otimes M_b)\big]
$$

This is exactly Lesson 9's generalized Born-rule expectation-value
formula, applied to the two-outcome product observable $M_a\otimes M_b$,
the same quantity a local hidden-variable model calls
$E(a,b)=\int A(a,\lambda)B(b,\lambda)p(\lambda)\,d\lambda$ (both are
"the average of the product of two $\pm1$ outcomes"), which is exactly
why the two theories' predictions for $S$ can be directly compared.
`correlationExpectation` implements $\text{Tr}[\rho(M_a\otimes M_b)]$
directly.

### Deriving E(a,b) = cos(θₐ − θ_b) for |Φ+⟩

Write $M(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}c&s\\s&-c\end{pmatrix}$ with
$c=\cos\theta,s=\sin\theta$. For $|\Phi^+\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)$:

$$
\langle\Phi^+|M(\theta_a)\otimes M(\theta_b)|\Phi^+\rangle = \tfrac12\Big[\langle00|M_aM_b|00\rangle+\langle00|M_aM_b|11\rangle+\langle11|M_aM_b|00\rangle+\langle11|M_aM_b|11\rangle\Big]
$$

Each term is a product of one matrix element from $M(\theta_a)$ and one
from $M(\theta_b)$: $\langle00|\cdot|00\rangle$ uses each operator's
$(0,0)$ entry, $c_ac_b$; $\langle11|\cdot|11\rangle$ uses each $(1,1)$
entry, $(-c_a)(-c_b)=c_ac_b$; the two cross terms
$\langle00|\cdot|11\rangle$ and $\langle11|\cdot|00\rangle$ each use the
$(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ entries respectively, both equal to $s_as_b$. Summing:

$$
E(a,b) = \tfrac12\big[c_ac_b + s_as_b + s_as_b + c_ac_b\big] = c_ac_b+s_as_b = \cos\theta_a\cos\theta_b+\sin\theta_a\sin\theta_b = \cos(\theta_a-\theta_b)
$$

using the angle-difference identity. A complete, exact derivation for
$|\Phi^+\rangle$ specifically, not a numerical fit. It is additionally
checked directly against `correlationExpectation`'s numerical output for
several angle pairs, as a regression test.

### The standard configuration: S = 2√2, exactly

Choose $\theta_a=0$ ($A=Z$), $\theta_{a'}=\pi/2$ ($A'=X$),
$\theta_b=\pi/4$, $\theta_{b'}=-\pi/4$, the standard optimal CHSH
configuration. Using $E(a,b)=\cos(\theta_a-\theta_b)$ for each term:

$$
S = \cos\!\big({-}\tfrac\pi4\big) + \cos\!\big(\tfrac\pi4\big) + \cos\!\big(\tfrac\pi4\big) - \cos\!\big(\tfrac{3\pi}4\big)
$$

Using $\cos(-\pi/4)=\cos(\pi/4)$ and $\cos(3\pi/4)=-\cos(\pi/4)$, every
term becomes $\cos(\pi/4)=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$, with the last term's minus
sign combined with $\cos(3\pi/4)$'s own minus sign flipping it positive:

$$
S = \cos\tfrac\pi4+\cos\tfrac\pi4+\cos\tfrac\pi4+\cos\tfrac\pi4 = 4\cdot\frac{\sqrt2}{2} = 2\sqrt2 \approx 2.828
$$

$2\sqrt2>2$: **the CHSH inequality is violated**, by an entangled
quantum state, at this specific and fully specified measurement
configuration. `chshValue` computes exactly this and returns
$2.8284271247461903$ for $|\Phi^+\rangle$ at these angles, matching
$2\sqrt2$ to floating-point precision, and additionally confirmed (by a
fine-grained numerical search over $\theta_b,\theta_{b'}$ with
$\theta_a,\theta_{a'}$ held fixed) to be the true maximum reachable by
*any* choice of $B,B'$ for this state, not merely a locally good guess.

### Tsirelson's bound

$2\sqrt2$ is not a coincidence of this particular state and these
particular angles: it is provably the maximum $|S|$ *any* quantum state
and *any* choice of observables can ever reach (**Tsirelson's bound**,
`CHSH_QUANTUM_BOUND`). Proving that in full generality requires operator-norm
bounds beyond this course's scope; what this lesson establishes directly
is that this specific, fully worked configuration reaches it exactly,
and that it cannot be improved by retuning $B,B'$ for this state (via
the numerical search above), strong, if not fully general, evidence for
the bound's tightness.

## Physical Interpretation

A CHSH violation, observed in a real laboratory (this has been done,
repeatedly, closing progressively more loopholes over decades), rules
out *every* local hidden-variable explanation simultaneously: not by
refuting one specific classical model, but by exceeding a bound that
Lesson 10 proved holds for the entire category. This is the precise,
falsifiable, and already-tested sense in which entanglement is not
classical correlation with unknown extra detail. It's a strictly
stronger kind of correlation, quantitatively bounded away from anything
locality permits.

## Worked Example

**Using $E(a,b)=\cos(\theta_a-\theta_b)$, compute $S$ if Bob's settings
are swapped ($\theta_b=-\pi/4,\theta_{b'}=\pi/4$) instead of the standard
choice.**

$$
S = \cos(0-(-\tfrac\pi4)) + \cos(0-\tfrac\pi4) + \cos(\tfrac\pi2-(-\tfrac\pi4)) - \cos(\tfrac\pi2-\tfrac\pi4)
= \cos\tfrac\pi4+\cos\tfrac\pi4+\cos\tfrac{3\pi}4-\cos\tfrac\pi4
$$

$=\cos(\pi/4)+\cos(3\pi/4) = \frac{\sqrt2}2-\frac{\sqrt2}2=0$. Simply
swapping which angle is called $b$ versus $b'$ changes which correlations
get a $+$ and which get a $-$ in $S$'s definition: the *maximum*
achievable violation depends on getting this assignment right, not just
on using the same four physical measurement angles.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Assuming any entangled state violates CHSH at any angles.** As the
worked example shows, even the maximally entangled $|\Phi^+\rangle$ gives
$S=0$ at a poorly chosen (or mis-assigned) set of angles. Violation
requires both an entangled state *and* a well-chosen measurement
configuration: entanglement is necessary but not sufficient for a given
choice of angles to show a violation.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Treating $2\sqrt2$ as an approximation.** It is an exact value,
reachable exactly (not merely approached) by the derivation above. The
engine's output ($2.8284271247461903$) differs from $2\sqrt2$ only by
ordinary floating-point rounding, not by any physical approximation.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $E(a,b)=\text{Tr}[\rho(M_a\otimes M_b)]$ is the quantum correlation,
  directly comparable to a local hidden-variable model's $E(a,b)$.
- For $|\Phi^+\rangle$ and in-plane spin observables,
  $E(a,b)=\cos(\theta_a-\theta_b)$ exactly, derived by direct
  calculation, not fit to data.
- At the standard configuration, $S=2\sqrt2\approx2.828$, exactly,
  violating the classical bound of 2 proven in the last lesson.
- Mixing noise into the state reduces $S$ monotonically, crossing back
  below the classical bound well before the state becomes fully
  unentangled, a quantitative link between mixedness and the strength
  of nonlocal correlation.
