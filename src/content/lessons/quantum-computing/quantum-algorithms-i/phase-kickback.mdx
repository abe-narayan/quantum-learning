## Motivation

The last lesson's oracle $U_f|x\rangle|y\rangle=|x\rangle|y\oplus f(x)\rangle$
writes $f(x)$'s value into a second qubit. That's the *honest* definition,
but every algorithm from here on actually wants $f(x)$'s value somewhere
else entirely: as a **phase** multiplying $|x\rangle$ directly, with no
second qubit needed at all. This lesson derives exactly when and why that
substitution is legitimate.

## Mathematical Development

### The derivation

Prepare the output qubit not in $|0\rangle$, but in
$|-\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)$, and apply $U_f$:

$$
U_f|x\rangle|-\rangle = U_f|x\rangle\frac{|0\rangle-|1\rangle}{\sqrt2} = \frac{U_f|x\rangle|0\rangle - U_f|x\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt2} = \frac{|x\rangle|0\oplus f(x)\rangle - |x\rangle|1\oplus f(x)\rangle}{\sqrt2}
$$

Two cases, both worth writing out fully. If $f(x)=0$:
$0\oplus0=0,\ 1\oplus0=1$, giving
$\frac{|x\rangle|0\rangle-|x\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}=|x\rangle|-\rangle$,
unchanged. If $f(x)=1$: $0\oplus1=1,\ 1\oplus1=0$, giving
$\frac{|x\rangle|1\rangle-|x\rangle|0\rangle}{\sqrt2}=-|x\rangle|-\rangle$,
picks up an overall minus sign. Both cases combine into one formula:

$$
\boxed{U_f|x\rangle|-\rangle = (-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle|-\rangle}
$$

### Why this is called "kickback"

The ancilla started in $|-\rangle$ and **ends in $|-\rangle$, completely
unchanged**. The phase $(-1)^{f(x)}$ that "should" have landed on the
ancilla (the qubit $U_f$ actually acts on) instead shows up attached to
$|x\rangle$, the register $U_f$ was never defined to modify directly. This
is why it's called *kickback*: the effect of the computation gets kicked
back onto the control-like register instead of staying on the target.

### Linearity extends this to a superposition over x

$U_f$ is linear, so applying it to $\left(\sum_x c_x|x\rangle\right)|-\rangle$
distributes the identity above over every term:

$$
U_f\left(\sum_x c_x|x\rangle\right)|-\rangle = \left(\sum_x (-1)^{f(x)}c_x|x\rangle\right)|-\rangle
$$

The ancilla factors out completely and stays $|-\rangle$ throughout; after
this step it can be ignored (or discarded) entirely, and what remains is
exactly `applyPhaseOracle`'s action: $\sum_x c_x|x\rangle \to \sum_x
(-1)^{f(x)}c_x|x\rangle$, i.e. a sign flip on exactly the $x$ where $f(x)=1$.
This is not a second, independent definition: it's the same $U_f$, checked
directly in `oracles.test.ts` by preparing $|+\rangle|-\rangle$, applying
`applyBitOracle`, and confirming the reduced state on the $x$ register
matches `applyPhaseOracle` applied directly, to floating-point precision.

## Physical Interpretation

Phase kickback is the mechanism that turns "compute $f$" into "multiply
each branch of a superposition by a sign depending on $f$," a *global*
property of the whole superposition rather than a per-branch classical bit.
Once $f(x)$'s value is a phase, it becomes something interference (via
Hadamards, or the QFT) can act on, which is precisely why this trick shows
up as the very first step inside Deutsch-Jozsa and Grover's algorithm both.

## Worked Example

**Confirm the kickback identity directly for $x$ such that $f(x)=1$, using
$|-\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)$ and the oracle's action on
each basis term separately.**

$U_f|x\rangle|0\rangle = |x\rangle|0\oplus1\rangle=|x\rangle|1\rangle$.
$U_f|x\rangle|1\rangle=|x\rangle|1\oplus1\rangle=|x\rangle|0\rangle$. So:

$$
U_f|x\rangle\frac{|0\rangle-|1\rangle}{\sqrt2} = \frac{|x\rangle|1\rangle - |x\rangle|0\rangle}{\sqrt2} = -\frac{|x\rangle|0\rangle-|x\rangle|1\rangle}{\sqrt2} = -|x\rangle|-\rangle
$$

Exactly $(-1)^1|x\rangle|-\rangle = -|x\rangle|-\rangle$, matching the boxed
formula for $f(x)=1$.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Thinking the ancilla's state changes.** It doesn't: $|-\rangle$ is an
eigenstate of the "apply $X$ conditioned on $f(x)$" operation with
eigenvalue $(-1)^{f(x)}$, and applying an operator to one of its own
eigenstates never changes the state, only multiplies it by the eigenvalue.
</Callout>

<Callout type="mistake">
**Assuming this trick works for *any* ancilla state, not just $|-\rangle$
or $|0\rangle$.** Preparing the ancilla in $|0\rangle$ gives the ordinary
bit-flip oracle from the last lesson; preparing it in, say, $|+\rangle$
gives neither a clean phase nor a clean bit flip: $|-\rangle$ is
specifically the state that makes $X$ act as a $-1$ eigenvalue, which is
exactly what this derivation needs.
</Callout>

## Summary

- $U_f|x\rangle|-\rangle=(-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle|-\rangle$, derived directly
  from $U_f$'s XOR definition, not asserted.
- The ancilla, prepared in $|-\rangle$, is left completely unchanged; the
  effect of $f(x)$ is "kicked back" onto $|x\rangle$ as a phase instead.
- By linearity, this extends to any superposition over $x$, reproducing
  exactly `applyPhaseOracle`'s action, verified directly against
  `applyBitOracle`, not just defined to match.
