## Every Channel as a Unitary in Disguise

Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi proved every completely positive,
trace-preserving map $\mathcal E$ has a Kraus representation
$\mathcal E(\rho)=\sum_iK_i\rho K_i^\dagger$, by diagonalizing its Choi
matrix. That proof is a theorem, but it is entirely about
*matrices*: nowhere does it say what a Kraus operator physically *is*.
Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators introduced the amplitude-damping
$K_0,K_1$ as "the" description of spontaneous decay, without ever
showing the unitary, closed-system process that decay is secretly built
from. This lesson closes that gap: every Kraus channel, with no
exception, comes from ordinary Schrödinger evolution on a *larger*,
closed system, followed by not looking at part of it. That
<Term id="stinespring-dilation">theorem is the **Stinespring dilation**</Term>, and building its qubit-scale
realization explicitly is this lesson's payoff.

## The Isometry Hiding Inside a Kraus Set

### Building V out of the Kraus set

Take any CPTP map $\mathcal E$ on a $d$-dimensional system $S$ with
Kraus operators $\{K_i\}_{i=1}^r$, $\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i=I_S$ (the
prior lesson's Choi-rank argument already bounds $r\leq d^2$, since the
Choi matrix is only $d^2\times d^2$ and has at most $d^2$ nonzero
eigenvalues; that bound is what pins the environment dimension
below). Introduce an $r$-dimensional auxiliary system $E$ (the
**environment**) with orthonormal basis $\{|i\rangle_E\}$, and define a
linear map $V:\mathcal H_S\to\mathcal H_S\otimes\mathcal H_E$ by

$$
V|\psi\rangle_S \equiv \sum_{i=1}^r K_i|\psi\rangle_S\otimes|i\rangle_E
$$

**Claim: $V$ is an isometry**, i.e. $V^\dagger V=I_S$, which is what
makes it extendable to a full unitary, computed below.

### Extending $V$ to a unitary, and recovering the channel

<DerivationSteps>
  <DerivationStep annotation="Check directly on inner products, using ⟨i|j⟩ = δ_ij and the Kraus completeness relation Σ K_i†K_i = I in the last step. An isometry preserves every inner product, so V embeds H_S inside the larger H_S ⊗ H_E without distortion.">
    $$
    \langle\phi|V^\dagger V|\psi\rangle = \sum_{i,j}\langle\phi|K_i^\dagger K_j|\psi\rangle\langle i|j\rangle_E = \sum_i\langle\phi|K_i^\dagger K_i|\psi\rangle = \langle\phi|\Big(\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i\Big)|\psi\rangle = \langle\phi|\psi\rangle
    $$
  </DerivationStep>
  <DerivationStep annotation="V's image is a d-dimensional subspace of the d·r-dimensional joint space, isometric to H_S itself. Complete an orthonormal basis of that image's orthogonal complement, and map any fixed orthonormal basis of the 'extra' domain onto it. This is the same completion-to-an-orthonormal-basis move Schmidt Decomposition and Purification used to extend a basis beyond a degenerate eigenspace. This produces a unitary U on H_S ⊗ H_E, for the fixed reference state |0⟩_E.">
    $$
    U\big(|\psi\rangle_S\otimes|0\rangle_E\big) = V|\psi\rangle_S = \sum_iK_i|\psi\rangle_S\otimes|i\rangle_E
    $$
  </DerivationStep>
  <DerivationStep annotation="Now compute what tracing out E recovers, for a pure input |ψ⟩⟨ψ|_S, directly from the definition of V above.">
    $$
    U\big(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E\big)U^\dagger = V|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|V^\dagger = \sum_{i,j}K_i|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|K_j^\dagger\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|_E
    $$
  </DerivationStep>
  <DerivationStep annotation="Using Tr(|i⟩⟨j|) = δ_ij. This is exactly E(|ψ⟩⟨ψ|), and extends to any mixed ρ by linearity: a density matrix is a convex combination of pure-state projectors, each traced through identically.">
    $$
    \text{Tr}_E\Big[\,U\big(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E\big)U^\dagger\Big] = \sum_{i,j}K_i|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|K_j^\dagger\,\text{Tr}(|i\rangle\langle j|_E) = \sum_iK_i|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|K_i^\dagger
    $$
  </DerivationStep>
</DerivationSteps>

This is the theorem:

<TheoremBox title="Stinespring dilation" provenance="derived">

**Hypotheses.** $\mathcal E$ is completely positive and trace-preserving
on a Hilbert space of *finite* dimension $d$. Complete positivity is
what supplies the Kraus set the isometry is built from; trace
preservation is what makes $V^\dagger V=I$ rather than $\leq I$; and
finiteness is what lets the isometry be completed to a *unitary* instead
of stopping at an isometry.

Every such $\mathcal E$ arises as

<EquationReveal
  terms={[
    { id: "E", symbol: "ℰ(ρ)", gloss: "The channel's output, the same CPTP map whose Kraus form was built in the prior lesson." },
    { id: "U", symbol: "U", gloss: "A unitary acting on system + environment together, which is ordinary reversible Schrödinger evolution." },
    { id: "env", symbol: "|0⟩⟨0|_E", gloss: "A fixed, pure environment state, the ancilla the system is coupled to." },
    { id: "TrE", symbol: "Tr_E[·]", gloss: "Partial trace over the environment, discarding (not measuring) it after the joint evolution." },
  ]}
>

$$
\boxed{\mathcal E(\rho) = \text{Tr}_E\Big[\,U\big(\rho\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E\big)U^\dagger\,\Big]}
$$

</EquationReveal>

for some unitary $U$ on system + environment and some fixed pure
environment state $|0\rangle_E$, with environment dimension always
achievable at $\leq d^2$.

**Uniqueness.** The dilation is not unique, but the *minimal* one (the
one with $\dim\mathcal H_E$ equal to the Kraus rank) is unique up to a
unitary acting on $E$ alone. That is the same statement the
non-uniqueness section below reads off from the Kraus side.

Stinespring's original 1955 theorem is stated for completely positive
maps on C\*-algebras and does not need $d<\infty$, so the finiteness
above is a convenience rather than a necessity, but dropping it changes
the shape of the conclusion. In infinite dimensions the environment may
have to be infinite-dimensional, and what the theorem delivers is an
*isometry* $V:\mathcal H_S\to\mathcal H_S\otimes\mathcal H_E$ with
$\mathcal E(\rho)=\text{Tr}_E[V\rho V^\dagger]$. Promoting $V$ to a
unitary requires completing an orthonormal basis of its cokernel, and the
two complements need not have equal dimension. The isometric form is the
one that always holds; the unitary form is the finite-dimensional gloss
on it.

</TheoremBox>

The Kraus proof and this proof are not independent facts stacked on top
of each other: the Kraus operators $K_i$ are *literally* the blocks of
$U$ that connect $|0\rangle_E$ to each $|i\rangle_E$; the derivation
above extracts them rather than merely asserting their existence.

### The explicit qubit construction: amplitude damping

Specialize to $d=2$, $r=2$: this platform's own amplitude-damping
channel, $K_0=\text{diag}(1,\sqrt{1-\gamma})$,
$K_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt\gamma\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$, one environment
qubit. The isometry $V$ above becomes $V|0\rangle_S=|0\rangle_S|0\rangle_E$,
$V|1\rangle_S=\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,|1\rangle_S|0\rangle_E+\sqrt\gamma\,|0\rangle_S|1\rangle_E$,
the two conditions the construction has to satisfy. Extending
$V$ to a full unitary needs an image for the two remaining basis states
$|0\rangle_S|1\rangle_E$ and $|1\rangle_S|1\rangle_E$; the simplest
completion is a rotation confined entirely to the two-dimensional
subspace $\{|0\rangle_S|1\rangle_E,\,|1\rangle_S|0\rangle_E\}$, leaving
$|0\rangle_S|0\rangle_E$ and $|1\rangle_S|1\rangle_E$ as fixed points:

$$
U = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & \sqrt{1-\gamma} & \sqrt\gamma & 0\\
0 & -\sqrt\gamma & \sqrt{1-\gamma} & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\qquad \text{(basis order $|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle$, system qubit first)}
$$

One checks directly that its four columns are orthonormal (the middle
$2\times2$ block is a rotation, $c^2+s^2=1$ with
$c=\sqrt{1-\gamma}$, $s=\sqrt\gamma$), so $U$ is unitary for every
$\gamma\in[0,1]$, and by construction $U|1\rangle_S|0\rangle_E=\sqrt{1-\gamma}|1\rangle_S|0\rangle_E+\sqrt\gamma|0\rangle_S|1\rangle_E$,
the amplitude-damping realization the theorem promised: a
qubit decaying to $|0\rangle$ while handing its excitation, coherently,
to an environment qubit that starts in $|0\rangle_E$ and picks up
$|1\rangle_E$ with amplitude $\sqrt\gamma$.

### Kraus non-uniqueness *is* the residual freedom on the environment

The Stinespring construction above made two choices beyond $\mathcal E$
itself: the joint unitary $U$, and the reference state $|0\rangle_E$.
Once those are fixed, exactly one freedom survives: which orthonormal
basis $\{|i\rangle_E\}$ of the environment is used to *read out* the
$K_i$. Rotating that basis by any unitary $W$ on $E$ alone,
$|i\rangle_E\mapsto\sum_jW_{ij}|j\rangle_E$, reads the *same* physical
joint state $U(\rho\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E)U^\dagger$ into a
different-looking Kraus set

$$
K_i' = \sum_jW_{ij}K_j
$$

and $\text{Tr}_E$ is basis-independent, so $\sum_iK_i'\rho K_i'^\dagger=\sum_iK_i\rho K_i^\dagger$
identically, so the channel cannot tell which basis was used to describe
its own environment. This is the *entire* content of Kraus
non-uniqueness: not a defect of the Kraus picture, but the same
"which orthonormal basis?" freedom that made purifications non-unique
in Schmidt Decomposition and Purification, now applied to the
environment side of a dilation instead of the purifying side of a mixed
state. The worked example below verifies this with an explicit $W$
(a Hadamard rotation on the environment basis), not just the general
argument.

### Dilation *is* purification, restated

Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi built the Choi matrix as
$J(\mathcal E)=d\,(\mathbb 1\otimes\mathcal E)(|\Omega^+\rangle\langle\Omega^+|)$:
send half of a maximally entangled pair through $\mathcal E$. Feed that
same half through *this* lesson's $U$ instead of abstractly through
$\mathcal E$, and keep the environment around rather than discarding it:

$$
|\Psi\rangle_{S'SE} \equiv (\mathbb 1_{S'}\otimes U)\big(|\Omega^+\rangle_{S'S}\otimes|0\rangle_E\big)
$$

is a pure state on three systems whose reduced state on $S'S$ (tracing
out $E$) is exactly $J(\mathcal E)/d$ by the theorem just proved. In
other words: **the Stinespring dilation's joint output state is a
purification of the channel's own Choi state**, with the physical
environment playing the role of the purifying system. "Every mixed
state has a purification" and "every channel has a unitary dilation"
are not two separate facts this course happens to cover back to back:
they are the same fact about extending to a larger system, once applied
to a state and once applied to a channel's Choi state.

## Purity Is Not Conserved, and That Is the Point

<InsightBlock>
  Purity is not conserved, and that is the whole point. What *is*
  conserved is the purity of the joint system, and the system's loss is
  the environment's gain, matched exactly rather than approximately.
</InsightBlock>

"Decoherence is entanglement with an unmonitored environment" is usually
offered as a slogan. Stinespring makes it a theorem: any physically valid
noise process is unitary evolution on system-plus-environment, followed
by discarding $E$. Nothing leaves the universe. Things leave the
*system's description* the moment its correlated partner is inaccessible.

The dilation built above lets that be watched happening.
Sweeping $\gamma$ from $0$ to $1$ and computing three purities from the
same joint state:

<ParametricCurve
  frames={purityFrames}
  xAxisLabel="damping strength γ"
  yAxisLabel="purity Tr(ρ²)"
  ariaLabel="Three purities plotted against amplitude-damping strength gamma from 0 to 1. Three things in that plot are worth reading carefully.

The joint curve never moves: it sits at
{minJointPurity.toFixed(4)} for every $\gamma$, because $U$ is unitary
and a unitary cannot make a pure state mixed. Whatever "damage" amplitude
damping does, it is not done to the joint state.

The system curve dips to {minSystemPurity.toFixed(3)} in the middle and
comes *back* to $1$ at $\gamma=1$. Total decay is not maximal
decoherence. At $\gamma=1$ every input is sent to $|0\rangle$, a pure
state, so the system ends pure again while having lost all its
information. Purity is not a measure of how much information survived.

The system and environment curves coincide, to within
{maxMarginalMismatch.toExponential(1)} across the sweep. That is not a
coincidence of amplitude damping; it is the Schmidt decomposition. A pure
bipartite state's two marginals share their nonzero spectrum, so they
share every spectral function, purity included. The system is mixed
*precisely* to the extent that it is entangled with $E$, and the
strongest mixing sits where the entanglement is greatest, not where the
noise parameter is largest.

The amplitude-damping construction makes the bookkeeping literal: the
"missing" excitation after decay did not vanish; it is sitting in the
environment qubit's $|1\rangle_E$ amplitude, $\sqrt\gamma$, the same
number that used to be the qubit's own decay amplitude.

This is also where **quantum error correction's entire framework**
starts. In the amplitude-damping dilation, the environment's final state
carries a record of *which* branch occurred: $\text{Tr}\big[K_1\rho
K_1^\dagger\big]=\gamma\rho_{11}$ is exactly the probability the
environment is found in $|1\rangle_E$, having "learned" that a decay
happened. If that record depended on the *logical* information encoded
in $\rho$ (not just on which error occurred), the environment would have
learned something about the protected data itself, and by the same
dilation argument, that leaked information shows up back on the system
as decoherence of the encoded qubit. A working error-correcting code is
built so the environment can learn only a *syndrome* (which error, if
any) while remaining completely uncorrelated with the *encoded*
quantum state: the precise condition later error-correction lessons
build codes to satisfy.

Finally, this is the **discrete-time seed of the Lindblad master
equation** already covered in this track. Chop a continuous-time open
evolution into many short steps of duration $dt$; each step is its own
tiny Stinespring dilation against a *fresh*, never-before-touched patch
of environment (the Markovian assumption). Amplitude damping's own
$\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}\approx dt/T_1$ for small $dt$ (from
`decayProbabilityForTimestep`, used throughout this track) shows the
pattern directly: $K_1(dt)=O(\sqrt{dt})$, $K_0(dt)=I+O(dt)$, and
resumming infinitely many such small dilations is what produces
a first-order-in-$dt$ Lindblad generator in the continuum limit.

## Amplitude Damping, Dilated Explicitly

**Take $\gamma=0.3$ and $\rho=|+\rangle\langle+|=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0.5\\0.5&0.5\end{pmatrix}$
(the same state and channel strength Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi
already used). Build the explicit Stinespring unitary, verify it
reproduces the Kraus-sum output exactly, and verify Kraus
non-uniqueness with an explicit environment rotation.**

With $c=\sqrt{0.7}\approx0.836660$, $s=\sqrt{0.3}\approx0.547723$:

$$
U = \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\0&0.836660&0.547723&0\\0&-0.547723&0.836660&0\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

Reading off $U$'s blocks connecting environment-input-$0$ to
environment-output-$0$ and environment-output-$1$ (rows/columns
$(0,2)$ for output-$0$, $(1,3)$ for output-$1$, restricted to input
columns $0,2$) recovers

$$
K_0^{\text{recovered}} = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0.836660\end{pmatrix}, \qquad K_1^{\text{recovered}} = \begin{pmatrix}0&0.547723\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

**identical** to `amplitudeDampingChannel(0.3)`'s own $K_0,K_1$ to full
numerical precision, not approximately, since both come from the same
$\sqrt\gamma,\sqrt{1-\gamma}$ values by construction. Applying $U$ to
$\rho\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E$ and tracing out the environment
(`reducedDensityMatrixQubit0` of `evolveDensityMatrix(jointInput, U)`)
gives

$$
\text{Tr}_E\big[U(\rho\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E)U^\dagger\big] = \begin{pmatrix}0.65&0.418330\\0.418330&0.35\end{pmatrix} = K_0\rho K_0^\dagger + K_1\rho K_1^\dagger
$$

This matches exactly. It is the concrete instance of the general theorem
proved above, and the same numerical output Quantum Channels: Kraus and
Choi already trusted for this channel and state.

For non-uniqueness, mix the Kraus operators with the Hadamard-like
environment rotation $W=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$:
$K_0'=(K_0+K_1)/\sqrt2$, $K_1'=(K_0-K_1)/\sqrt2$. These are visibly
*different* matrices from $K_0,K_1$.

$\begin{pmatrix}0.65&0.418330\\0.418330&0.35\end{pmatrix}$, the same
physical channel, read out through a rotated environment basis.

## Forgetting the Environment Must Start Pure

<Callout type="warning">
**Assuming the environment can start mixed or correlated with the
system.** The Stinespring theorem specifically requires a *pure*,
uncorrelated reference state $|0\rangle_E$; that purity is what makes
the resulting evolution unitary on the joint space in the first
place. If the true physical environment starts mixed, it must first be
purified against a *further*, larger system (Schmidt Decomposition and
Purification's own construction) before this theorem's $U$ applies to
it directly.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Treating a different Kraus decomposition as a different physical
process.** $\{K_i\}$ and $\{K_i'=\sum_jW_{ij}K_j\}$ for unitary $W$
describe the same joint unitary $U$, the same physical interaction, and
the same environment. Only the orthonormal
basis used to *label* the environment's outcomes changed, which is pure
bookkeeping. Nothing about the system's reduced dynamics can depend on
that labeling, which is why $\text{Tr}_E$ erases it.
</Callout>

## Practice Questions

1. Verify directly from $\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i=I$ that
   $V|\psi\rangle=\sum_iK_i|\psi\rangle\otimes|i\rangle_E$ satisfies
   $V^\dagger V=I$, and explain in one sentence why that makes $V$
   extendable to a unitary on the joint space.

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Compute the inner product of two images and watch the environment
   labels do the work:

   $$
   \langle\phi|V^\dagger V|\psi\rangle
   =\sum_{i,j}\langle\phi|K_i^\dagger K_j|\psi\rangle\,\langle i|j\rangle_E
   =\sum_i\langle\phi|K_i^\dagger K_i|\psi\rangle
   =\langle\phi|\Big(\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i\Big)|\psi\rangle
   =\langle\phi|\psi\rangle,
   $$

   using $\langle i|j\rangle_E=\delta_{ij}$ to collapse the double sum and
   trace preservation in the final step. Holding for every $|\phi\rangle$
   and $|\psi\rangle$, this is $V^\dagger V=I$.

   Notice which hypothesis each step consumed. The orthonormality of the
   environment basis kills the $i\neq j$ cross terms, which is why the
   Kraus index has to become a *label on a fresh system* rather than
   anything internal to $S$. And $\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i=I$, exactly trace
   preservation, is what makes the answer $I$ rather than some other
   positive operator. A trace-*decreasing* CP map, one branch of a
   measurement, would give $V^\dagger V\leq I$ and would not be an
   isometry.

   The extendability, in one sentence: an isometry preserves inner
   products, so it carries an orthonormal basis of the $d$-dimensional
   $\mathcal H_S$ to $d$ orthonormal vectors inside the $dr$-dimensional
   joint space, and $d$ orthonormal vectors in a $dr$-dimensional space can
   always be completed to a full orthonormal basis by Gram-Schmidt on the
   orthogonal complement.

   Spelled out slightly, since the counting is the whole content: $U$ is
   already determined on the $d$-dimensional subspace
   $\mathcal H_S\otimes|0\rangle_E$, and what remains is to define it on
   the orthogonal complement of that subspace, of dimension $d(r-1)$, by
   mapping it isometrically onto the orthogonal complement of $V$'s range,
   of dimension $dr-d=d(r-1)$. The two numbers are equal, so an
   isometric identification exists and the result is unitary.

   That equality is precisely what fails in infinite dimensions, which is
   why the theorem box states the general result as an *isometry* rather
   than a unitary. Two subspaces of an infinite-dimensional space can both
   be infinite-dimensional and still fail to be unitarily identifiable in
   the way this counting assumes; the finite-dimensional case is the one
   where dimension alone settles it. Note also that $V^\dagger V=I$ does
   not give $VV^\dagger=I$: the latter is $\mathbb 1$ on the joint space
   and is false, since $VV^\dagger$ is the projector onto $V$'s
   $d$-dimensional range. Isometry, not unitarity, is what $V$ is.

   </details>

2. The dephasing channel has
   $K_0=\sqrt{1-\lambda/2}\,I$, $K_1=\sqrt{\lambda/2}\,Z$. Following the
   same pattern as this lesson's amplitude-damping construction, write
   down its $4\times4$ Stinespring unitary $U_{\text{dephasing}}(\lambda)$
   explicitly, and check it reduces to the identity at $\lambda=0$.

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Write $c=\sqrt{1-\lambda/2}$ and $s=\sqrt{\lambda/2}$, so $c^2+s^2=1$.
   The isometry is fixed by its action on the two system basis states:

   $$
   V|0\rangle_S=c\,|0\rangle_S|0\rangle_E+s\,Z|0\rangle_S|1\rangle_E=c|00\rangle+s|01\rangle,
   $$

   $$
   V|1\rangle_S=c\,|1\rangle_S|0\rangle_E+s\,Z|1\rangle_S|1\rangle_E=c|10\rangle-s|11\rangle,
   $$

   the minus sign being the only trace $Z$ leaves. Those two vectors are
   the images of $|00\rangle$ and $|10\rangle$, so they are the first and
   third columns of $U$. Completing with anything orthonormal in the two
   leftover directions, the tidiest choice gives

   $$
   U_{\text{dephasing}}(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}
   c & -s & 0 & 0\\
   s & \phantom{-}c & 0 & 0\\
   0 & \phantom{-}0 & c & s\\
   0 & \phantom{-}0 & -s & c
   \end{pmatrix}
   \qquad\text{(basis order }|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle\text{, system first).}
   $$

   It is unitary for every $\lambda\in[0,1]$, being block diagonal with two
   $2\times2$ rotation blocks, and at $\lambda=0$ we have $c=1$, $s=0$, so
   both blocks are the identity and $U=I_4$: no coupling to the
   environment, no dephasing, exactly as it should be.

   The structure is more informative than the matrix. Writing
   $c=\cos\varphi$, $s=\sin\varphi$, the two blocks are rotations by
   $+\varphi$ and $-\varphi$, so

   $$
   U=|0\rangle\langle0|_S\otimes R(\varphi)+|1\rangle\langle1|_S\otimes R(-\varphi):
   $$

   a controlled rotation that turns the environment one way if the system
   is $|0\rangle$ and the other way if it is $|1\rangle$. The environment
   is being asked which state the system is in, and how sharply it learns
   the answer is set by how distinguishable the two environment outcomes
   are. Those outcomes are $R(\pm\varphi)|0\rangle_E=(c,\pm s)$, with
   overlap

   $$
   \langle e_0|e_1\rangle=c^2-s^2=\left(1-\tfrac\lambda2\right)-\tfrac\lambda2=1-\lambda,
   $$

   which is exactly the factor by which dephasing shrinks the system's
   coherence. The which-path slogan is a theorem here: the surviving
   coherence *equals* the overlap of the environment states, so the
   information the environment gains is the coherence the system loses,
   with no slack between them.

   Contrast with the amplitude-damping dilation in the lesson body, where
   the joint unitary moves an *excitation* into the environment and the
   fixed points are $|00\rangle$ and $|11\rangle$. Dephasing moves no
   population at all: every diagonal element of $U$'s action leaves the
   system's populations alone, and the entire effect is on the relative
   phase. Two channels, two very different unitaries, the same theorem.

   </details>

3. Using the fact that a single qubit's Choi matrix is $4\times4$ (so
   has minimal Kraus rank at most $4$), explain why *no* single-qubit
   channel ever needs an environment larger than two qubits in its
   Stinespring dilation.

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Because the environment dimension in the construction is the number of
   Kraus operators, and that number is capped by the rank of a $4\times4$
   matrix.

   Chain the three facts. Choi's theorem builds one Kraus operator per
   nonzero eigenvalue of $J(\mathcal E)$, so the minimal Kraus rank is
   $r=\mathrm{rank}\,J(\mathcal E)$. For a single qubit $J$ is
   $d^2\times d^2=4\times4$, so $r\leq4$. The Stinespring isometry
   $V|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}K_i|\psi\rangle\otimes|i\rangle_E$ needs
   the environment only to hold the index $i$, so
   $\dim\mathcal H_E=r\leq4=2^2$: two qubits, and never more.

   The bound is tight, which is worth checking so it does not read as
   pessimism. The fully depolarizing channel
   $\rho\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)\,I/2$ has Kraus set
   $\{I,X,Y,Z\}/2$, rank exactly $4$, and genuinely needs two environment
   qubits. At the other end, both channels in this course, amplitude
   damping and dephasing, have rank $2$ and need only one, which is why
   their dilations came out $4\times4$ rather than $8\times8$.

   The general statement scales the same way: an $n$-qubit channel has a
   $4^n\times4^n$ Choi matrix, so $r\leq4^n$ and the environment never
   needs more than $2n$ qubits. Doubling the register is always enough.

   Two clarifications keep this from being over-read. The bound is on the
   *minimal* dilation; nothing stops a non-minimal one from using a huge
   environment, and the theorem only promises that a small one exists.
   And it is a statement about mathematics, not about physics. A real
   qubit is coupled to an enormous physical environment of phonons,
   two-level defects and stray photons. What the bound says is that
   whatever that environment does to the qubit, its effect is exactly
   reproducible by two qubits, because only a two-qubit worth of the
   environment ever becomes correlated with the system. Everything else in
   the bath is a spectator that the partial trace was going to discard
   anyway.

   </details>

## Summary

- Every Kraus set $\{K_i\}$ defines an isometry
  $V|\psi\rangle=\sum_iK_i|\psi\rangle\otimes|i\rangle_E$, proved via
  $V^\dagger V=\sum_iK_i^\dagger K_i=I$; extending $V$ to a unitary $U$
  and computing $\text{Tr}_E[U(\rho\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|_E)U^\dagger]$
  reproduces $\sum_iK_i\rho K_i^\dagger$ exactly. That is the
  Stinespring dilation theorem.
- Amplitude damping's own $K_0,K_1$ come from a concrete $4\times4$
  qubit-environment unitary (a rotation on the $\{|01\rangle,|10\rangle\}$
  subspace), verified numerically to reproduce the channel's output to
  full precision.
- Kraus non-uniqueness is exactly the residual freedom to rotate the
  environment's readout basis after $U$ and $|0\rangle_E$ are fixed,
  demonstrated explicitly with a Hadamard rotation reproducing the
  identical channel output.
- A channel's Stinespring dilation is a purification of its Choi state:
  "every mixed state has a purification" and "every channel has a
  unitary dilation" are the same fact about extending to a larger
  system.
- This is the rigorous content behind "decoherence is entanglement with
  an environment," the starting point for how quantum error correction
  frames what an error-correcting code must prevent an environment from
  learning, and the discrete-time seed of the Lindblad master equation.
