};

export const a = annihilationOperator(dim);
export const aDag = creationOperator(dim);
export const xOp = positionOperator(dim, 1, 1);

export const expectation = (op, amps) => {
  const opAmps = op.apply(amps);
  return amps.reduce((total, amp, i) => total.add(amp.conjugate().mul(opAmps[i])), Complex.ZERO).re;
};

## Motivation

The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator built energy eigenstates $|n\rangle$
algebraically, states of definite energy, but *maximally* non-classical
in another sense: $\langle x\rangle=\langle p\rangle=0$ for every
$|n\rangle$ with $n>0$, nothing at all like a classical oscillator's
position and momentum sweeping back and forth in time. **Coherent
states** are the opposite extreme: not energy eigenstates, but the states
that most closely track a classical trajectory, and (not a coincidence)
exactly the states real laser light approximates. This lesson builds them
directly from $a,a^\dagger$, no new postulate needed.

## Mathematical Development

### Coherent states as eigenstates of â

Define $|\alpha\rangle$ (for any complex $\alpha$) by
$a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle$. Since $a$ is *not* Hermitian, this
eigenvalue equation is perfectly consistent with a complex eigenvalue,
unlike anything from Hermitian Operators. Expanding
$|\alpha\rangle=\sum_nc_n|n\rangle$ in the Fock basis and using
$a|n\rangle=\sqrt n|n-1\rangle$:

$$
a|\alpha\rangle=\sum_n c_n\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle = \alpha\sum_n c_n|n\rangle
$$

Matching coefficients of $|n\rangle$ (shift the left sum's index):
$c_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1}=\alpha c_n$, i.e. $c_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{n+1}}c_n$.
Iterating from $c_0$: $c_n=\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}c_0$. Normalizing
($\sum_n|c_n|^2=|c_0|^2e^{|\alpha|^2}=1$) fixes $c_0=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}$:

$$
\boxed{\;|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,|n\rangle\;}
$$

The number-state probabilities $|c_n|^2=e^{-|\alpha|^2}|\alpha|^{2n}/n!$
are exactly **Poisson-distributed** with mean $\langle N\rangle=|\alpha|^2$
(a checkable statement: this is the Poisson distribution's own
closed form). Built directly from this platform's own truncated
`annihilationOperator`/`creationOperator` matrices (the same finite Fock
basis The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator used):

### Coherent states saturate the uncertainty bound

Write $x=\sqrt{\tfrac1{2}}(a+a^\dagger)$, $p=i\sqrt{\tfrac12}(a^\dagger-a)$
(natural units $m=\omega=\hbar=1$, this platform's own `positionOperator`
convention). For real $\alpha$, using $a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle$,
$\langle\alpha|a^\dagger=\alpha\langle\alpha|$, and $aa^\dagger=a^\dagger
a+1$:

$$
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt2\,\alpha, \qquad
\langle x^2\rangle = \tfrac12\left(2\alpha^2+2\alpha^2+1\right)=2\alpha^2+\tfrac12
\;\Longrightarrow\; \Delta x^2 = \tfrac12
$$

$$
\langle p\rangle = 0, \qquad \langle p^2\rangle = \tfrac12 \;\Longrightarrow\; \Delta p^2=\tfrac12
$$

$$
\Delta x\,\Delta p = \tfrac12
$$

**Exactly** the Heisenberg minimum from Expectation Values and
Uncertainty, and $\Delta x=\Delta p=1/\sqrt2$: identical to the ground
state $|0\rangle$'s own spread. This is the coherent state's defining
feature: it's a *displaced* vacuum, same shape, shifted mean position and
momentum, which is exactly why it tracks a classical trajectory (the
"blob" doesn't spread or distort as $\langle x\rangle,\langle p\rangle$
oscillate). Checked directly against the real engine's coherent-state
amplitudes at $\alpha=1.5$: $\Delta x=\Delta p\approx0.7071$ (machine
precision, in the 30-level truncated basis, far above the state's own
$\langle N\rangle=2.25$).

### Squeezed states: unequal spread, still minimal

Coherent states are not the *only* minimum-uncertainty states, just the
ones with $\Delta x=\Delta p$. Define a new operator via a **Bogoliubov
transformation**, $b=a\cosh r+a^\dagger\sinh r$ (real squeeze parameter
$r$), and its eigenstates $b|\beta\rangle=\beta|\beta\rangle$: the
**squeezed states**. $b$ satisfies the *same* commutation relation as
$a$: $[b,b^\dagger]=\cosh^2r\,[a,a^\dagger]+\sinh^2r\,[a^\dagger,a]=(\cosh^2r-\sinh^2r)[a,a^\dagger]=1$,
so everything the earlier coherent-state derivation used (eigenstates
existing, normalizability, the Fock-like expansion argument) carries over
to $b,|\beta\rangle$ unchanged, just relabeled.

Inverting the Bogoliubov transform, $a=b\cosh r-b^\dagger\sinh r$
(direct algebra: $b\cosh r-b^\dagger\sinh r=(a\cosh r+a^\dagger\sinh
r)\cosh r-(a^\dagger\cosh r+a\sinh r)\sinh r=a(\cosh^2r-\sinh^2r)=a$),
checked here as an exact matrix identity (holds at *any* truncation,
since it's pure linear-operator algebra, not an eigenvalue statement):

Substituting into $x=\sqrt{\tfrac12}(a+a^\dagger)$:
$a+a^\dagger=(\cosh r-\sinh r)(b+b^\dagger)=e^{-r}(b+b^\dagger)$ (using
$\cosh r-\sinh r=e^{-r}$), so $x=e^{-r}\times\sqrt{\tfrac12}(b+b^\dagger)$.
Since $b,b^\dagger$ obey the identical algebra $a,a^\dagger$ did, the
*same* calculation that gave $\Delta\left[\sqrt{\tfrac12}(a+a^\dagger)\right]=1/\sqrt2$
for $|\alpha\rangle$ gives $\Delta\left[\sqrt{\tfrac12}(b+b^\dagger)\right]=1/\sqrt2$
for $|\beta\rangle$, so:

$$
\Delta x = \frac{e^{-r}}{\sqrt2}
$$

Similarly $a^\dagger-a=(\cosh r+\sinh r)(b^\dagger-b)=e^{r}(b^\dagger-b)$,
giving:

$$
\Delta p = \frac{e^{r}}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \Delta x\,\Delta p = \frac{e^{-r}}{\sqrt2}\cdot\frac{e^{r}}{\sqrt2}=\frac12
$$

**Still exactly minimal**, for *any* $r$: squeezing trades $\Delta x$
against $\Delta p$ in inverse lockstep, never violating (or loosening)
the Heisenberg bound.

## Physical Interpretation

"Squeezing" is exactly what the name suggests: at $r=0.5$, position spread
shrinks to about $61\%$ of the coherent-state value while momentum spread
grows to about $165\%$, the ellipse of uncertainty (a visual
picture, even though this lesson stays in the algebra) stretched along
one quadrature and compressed along the other, its *area* fixed by
Heisenberg. This isn't a mathematical curiosity: squeezed light is a real
laboratory tool (LIGO's gravitational-wave detector uses squeezed light
specifically to push measurement noise below what an ordinary coherent
state, let alone classical light, could achieve on the "compressed"
quadrature, at the unavoidable cost of extra noise on the other one).

## Worked Example

**Compute $\Delta x$ and $\Delta p$ for $r=0.5$ directly from the boxed
formulas, and confirm the product is still exactly $\tfrac12$.**

$e^{-0.5}\approx0.6065$, so $\Delta x=0.6065/\sqrt2\approx0.4289$.
$e^{0.5}\approx1.6487$, so $\Delta p=1.6487/\sqrt2\approx1.1658$.
Product: $0.4289\times1.1658\approx0.5000$, matching $\tfrac12$ to four
decimal places, a direct numeric confirmation (not just the general
algebraic argument) that squeezing preserves the minimum-
uncertainty product while redistributing it unevenly.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Assuming any state with small $\Delta x$ is "squeezed."** Squeezed
states are specifically the minimum-uncertainty states with *unequal*
$\Delta x,\Delta p$ that still saturate $\Delta x\Delta p=\tfrac12$
exactly; a state with small $\Delta x$ but $\Delta x\Delta p\gg\tfrac12$
(most states) is just localized, not squeezed in this technical sense.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Thinking $a$ being non-Hermitian makes $a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle$
suspicious.** Only Hermitian operators are guaranteed *real* eigenvalues
and an orthonormal eigenbasis (Hermitian Operators); $a$ is not Hermitian,
so a complex eigenvalue $\alpha$ and a non-orthogonal eigenbasis
(distinct $|\alpha\rangle$'s have nonzero overlap) are both expected, not
contradictions.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Coherent states $|\alpha\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n(\alpha^n/\sqrt{n!})|n\rangle$
  are eigenstates of $a$ (not Hermitian, so a complex eigenvalue is
  expected), with Poisson-distributed photon number, verified directly
  against this platform's own truncated ladder operators.
- They saturate $\Delta x\Delta p=\tfrac12$ with **equal**
  $\Delta x=\Delta p=1/\sqrt2$: a displaced vacuum, the closest quantum
  analogue to a classical oscillator trajectory.
- Squeezed states, eigenstates of the Bogoliubov-transformed
  $b=a\cosh r+a^\dagger\sinh r$, generalize this to **unequal**
  $\Delta x=e^{-r}/\sqrt2$, $\Delta p=e^r/\sqrt2$, still exactly saturating
  the same bound for any $r$.
