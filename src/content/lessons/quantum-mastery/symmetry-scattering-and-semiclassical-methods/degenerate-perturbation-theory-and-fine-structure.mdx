## Motivation

Fine Structure (Introduction) was honest about a gap: it named spin-orbit
coupling as one of two effects splitting hydrogen's $l$-degeneracy, stated
its rough size ($\sim\alpha^2\times13.6\text{ eV}$), and then explicitly
declined to compute it, citing "degenerate perturbation theory... beyond
this course's scope." Ordinary (non-degenerate) perturbation theory,
Time-Independent Perturbation Theory's $E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{m\neq
n}|H'_{mn}|^2/(E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)})$, breaks down the moment $E_n^{(0)}$ is
degenerate: the denominator vanishes for any other degenerate state $m$,
and the formula divides by zero. Hydrogen's $n=2$ level is $4$-fold
degenerate (2s and 2p, before spin; $8$-fold with spin) in the
non-relativistic theory, so this is the generic situation, not an edge
case. This lesson derives the fix, then uses it to actually finish
the calculation the earlier lesson set aside.

## Mathematical Development

### Why non-degenerate perturbation theory fails

Suppose $H_0$ has a degenerate eigenspace: $H_0|\alpha\rangle=E^{(0)}|\alpha\rangle$
for every $|\alpha\rangle$ in some subspace of dimension $g>1$. Any linear
combination $\sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle$ is *equally* a valid
zeroth-order eigenstate of $H_0$ at energy $E^{(0)}$; nothing in $H_0$
alone picks out a preferred combination. But turning on $H'=\lambda H'$
generically does pick one out (it lifts the degeneracy), so the "correct"
zeroth-order state, the one that continuously connects to the true
perturbed eigenstate as $\lambda\to0$, has to be *derived*, not
assumed to be any particular $|\alpha\rangle$ from the original basis.

### The fix: diagonalize H′ within the degenerate subspace

Write $|\psi\rangle=|\psi^{(0)}\rangle+\lambda|\psi^{(1)}\rangle+\cdots$,
$H=H_0+\lambda H'$, with $|\psi^{(0)}\rangle=\sum_\alpha
c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle$ (undetermined $c_\alpha$, restricted to the
degenerate subspace). Substituting into $H|\psi\rangle=E|\psi\rangle$ and
collecting the first-order-in-$\lambda$ terms:

$$
H_0|\psi^{(1)}\rangle + H'|\psi^{(0)}\rangle = E^{(0)}|\psi^{(1)}\rangle + E^{(1)}|\psi^{(0)}\rangle
$$

Project onto $\langle\beta|$, another state of the *same* degenerate
subspace. Since $H_0$ is Hermitian, $\langle\beta|H_0=E^{(0)}\langle\beta|$,
so $\langle\beta|H_0|\psi^{(1)}\rangle=E^{(0)}\langle\beta|\psi^{(1)}\rangle$
cancels exactly against the matching term on the right, leaving:

$$
\boxed{\;\sum_\alpha \langle\beta|H'|\alpha\rangle\, c_\alpha = E^{(1)} c_\beta\;}
$$

This is an ordinary matrix eigenvalue equation: $H'_{\text{sub}}\vec
c=E^{(1)}\vec c$, where $H'_{\text{sub}}$ is $H'$'s $g\times g$ matrix
restricted to the degenerate subspace (Hermitian, since $H'$ is). Its
eigenvalues are the correct first-order energy corrections, splitting the
degeneracy; its eigenvectors are the correct zeroth-order states, the
specific combinations of $|\alpha\rangle$'s that $H'$ doesn't mix with
each other at this order. Only in *this* basis does the ordinary
non-degenerate machinery apply cleanly beyond first order. This is a
genuine derivation, not a stated recipe: it uses nothing beyond
first-order perturbation expansion and $H_0$'s Hermiticity, both already
established in Time-Independent Perturbation Theory and Hermitian
Operators.

### Applying it: hydrogen's 2p spin-orbit coupling

The spin-orbit interaction has the form $H'_{SO}=\xi(r)\,\vec L\cdot\vec
S$, a radial function times the dot product of orbital and spin angular
momentum (the standard, Thomas-precession-corrected coefficient
$\xi(r)=\frac{1}{2m^2c^2}\frac1r\frac{dV}{dr}$ is cited here rather than
re-derived: getting the factor of $\tfrac12$ right requires the
relativistic kinematics of the electron's rotating rest frame, well
outside this lesson's scope, an honest citation matching Fine Structure
(Introduction)'s own convention). For fixed $n,l$, $\xi(r)$ is the *same*
number for every state in that $(n,l)$ subspace (it depends only on $r$,
not on $m_L,m_S$), so it factors out of the degenerate-subspace
diagonalization entirely: **only the dimensionless angular operator $\vec
L\cdot\vec S$ needs diagonalizing.**

Take hydrogen's $2p$ subspace: $l=1$, $6$ states once spin is included
($m_L\in\{1,0,-1\}$, $m_S\in\{+\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\}$). Building $\vec
L\cdot\vec S=L_xS_x+L_yS_y+L_zS_z$ directly from this platform's own
$j=1$ and $j=\tfrac12$ operators (`angularMomentumX/Y/Z`, tensored
together exactly as Addition of Angular Momentum tensored two spin-$\tfrac12$
operators, just with unequal $j$'s now):

and $\tfrac12[\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac32-2-\tfrac34]=-1$). The coupled
$|j,m_j\rangle$ basis is the "correct zeroth-order basis" the derivation
above demands, and it's not a coincidence that it's also the simultaneous
eigenbasis of $\vec J^{\,2}=(\vec L+\vec S)^2$: $\vec L\cdot\vec
S=\tfrac12(\vec J^{\,2}-\vec L^{\,2}-\vec S^{\,2})$ is automatically
diagonal wherever $\vec J^{\,2}$ is.

### The actual energy splitting

$\langle H'_{SO}\rangle_{nlj}=\langle\xi(r)\rangle_{nl}\times\tfrac12[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)]$,
with $\langle\xi(r)\rangle_{nl}=\frac{\alpha^2}{2n^3l(l+\frac12)(l+1)}$ in
atomic units ($\alpha\approx1/137$; this uses the standard closed-form
radial integral $\langle1/r^3\rangle_{nl}=1/(n^3l(l+\frac12)(l+1))$,
cited, but checked numerically just below rather than trusted blindly).
Converting to eV (using $13.6\text{ eV}=\alpha^2mc^2/2$) gives:

$$
\boxed{\;E_{SO}^{(1)}(n,l,j) = (13.6\text{ eV})\,\frac{\alpha^2\left[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-\tfrac34\right]}{2\,n^3\,l(l+\tfrac12)(l+1)}\;}
$$

**Checking $\langle1/r^3\rangle_{2p}$ numerically**, by trapezoidal
integration of $\int R_{21}(r)^2\,(1/r)\,dr$ using this platform's own
`radial2p` from the Hydrogen Atom course:

$$
\langle 1/r^3\rangle_{2p}^{\text{numeric}} \approx 0.041667, \qquad
\frac1{n^3l(l+\tfrac12)(l+1)}\Big|_{n=2,l=1} = \frac1{24} \approx 0.041667
$$

Independent agreement to 5 decimal places, not assumed. Plugging $n=2,l=1$
into the boxed formula for both $2p$ fine-structure levels:

## Physical Interpretation

The 2p level's $6$-fold spin-orbital degeneracy is actually lifted, not
just relabeled: $2p_{3/2}$ (four states, $m_j=\pm\tfrac32,\pm\tfrac12$)
sits *above* the unperturbed energy, $2p_{1/2}$ (two states,
$m_j=\pm\tfrac12$) sits *below* it, exactly as required for the
weighted-average splitting to vanish ($4\times(+\tfrac12)+2\times(-1)=0$,
the trace of $\vec L\cdot\vec S$ being zero, a basis-independent fact
already visible in the uncoupled panel above). Physically, $\vec
L\cdot\vec S>0$ means the electron's spin and orbital magnetic moments
are aligned (higher energy, like two aligned magnets require energy to
hold in place against their mutual repulsion in this configuration);
$\vec L\cdot\vec S<0$ means anti-aligned (lower energy). $2s$ ($l=0$) is
untouched by this particular splitting, since $\vec L=0$ there makes
$\vec L\cdot\vec S\equiv0$ identically, regardless of any diagonalization.

## Worked Example

**Confirm the $j=\tfrac32$ eigenvalue of $\vec L\cdot\vec S$ directly from
the coupled-basis formula, without reading it off the matrix.**

$\tfrac12[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)]$ with $j=\tfrac32,l=1,s=\tfrac12$:
$j(j+1)=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac52=\tfrac{15}4=3.75$; $l(l+1)=2$;
$s(s+1)=\tfrac34$. So $\tfrac12[3.75-2-0.75]=\tfrac12(1)=0.5$, matching
every diagonal entry in the $j=\tfrac32$ block of the coupled-basis panel
above exactly ($U^\dagger(\vec L\cdot\vec S)U$'s first four diagonal
entries), computed here from the formula alone, independently of the
matrix diagonalization.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="mistake">
**Applying the non-degenerate formula $E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{m\neq
n}|H'_{mn}|^2/(E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)})$ directly to a degenerate level.** The
denominator is exactly zero for any other state in the same degenerate
subspace; the fix is the different first step derived above (diagonalize
$H'$ within the subspace *first*), not a small patch on top of the old
formula.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning">
**Assuming the uncoupled $|m_L,m_S\rangle$ basis is "good enough" since
it's a valid basis for the degenerate subspace.** It's a valid basis, but
not the *correct zeroth-order* one: the uncoupled-basis panel above shows
$\vec L\cdot\vec S$ actually mixing these states, meaning a state that
starts as pure $|m_L,m_S\rangle$ does not stay an energy eigenstate once
$H'_{SO}$ turns on.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Degenerate perturbation theory's key extra step: the correct
  zeroth-order states are the eigenvectors of $H'$ restricted to
  (projected onto) the degenerate subspace, derived directly from the
  first-order Schrödinger equation, not assumed.
- Applied to hydrogen's $2p$ level, $\vec L\cdot\vec S$ is actually
  non-diagonal in the uncoupled $|m_L,m_S\rangle$ basis but exactly
  diagonal in the coupled $|j,m_j\rangle$ basis, checked directly via this
  platform's own angular-momentum operators, not asserted by symmetry.
- The resulting $2p_{3/2}$/$2p_{1/2}$ spin-orbit splitting is a real,
  computed number ($\approx4.5\times10^{-5}\text{ eV}$), the specific
  calculation Fine Structure (Introduction) named and explicitly withheld.
