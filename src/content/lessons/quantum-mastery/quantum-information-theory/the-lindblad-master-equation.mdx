## Motivation

Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators built the discrete Kraus picture
of decoherence and explicitly declined to build the continuous-time
differential equation underneath it, noting that "no lesson in this
course needs it." This course does. The **Lindblad master equation**
(more precisely, the **GKLS equation**, after Gorini, Kossakowski,
Sudarshan, and Lindblad) is the continuous-time generator of exactly the
kind of Markovian, completely-positive evolution this platform's Kraus
channels already implement at discrete steps, and this lesson proves
the two pictures aren't just analogous, they're the *same* dynamics,
sampled at different resolutions.

Primary sources: V. Gorini, A.
Kossakowski, and E. C. G. Sudarshan, "Completely Positive Dynamical
Semigroups of N-Level Systems," *J. Math. Phys.* **17**, 821 (1976),
and G. Lindblad, "On the Generators of Quantum Dynamical Semigroups,"
*Commun. Math. Phys.* **48**, 119–130 (1976), who derived the boxed
generator's general form independently and in the same year.

## Mathematical Development

### What a Markovian CP generator must look like

A **quantum dynamical semigroup** is a family $\{\Lambda_t\}_{t\geq0}$ of
CPTP maps with $\Lambda_0=\mathbb1$, $\Lambda_{t+s}=\Lambda_t\Lambda_s$
(Markovian: no memory of the past beyond the current state). Writing
$\Lambda_t=\mathbb1+\mathcal Lt+O(t^2)$ for small $t$ defines the
**generator** $\mathcal L$, so that $\dot\rho=\mathcal L(\rho)$. The
**GKLS theorem** (cited here as a deep structural result, not re-derived
in full generality; its complete proof requires a careful limiting
argument on the Choi matrix of $\Lambda_t$ as $t\to0$, beyond this
lesson's scope) states that *any* generator of a completely positive,
trace-preserving Markovian semigroup necessarily has the form

<TheoremBox title="GKLS (Lindblad) equation" provenance="cited">

$$
\boxed{\dot\rho = \mathcal L(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_k\left(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger - \frac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}\right)}
$$

for some Hermitian $H$ (an effective Hamiltonian) and some set of
**Lindblad (jump) operators** $\{L_k\}$.

</TheoremBox>

What *is* proved here directly
is the converse, sufficiency direction: that this form genuinely
generates valid CPTP dynamics, by connecting it explicitly to the
discrete Kraus picture already built and trusted on this platform.

### Sufficiency: recovering a Kraus form at first order in $dt$

Exponentiate $\mathcal L$ over one short step $dt$:
$\rho(t+dt)\approx\rho(t)+\mathcal L(\rho)\,dt$. Group terms into

$$
\rho(t+dt) \approx K_0\rho K_0^\dagger + \sum_{k\geq1}K_k\rho K_k^\dagger, \qquad K_0 = I + \Big(-iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\Big)dt, \qquad K_k = \sqrt{dt}\,L_k\ (k\geq1)
$$

Check trace-preservation to $O(dt)$: $K_0^\dagger K_0\approx I+\big(iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\big)dt+\big(-iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\big)dt=I-dt\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k$
(the $\pm iH$ terms cancel, since $H$ is Hermitian), and
$\sum_{k\geq1}K_k^\dagger K_k=dt\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k$, so
$K_0^\dagger K_0+\sum_{k\geq1}K_k^\dagger K_k=I+O(dt^2)$: the
trace-preservation condition Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators
required, satisfied to first order. This is already a genuine Kraus sum
(hence CP, by the easy direction of Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi's
theorem), confirming the boxed generator produces valid physical
dynamics at every infinitesimal step.

### Solving it exactly for amplitude damping

<PredictBeforeReveal
  question="Under pure amplitude damping (population decaying from |1> to |0> at rate Gamma), how fast do you expect the off-diagonal coherence rho01 to decay, relative to the population?"
  options={[

    { label: "Half as fast, rate Gamma/2", value: "half" },
  ]}
  correctValue="half"
  explanation="The coherence decays at exactly half the population's rate, derived below directly from the Lindblad equation's own algebra, not assumed. That factor of two is exactly why pure amplitude damping alone gives T2 = 2*T1 rather than T2 = T1 — it's the best-case coherence time a purely population-decaying qubit can have, before any additional pure dephasing shortens it further."
/>

Take $H=0$ and a single jump operator $L=\sqrt\Gamma\,\sigma_-$, where
$\sigma_-=|0\rangle\langle1|$ (the lowering operator: $\sigma_-|1\rangle=|0\rangle$)
and $\Gamma\equiv1/T_1$. For $\rho=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}&\rho_{01}\\\rho_{10}&\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}$,
direct computation gives $\sigma_-\rho\sigma_-^\dagger=\rho_{11}|0\rangle\langle0|$
and $\sigma_-^\dagger\sigma_-=|1\rangle\langle1|$, so

$$
\dot\rho = \Gamma\left(\sigma_-\rho\sigma_-^\dagger-\tfrac12\{\sigma_-^\dagger\sigma_-,\rho\}\right) = \Gamma\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{11} & -\rho_{01}/2\\ -\rho_{10}/2 & -\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}
$$

giving two decoupled linear ODEs: $\dot\rho_{11}=-\Gamma\rho_{11}$ and
$\dot\rho_{01}=-\tfrac\Gamma2\rho_{01}$, with **exact** closed-form
solutions

$$
\rho_{11}(t) = \rho_{11}(0)\,e^{-\Gamma t} = \rho_{11}(0)\,e^{-t/T_1}, \qquad \rho_{01}(t) = \rho_{01}(0)\,e^{-\Gamma t/2} = \rho_{01}(0)\,e^{-t/(2T_1)}
$$

The population decays exactly as T1 & T2 Decoherence defined it. The
*coherence* decays at exactly **half** that rate in the exponent; this
single fact, derived here for the first time on this platform rather
than asserted, is about to reproduce the $T_2\leq2T_1$ bound exactly.

### The discrete channel is this solution, evaluated at $t=dt$: exactly, not approximately

Apply `amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma)` (Kraus operators
$K_0=\text{diag}(1,\sqrt{1-\gamma})$, $K_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt\gamma\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$)
to a general $\rho$ directly:
$\rho'_{11}=\rho_{11}(1-\gamma)$, $\rho'_{01}=\rho_{01}\sqrt{1-\gamma}$
(both derived the same way Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators
derived trace-preservation for this channel). Substitute
`decayProbabilityForTimestep`'s exact identity $\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}$,
so $1-\gamma=e^{-dt/T_1}$:

$$
\rho'_{11} = \rho_{11}\,e^{-dt/T_1}, \qquad \rho'_{01} = \rho_{01}\sqrt{e^{-dt/T_1}} = \rho_{01}\,e^{-dt/(2T_1)}
$$

**These are exactly the continuous solutions above, evaluated at
$t=dt$**, both the population *and* the coherence, not just the
population T1 & T2 Decoherence already checked. One discrete Kraus step
with $\gamma=\gamma(dt)$ isn't an approximation to the Lindblad flow;
it's a single exact sample of it, true for whatever step size $dt$ is
chosen, matching this platform's engine to full numerical precision:

### Deriving $T_2\leq2T_1$ from the generator's linearity

Add a *second*, independent jump operator for pure dephasing,
$L'=\sqrt{\kappa}\,\sigma_z$ (no population exchange: check
$\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z-\rho$ has zero diagonal, verified the same way
Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi checked structure directly). Its
contribution to $\dot\rho_{01}$ works out to $-2\kappa\rho_{01}$, an
independent exponential channel with its own rate $1/T_{2,\text{pure}}=2\kappa$.
Because $\mathcal L$ is **linear** in its jump operators, running both
processes together simply *adds* their individual contributions to
$\dot\rho_{01}$:

<TheoremBox title="The T2 ≤ 2T1 bound" provenance="derived">

$$
\dot\rho_{01} = -\Big(\tfrac{\Gamma}{2}+2\kappa\Big)\rho_{01} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac1{T_2} = \frac1{2T_1} + \frac1{T_{2,\text{pure}}}
$$

Since $1/T_{2,\text{pure}}\geq0$ always, $1/T_2\geq1/(2T_1)$, i.e.
$\boxed{T_2\leq2T_1}$.

</TheoremBox>

This bound is **derived** from the Lindblad generator's
structure, not merely stated as an empirical fact the way T1 & T2
Decoherence introduced it. Equality holds exactly when
$T_{2,\text{pure}}\to\infty$ (no pure dephasing at all, a "T1-limited"
device in that lesson's language).

## Physical Interpretation

The Lindblad equation says decoherence is a *continuous drift*, not a
sequence of discrete jolts: the discrete Kraus channel was always a
coarse-grained snapshot of this same underlying continuous process, and
this lesson's exact-match calculation proves that snapshot loses no
information whatsoever about the dynamics, for any step size. The
$T_2\leq2T_1$ bound stops being an empirical hardware-datasheet rule and
becomes a structural consequence of how independent noise channels
combine: any two Lindblad jump operators contribute additively to every
coherence's decay rate, so adding a *new* noise source can only shrink
$T_2$ further, never lengthen it.

## Worked Example

**Verify $P_1(T_1)=e^{-1}$ using $N=2000$ discrete steps of size
$dt=T_1/N$, confirming the exact match holds even far from the
infinitesimal limit.**

With $T_1=2$, $dt=0.001$ (so $N=2000$ steps reach $t=T_1$),
$\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}\approx0.00049988$. Stepping
`amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma(dt))` $N=2000$ times via
`applyChannelRepeatedly` on $\rho=|1\rangle\langle1|$ gives
$\rho_{11}(N\,dt)\approx0.367879441$, matching $e^{-1}\approx0.367879441$
to 9 decimal places; this platform's own existing test suite already
confirms exactly this (T1 & T2 Decoherence cites it for $N=10,100,1000$;
here it's shown to hold for the coherence too, not only the population).
Starting instead from $\rho=|+\rangle\langle+|$ and taking just **one**
step of size $dt=0.001$: the off-diagonal entry shrinks from $0.5$ to
$0.5\,e^{-dt/(2T_1)}\approx0.499875$, matching
`applyChannelRepeatedly`'s single-step output exactly.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning">
**Thinking coherence decays at the same rate as population under pure
amplitude damping.** The derivation above shows the coherence decay
*rate* is exactly half the population decay rate ($\Gamma/2$ vs
$\Gamma$), which is precisely why pure amplitude damping alone gives
$T_2=2T_1$, not $T_2=T_1$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Assuming the Kraus-channel/Lindblad-equation match is only
approximate for small $dt$.** It's exact, for both channels covered
here, at *any* step size: the algebra above never used a small-$dt$
approximation once $\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}$ (the platform's own
`decayProbabilityForTimestep`) was substituted in; the first-order
$K_0,K_k$ construction earlier in this lesson is the *general* sufficiency
argument, not what's being used for this specific numerical check.
</Callout>

## Summary

- The GKLS/Lindblad equation
  $\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_k(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\})$
  is the most general Markovian, completely-positive generator (stated
  as a cited structural theorem); this lesson proved the converse:
  that it genuinely generates valid Kraus-form dynamics at every
  infinitesimal step.
- Solved exactly for amplitude damping: $\rho_{11}(t)=\rho_{11}(0)e^{-t/T_1}$,
  $\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/(2T_1)}$, coherence decays at exactly
  half the population's rate.
- This platform's existing discrete `amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma(dt))`
  reproduces this continuous solution **exactly** at $t=dt$, for both
  population and coherence, at any step size, not an approximation.
- $T_2\leq2T_1$ is derived, not just stated: independent Lindblad jump
  operators contribute additively to a coherence's total decay rate.
