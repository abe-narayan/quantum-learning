## From Discrete Kraus Steps to a Generator

Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators built the discrete Kraus picture
of decoherence and explicitly declined to build the continuous-time
differential equation underneath it, noting that "no lesson in this
course needs it." This course does. The **Lindblad master equation**
(more precisely, the **GKLS equation**, after Gorini, Kossakowski,
Sudarshan, and Lindblad) is the continuous-time generator of the kind
of Markovian, completely-positive evolution this platform's Kraus
channels already implement at discrete steps. What this lesson proves is
sharper than an analogy: one discrete Kraus step is an exact sample of
the continuous flow, at any step size, not an approximation to it.

Primary sources: V. Gorini, A.
Kossakowski, and E. C. G. Sudarshan, "Completely Positive Dynamical
Semigroups of N-Level Systems," *J. Math. Phys.* **17**, 821 (1976),
and G. Lindblad, "On the Generators of Quantum Dynamical Semigroups,"
*Commun. Math. Phys.* **48**, 119–130 (1976), who derived the boxed
generator's general form independently and in the same year.

## The Most General Markovian CP Generator

### What a Markovian CP generator must look like

A **quantum dynamical semigroup** is a family $\{\Lambda_t\}_{t\geq0}$ of
CPTP maps with $\Lambda_0=\mathbb1$, $\Lambda_{t+s}=\Lambda_t\Lambda_s$
(Markovian: no memory of the past beyond the current state). Writing
$\Lambda_t=\mathbb1+\mathcal Lt+O(t^2)$ for small $t$ defines the
**generator** $\mathcal L$, so that $\dot\rho=\mathcal L(\rho)$. The
**GKLS theorem** (cited here as a deep structural result, not re-derived
in full generality; its complete proof requires a careful limiting
argument on the Choi matrix of $\Lambda_t$ as $t\to0$, beyond this
lesson's scope) states that *any* generator of a completely positive,
trace-preserving Markovian semigroup necessarily has the form

<TheoremBox title="GKLS (Lindblad) equation" provenance="cited">

**Hypotheses.** $\mathcal H$ is finite-dimensional, and
$\{\Lambda_t\}_{t\geq0}$ is a CPTP semigroup that is *uniformly*
continuous, meaning $\|\Lambda_t-\mathbb1\|\to0$ in operator norm as
$t\to0$. That is what makes the generator $\mathcal L$ a bounded operator
with a power series, and in finite dimensions it comes free with
continuity. In infinite dimensions it does not, and the difference is
real: a damped harmonic oscillator's semigroup on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ is
only *strongly* continuous, its generator is unbounded, and the boxed
form then holds only formally, on a dense domain, with the sum over $k$
requiring its own convergence argument. Every qubit in this lesson is
safely finite-dimensional.

$$
\boxed{\dot\rho = \mathcal L(\rho) = -i[H,\rho] + \sum_k\left(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger - \frac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}\right)}
$$

for some Hermitian $H$ (an effective Hamiltonian) and some set of
**<Term id="lindblad-master-equation">Lindblad (jump) operators</Term>** $\{L_k\}$.

</TheoremBox>

What *is* proved here directly
is the converse, sufficiency direction: that this form does generate
valid CPTP dynamics, by connecting it explicitly to the
discrete Kraus picture already built and trusted on this platform.

### Sufficiency: recovering a Kraus form at first order in $dt$

Exponentiate $\mathcal L$ over one short step $dt$:
$\rho(t+dt)\approx\rho(t)+\mathcal L(\rho)\,dt$. Group terms into

$$
\rho(t+dt) \approx K_0\rho K_0^\dagger + \sum_{k\geq1}K_k\rho K_k^\dagger, \qquad K_0 = I + \Big(-iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\Big)dt, \qquad K_k = \sqrt{dt}\,L_k\ (k\geq1)
$$

Check trace-preservation to $O(dt)$: $K_0^\dagger K_0\approx I+\big(iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\big)dt+\big(-iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\big)dt=I-dt\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k$
(the $\pm iH$ terms cancel, since $H$ is Hermitian), and
$\sum_{k\geq1}K_k^\dagger K_k=dt\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k$, so
$K_0^\dagger K_0+\sum_{k\geq1}K_k^\dagger K_k=I+O(dt^2)$: the
trace-preservation condition Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators
required, satisfied to first order. This is already a Kraus sum
(hence CP, by the easy direction of Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi's
theorem), confirming the boxed generator produces valid physical
dynamics at every infinitesimal step.

### Solving it exactly for amplitude damping

Take $H=0$ and a single jump operator $L=\sqrt\Gamma\,\sigma_-$, where
$\sigma_-=|0\rangle\langle1|$ (the lowering operator: $\sigma_-|1\rangle=|0\rangle$)
and $\Gamma\equiv1/T_1$. For $\rho=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}&\rho_{01}\\\rho_{10}&\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}$,
direct computation gives $\sigma_-\rho\sigma_-^\dagger=\rho_{11}|0\rangle\langle0|$
and $\sigma_-^\dagger\sigma_-=|1\rangle\langle1|$, so

$$
\dot\rho = \Gamma\left(\sigma_-\rho\sigma_-^\dagger-\tfrac12\{\sigma_-^\dagger\sigma_-,\rho\}\right) = \Gamma\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{11} & -\rho_{01}/2\\ -\rho_{10}/2 & -\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix}
$$

giving two decoupled linear ODEs: $\dot\rho_{11}=-\Gamma\rho_{11}$ and
$\dot\rho_{01}=-\tfrac\Gamma2\rho_{01}$, with **exact** closed-form
solutions

$$
\rho_{11}(t) = \rho_{11}(0)\,e^{-\Gamma t} = \rho_{11}(0)\,e^{-t/T_1}, \qquad \rho_{01}(t) = \rho_{01}(0)\,e^{-\Gamma t/2} = \rho_{01}(0)\,e^{-t/(2T_1)}
$$

The population decays exactly as T1 & T2 Decoherence defined it. The
*coherence* decays at exactly **half** that rate in the exponent; this
single fact, derived here for the first time on this platform, is about
to reproduce the $T_2\leq2T_1$ bound exactly.

### The discrete channel is this solution, evaluated at $t=dt$: exactly, not approximately

Apply `amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma)` (Kraus operators
$K_0=\text{diag}(1,\sqrt{1-\gamma})$, $K_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt\gamma\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$)
to a general $\rho$ directly:
$\rho'_{11}=\rho_{11}(1-\gamma)$, $\rho'_{01}=\rho_{01}\sqrt{1-\gamma}$
(both derived the same way Open Quantum Systems & Kraus Operators
derived trace-preservation for this channel). Substitute
`decayProbabilityForTimestep`'s exact identity $\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}$,
so $1-\gamma=e^{-dt/T_1}$:

$$
\rho'_{11} = \rho_{11}\,e^{-dt/T_1}, \qquad \rho'_{01} = \rho_{01}\sqrt{e^{-dt/T_1}} = \rho_{01}\,e^{-dt/(2T_1)}
$$

**These are exactly the continuous solutions above, evaluated at
$t=dt$**, both the population *and* the coherence, not just the
population T1 & T2 Decoherence already checked. One discrete Kraus step
with $\gamma=\gamma(dt)$ isn't an approximation to the Lindblad flow;
it's a single exact sample of it, true for whatever step size $dt$ is
chosen, matching this platform's engine to full numerical precision:

### Deriving $T_2\leq2T_1$ from the generator's linearity

Add a *second*, independent jump operator for pure dephasing,
$L'=\sqrt{\kappa}\,\sigma_z$ (no population exchange: check
$\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z-\rho$ has zero diagonal, verified the same way
Quantum Channels: Kraus and Choi checked structure directly). Its
contribution to $\dot\rho_{01}$ works out to $-2\kappa\rho_{01}$, an
independent exponential channel with its own rate $1/T_{2,\text{pure}}=2\kappa$.
Because $\mathcal L$ is **linear** in its jump operators, running both
processes together simply *adds* their individual contributions to
$\dot\rho_{01}$:

<TheoremBox title="The T2 ≤ 2T1 bound" provenance="derived">

**Hypotheses.** A single qubit with $H=0$ and exactly two jump
operators, $L=\sqrt\Gamma\,\sigma_-$ and $L'=\sqrt\kappa\,\sigma_z$ with
$\Gamma,\kappa\geq0$. The bound survives adding further jump operators,
since any additional $L''$ can only contribute a non-negative extra rate
to $\dot\rho_{01}$, but it does assume the dynamics is Markovian: a
non-Markovian bath can transiently return coherence and violate this as
an instantaneous statement.

$$
\dot\rho_{01} = -\Big(\tfrac{\Gamma}{2}+2\kappa\Big)\rho_{01} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac1{T_2} = \frac1{2T_1} + \frac1{T_{2,\text{pure}}}
$$

Since $1/T_{2,\text{pure}}\geq0$ always, $1/T_2\geq1/(2T_1)$, i.e.
$\boxed{T_2\leq2T_1}$.

</TheoremBox>

This bound is **derived** from the Lindblad generator's
structure, which is what turns the Markovian proviso T1 & T2
Decoherence could only assert into a hypothesis you can watch being
spent. Equality holds exactly when
$T_{2,\text{pure}}\to\infty$ (no pure dephasing at all, a "T1-limited"
device in that lesson's language).

The rate equation also fixes what a datasheet number may be fed into
which generator, and this is the one place the distinction bites in
practice. $T_2$ is the *total* coherence time, not the pure-dephasing
mechanism: it is $1/T_2=1/(2T_1)+1/T_{2,\text{pure}}$ already summed.
So the dephasing jump operator's constant is $T_{2,\text{pure}}$, and
handing it a measured $T_2$ while an amplitude-damping generator with
that device's $T_1$ is also running counts the relaxation contribution
twice and predicts coherence decaying faster than the device does. The
same caution applies to this platform's `dephasingChannel`, whose
strength parameter reproduces a measured $T_2$ envelope only when it acts
alone.

## Decoherence as Continuous Drift

The Lindblad equation says decoherence is a *continuous drift*, not a
sequence of discrete jolts: the discrete Kraus channel was always a
coarse-grained snapshot of this same underlying continuous process, and
this lesson's exact-match calculation proves that snapshot loses no
information whatsoever about the dynamics, for any step size. The
$T_2\leq2T_1$ bound stops being an empirical hardware-datasheet rule and
becomes a structural consequence of how independent noise channels
combine: any two Lindblad jump operators contribute additively to every
coherence's decay rate, so adding a *new* noise source can only shrink
$T_2$ further, never lengthen it.

## The Discrete Channel Recovered Exactly

**Verify $P_1(T_1)=e^{-1}$ using $N=2000$ discrete steps of size
$dt=T_1/N$, confirming the exact match holds even far from the
infinitesimal limit.**

With $T_1=2$, $dt=0.001$ (so $N=2000$ steps reach $t=T_1$),
$\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}\approx0.00049988$. Stepping
`amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma(dt))` $N=2000$ times via
`applyChannelRepeatedly` on $\rho=|1\rangle\langle1|$ gives
$\rho_{11}(N\,dt)\approx0.367879441$, matching $e^{-1}\approx0.367879441$
to 9 decimal places; this platform's own existing test suite already
confirms this (T1 & T2 Decoherence cites it for $N=10,100,1000$;
here it's shown to hold for the coherence as well).
Starting instead from $\rho=|+\rangle\langle+|$ and taking just **one**
step of size $dt=0.001$: the off-diagonal entry shrinks from $0.5$ to
$0.5\,e^{-dt/(2T_1)}\approx0.499875$, matching
`applyChannelRepeatedly`'s single-step output exactly.

## Where the Factor of Two Comes From

<Callout type="warning">
**Thinking coherence decays at the same rate as population under pure
amplitude damping.** The derivation above shows the coherence decay
*rate* is exactly half the population decay rate ($\Gamma/2$ vs
$\Gamma$), which is why pure amplitude damping alone gives
$T_2=2T_1$, not $T_2=T_1$.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
**Assuming the Kraus-channel/Lindblad-equation match is only
approximate for small $dt$.** It's exact, for both channels covered
here, at *any* step size: the algebra above never used a small-$dt$
approximation once $\gamma(dt)=1-e^{-dt/T_1}$ (the platform's own
`decayProbabilityForTimestep`) was substituted in; the first-order
$K_0,K_k$ construction earlier in this lesson is the *general* sufficiency
argument, not what's being used for this specific numerical check.
</Callout>

## Practice Questions

1. Derive $\dot\rho_{00}$ from the amplitude-damping Lindblad equation
   above, and confirm $\rho_{00}(t)+\rho_{11}(t)=1$ is preserved for all
   $t$.

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   $\dot\rho_{00}=+\Gamma\rho_{11}$, the exact mirror of
   $\dot\rho_{11}=-\Gamma\rho_{11}$, so the two rates cancel and the trace
   never moves.

   Take the $(0,0)$ entry of each term separately, with
   $\sigma_-=|0\rangle\langle1|$ and $\sigma_-^\dagger\sigma_-=|1\rangle\langle1|$.

   The jump term contributes everything:
   $\sigma_-\rho\sigma_-^\dagger=|0\rangle\langle1|\rho|1\rangle\langle0|=\rho_{11}|0\rangle\langle0|$,
   whose $(0,0)$ entry is $\rho_{11}$.

   The anticommutator term contributes nothing:
   $\{|1\rangle\langle1|,\rho\}$ has $(0,0)$ entry
   $\langle0|1\rangle\langle1|\rho|0\rangle+\langle0|\rho|1\rangle\langle1|0\rangle=0$,
   since $\langle0|1\rangle=0$ both times.

   So $\dot\rho_{00}=\Gamma\rho_{11}$, matching the matrix printed above.
   Integrating with $\rho_{11}(t)=\rho_{11}(0)e^{-\Gamma t}$,

   $$
   \rho_{00}(t)=\rho_{00}(0)+\rho_{11}(0)\left(1-e^{-\Gamma t}\right),
   $$

   and adding the two,

   $$
   \rho_{00}(t)+\rho_{11}(t)=\rho_{00}(0)+\rho_{11}(0)-\rho_{11}(0)e^{-\Gamma t}+\rho_{11}(0)e^{-\Gamma t}=1.
   $$

   Every bit of population that leaves $|1\rangle$ arrives in $|0\rangle$,
   which is what a decay process should do and what the $\sigma_-$ jump
   operator was chosen to say.

   The more useful observation is that this is not a special property of
   amplitude damping. Trace preservation is built into the shape of the
   GKLS generator for *any* $H$ and any $\{L_k\}$:

   $$
   \mathrm{Tr}\,\mathcal L(\rho)=\underbrace{-i\,\mathrm{Tr}[H,\rho]}_{=\,0}
   +\sum_k\Big(\underbrace{\mathrm{Tr}(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger)}_{=\,\mathrm{Tr}(L_k^\dagger L_k\rho)}
   -\tfrac12\underbrace{\mathrm{Tr}\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}}_{=\,2\,\mathrm{Tr}(L_k^\dagger L_k\rho)}\Big)=0,
   $$

   using cyclicity of the trace on the jump term and
   $\mathrm{Tr}\{A,\rho\}=2\,\mathrm{Tr}(A\rho)$ on the anticommutator. The
   factor of $\tfrac12$ in the boxed equation is there for exactly this
   reason: it is the unique coefficient that makes the anticommutator's
   trace cancel the jump term's. Change it and the equation stops
   describing probability. That is worth knowing when reading the boxed
   form, because the $\tfrac12$ otherwise looks like a convention.

   </details>

2. Using the dephasing-only Lindblad equation ($\Gamma=0$,
   $\kappa=1/(2T_{2,\text{pure}})$), verify that populations $\rho_{00},\rho_{11}$
   are exactly constant in time, consistent with "pure" dephasing
   meaning no energy exchange.

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   With $H=0$, $\Gamma=0$ and the single jump operator
   $L'=\sqrt\kappa\,\sigma_z$, note first that $\sigma_z^\dagger\sigma_z=I$,
   so the anticommutator term collapses to
   $-\tfrac12\kappa\{I,\rho\}=-\kappa\rho$. The generator is therefore

   $$
   \dot\rho=\kappa\left(\sigma_z\rho\,\sigma_z-\rho\right).
   $$

   Now compute $\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z$ entry by entry. Conjugating by
   $\sigma_z=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$ multiplies the $(i,j)$ entry by
   $(-1)^i(-1)^j$, so the diagonal is untouched and the off-diagonals flip
   sign:

   $$
   \sigma_z\rho\,\sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{00}&-\rho_{01}\\-\rho_{10}&\rho_{11}\end{pmatrix},
   \qquad
   \dot\rho=\kappa\begin{pmatrix}0&-2\rho_{01}\\-2\rho_{10}&0\end{pmatrix}.
   $$

   So $\dot\rho_{00}=\dot\rho_{11}=0$ exactly, at every instant and for
   every initial state, while $\dot\rho_{01}=-2\kappa\rho_{01}$ gives
   $\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-2\kappa t}=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/T_{2,\rm pure}}$
   with $\kappa=1/(2T_{2,\rm pure})$, which is the rate the lesson quotes.

   "Exactly constant" is worth pausing on: this is not a decay so slow it
   is negligible, it is a derivative that is identically zero. The
   structural reason is that $\sigma_z$ is diagonal in the population
   basis, so $\sigma_z|i\rangle\langle i|\sigma_z=|i\rangle\langle i|$ and
   the jump leaves every population eigenstate exactly where it was. The
   channel is a random unitary, applying $Z$ with some probability and $I$
   otherwise, and $Z$ does nothing observable to a population.

   That is the precise sense in which the dephasing is "pure". If the
   qubit's own Hamiltonian is $H_0\propto\sigma_z$, then $[L',H_0]=0$, so
   $\langle H_0\rangle$ is a constant of the motion and the process
   exchanges no energy with the bath at all. It only randomizes the
   relative phase. Contrast amplitude damping, where $\sigma_-$ maps
   $|1\rangle$ to $|0\rangle$ and $\langle H_0\rangle$ falls: that process
   necessarily dumps energy somewhere.

   One caution about the basis. Populations are constant *in the
   $\sigma_z$ eigenbasis*, the one $L'$ was written in. Start from
   $|{+}\rangle$ and the populations in the $\sigma_x$ basis decay all the
   way to $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$; that is the same coherence decay, read in
   rotated coordinates. "Dephasing does not change populations" is a
   statement about a preferred basis, and the preferred basis is whichever
   one the jump operator is diagonal in.

   </details>

3. A device has $T_1=50\mu s$ and a measured $T_2=40\mu s$. Using
   $1/T_2=1/(2T_1)+1/T_{2,\text{pure}}$, solve for $T_{2,\text{pure}}$,
   the time constant of whatever additional dephasing process is present
   beyond amplitude damping alone.

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   $T_{2,\rm pure}=200/3\approx66.7\,\mu s$.

   Work in rates, which is the only way these combine cleanly:

   $$
   \frac{1}{T_{2,\rm pure}}=\frac{1}{T_2}-\frac{1}{2T_1}=\frac{1}{40}-\frac{1}{100}=0.025-0.010=0.015\ \mu s^{-1},
   $$

   $$
   T_{2,\rm pure}=\frac{1}{0.015}=66.67\ \mu s.
   $$

   Two consistency checks before trusting it. The answer must be positive,
   and it is, which is the same statement as $T_2\leq2T_1$: here
   $40\leq100$, comfortably. And $T_{2,\rm pure}$ must exceed $T_2$, since
   it describes only one of the two contributions to a total rate; $66.7>40$,
   as required.

   The engineering content is in the rate split, not the time. Of the total
   coherence decay rate $0.025\ \mu s^{-1}$, amplitude damping supplies
   $0.010$ and pure dephasing supplies $0.015$: sixty percent of this
   device's decoherence has nothing to do with energy relaxation. So
   lengthening $T_1$ is the wrong thing to work on. Even an infinite $T_1$
   would only lift $T_2$ from $40$ to $66.7\,\mu s$, whereas eliminating
   the dephasing source entirely would take it to $2T_1=100\,\mu s$.
   Rate additivity is what makes that comparison possible at a glance and
   is the practical reason the Lindblad picture is worth having on a
   hardware team.

   The wrong turn to avoid is averaging or subtracting the *times*:
   $2T_1-T_2=60\,\mu s$ is not $T_{2,\rm pure}$ and has no meaning. Decay
   rates add because independent exponential processes multiply their
   survival probabilities; time constants do not.

   </details>

4. Explain why the first-order Kraus construction
   ($K_0\approx I+(\cdots)dt$, $K_k\approx\sqrt{dt}L_k$) needed $H$ to be
   Hermitian specifically to make $K_0^\dagger K_0+\sum K_k^\dagger K_k=I+O(dt^2)$
   work out: what would go wrong with a non-Hermitian $H$?

   <details className="answer-reveal">
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Because the $\pm iH$ terms in $K_0^\dagger K_0$ cancel only if
   $H^\dagger=H$. Otherwise a term survives at first order in $dt$, and it
   is a term that changes the trace, meaning probability leaks away or is
   manufactured at a rate proportional to how non-Hermitian $H$ is.

   Do the expansion without assuming Hermiticity. With
   $K_0=I+\big(-iH-\tfrac12\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\big)dt$,

   $$
   K_0^\dagger K_0=I+\Big(iH^\dagger-iH-\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k\Big)dt+O(dt^2),
   $$

   and $\sum_{k\geq1}K_k^\dagger K_k=dt\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k$ exactly. The
   $L$ terms cancel between the two by construction, leaving

   $$
   K_0^\dagger K_0+\sum_{k\geq1}K_k^\dagger K_k=I+i\big(H^\dagger-H\big)\,dt+O(dt^2).
   $$

   Hermiticity is precisely the condition that kills the surviving term.

   Quantify the damage when it fails. Write $H=H_R+iH_I$ with $H_R,H_I$
   Hermitian; then $i(H^\dagger-H)=2H_I$, so

   $$
   \mathrm{Tr}\,\rho(t+dt)=\mathrm{Tr}\Big[\rho\Big(I+2H_I\,dt\Big)\Big]+O(dt^2)=1+2\,dt\,\langle H_I\rangle_\rho+O(dt^2).
   $$

   Total probability drifts at rate $2\langle H_I\rangle$, which is
   state-dependent, so it is not even a uniform rescaling one could
   normalize away. A second failure arrives at the same time: for
   non-Hermitian $H$, $(-i[H,\rho])^\dagger=-i[H^\dagger,\rho]\neq-i[H,\rho]$,
   so $\dot\rho$ stops being Hermitian and $\rho$ leaves the space of
   density matrices in a second, independent way.

   The instructive part is that non-Hermiticity is not forbidden here; it
   is already present, in a precisely metered amount. $K_0$ itself is the
   short-time propagator of the **effective Hamiltonian**

   $$
   H_{\rm eff}=H-\frac{i}{2}\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k,
   $$

   which is deliberately non-Hermitian, and the norm it loses is exactly
   the probability that a quantum jump occurred instead. That is the entire
   basis of the quantum-trajectory (Monte Carlo wavefunction) method:
   propagate with $H_{\rm eff}$, watch the norm decay, and interpret the
   deficit as the chance to apply an $L_k$ and renormalize.

   So the correct reading of the condition is not "the generator must be
   Hermitian". It is that the anti-Hermitian part of the generator must be
   exactly $-\tfrac{i}{2}\sum_kL_k^\dagger L_k$ and nothing more, because
   that is the amount the jump terms $\sum_{k\geq1}K_k\rho K_k^\dagger$ put
   back. Any extra anti-Hermitian piece in $H$ has no jump term to
   compensate it, and the books do not balance.

   </details>

## Summary

- The GKLS/Lindblad equation
  $\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_k(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\})$
  is the most general Markovian, completely-positive generator (stated
  as a cited structural theorem); this lesson proved the converse:
  that it generates valid Kraus-form dynamics at every
  infinitesimal step.
- Solved exactly for amplitude damping: $\rho_{11}(t)=\rho_{11}(0)e^{-t/T_1}$,
  $\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/(2T_1)}$, coherence decays at exactly
  half the population's rate.
- This platform's existing discrete `amplitudeDampingChannel(gamma(dt))`
  reproduces this continuous solution **exactly** at $t=dt$, for both
  population and coherence, at any step size.
- $T_2\leq2T_1$ is derived, not just stated: independent Lindblad jump
  operators contribute additively to a coherence's total decay rate.
