## The 60-second verdict

A newcomer landing on `/` sees a real, moving physics simulation next to one clear button — `Start here: What Is a Qubit?` — and one honest fallback (`Prefer rigor first?`). That first ten seconds is genuinely good: one thing to do, backed by a real computation, not a stock photo. Scrolling costs them nothing new to learn — six sections, each with one CTA and a live/illustrative figure — until "Quantum Mastery" and "Apex" openly say *this part isn't for you yet* ("for readers who've finished the core curriculum"). That is an honest, well-signposted structure, and it's the site's strongest asset.

The trouble starts the moment they click through. `Hero.tsx` and `MechanicsSection.tsx` already print bra-ket algebra (`c_a|a⟩ + c_b|b⟩`, `(|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2`) on the homepage itself, before any lesson has explained the notation. The first real lesson (`what-is-a-qubit.mdx`) is genuinely well-built pedagogy — but the *other* recommended starting point, the "rigor-first" path's actual lesson one (`complex-numbers-for-physics.mdx`), derives Euler's formula from a Taylor series with no scaffolding, on a course explicitly advertised as needing nothing beforehand. A 15-year-old with "decent algebra" does not reliably have Taylor series. That is a real, immediate blocker on one of the platform's two official front doors.

The site is not remotely kid-ified — I found zero emoji, mascots, streaks, badges-as-rewards, confetti, or "Great job!"-style copy anywhere in `src/content` or `src/components` (checked by pattern search across the whole tree; the only non-ASCII glyphs in that range are `✓`/`⚠` used as functional icons). If anything the balance is currently tilted the other way: the infrastructure for softening jargon exists (`src/components/mdx/Term.tsx`) but is *installed and unused* — zero of 219 lessons call it, including the flagship beginner lesson, which bolds "Dirac notation," "Born rule," and "collapse" as new vocabulary and links none of them anywhere. **[Resolved 2026-08-29 — see Blocker 2.]**

## Blockers — where a beginner gives up

**1. `src/content/lessons/quantum-mechanics/mathematical-foundations/complex-numbers-for-physics.mdx` — Taylor series assumed with no scaffolding, on the course with zero prerequisites.**
This is lesson one of `mathematical-foundations`, the only course `COURSES.filter(c => c.prerequisites.length === 0)` returns (see `src/app/learn/page.tsx:38`) — i.e., the site's own "rigor-first" front door, offered right on the homepage hero as the alternative to "What Is a Qubit?". Its second learning objective is "Derive Euler's formula from the Taylor series," and the `### Deriving Euler's formula` section (lines 158–182) opens with "The Taylor series for $e^x$ converges for complex inputs too, so substitute $x = i\theta$" and proceeds directly into a four-term series expansion with no definition of what a Taylor series is or why it exists. `/learn`'s own copy promises "strong high-school math" (`src/app/learn/page.tsx:86`); Taylor series is a calculus topic most strong-high-school-math students (including this persona) have not seen. A reader who takes the site's "prefer rigor first" option at face value hits this in the first 20 minutes and has no offramp — `lessonMeta.prerequisites` is `[]` (line 30), so `PrerequisiteReadout` correctly (and unhelpfully) tells them "No prerequisites. This is a starting point."

**2. Zero lessons use the new inline term-gloss component — including on its own bolded vocabulary.**
`src/components/mdx/Term.tsx` is real, well-built, accessible (checkbox+`:has()` disclosure, no floating-tooltip viewport problem, throws at render if the `id` doesn't exist in `GLOSSARY_TERMS`), and registered in `src/mdx-components.tsx:85` — but `grep -rn "<Term " src --include=*.mdx` returns nothing. Zero of 219 lessons call it. Concretely, in the site's own flagship beginner lesson, `src/content/lessons/quantum-computing/qubits-and-quantum-states/what-is-a-qubit.mdx`:
- Line 75: `We write a qubit's state using **Dirac notation**.` — bold, no link, even though `src/lib/content/glossary.ts:36` already has a `dirac-notation` entry sitting unused.
- Line 108: `the connection... is the **Born rule**` — bold, no link, and **no glossary entry for "Born rule" exists at all** (checked `glossary.ts` and `concepts.ts` directly; "Born rule" only appears parenthetically inside the `measurement` concept's definition text, not as its own addressable term).
- Line 112: `the superposition **collapses**` — same: bolded, no entry, no link.

So the one mechanism built specifically to solve "hit a word I don't know with no way to find out what it means" (the task's own diagnostic #4) is not reaching the terms that most need it, in the one lesson the whole site funnels beginners toward. `EquationReveal` (used in this same lesson, 32/219 lessons total) glosses math *symbols* (α, β, |0⟩) well, but it doesn't cover prose vocabulary — that's exactly the gap `Term` exists to close, and it isn't closing it yet.

> **Resolved 2026-08-29.** The finding above is left as written because it is what the tree looked like at review time; it no longer describes the tree. `<Term>` is now called **562 times across 191 of the 219 lessons** (recounted 2026-08-29), and all three lines named above are wrapped: `what-is-a-qubit.mdx` glosses `superposition`, `dirac-notation`, `amplitude`, `normalization`, `born-rule`, `wavefunction-collapse` and `bloch-sphere-term` inline. The specific gap "no glossary entry for Born rule exists at all" is closed too — `born-rule` is now an authored, `foundational`-level entry, and the glossary has grown from the top-heavy set this review describes to **272 terms with a deliberate beginner layer** (a `START_HERE_IDS` reading-order tier, and a `glossary.test.ts` case that fails if the foundational tier drops below 40 entries or if any of nine named beginner terms goes missing). The counts here are current as of this note; `grep -c '<Term' -r src/content/lessons` is the check.

**3. Bra-ket notation appears on the homepage itself, unexplained, before the first click.**
`src/components/home/Hero.tsx`'s field caption and `src/components/home/MechanicsSection.tsx:61` ("`c_a|a⟩ + c_b|b⟩`") and `ComputingSection.tsx:41` ("`(|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2`") print Dirac notation directly into homepage prose, above any explanation of what a ket is. A first-time visitor hasn't clicked anything yet and is already reading formalism. This is defensible as "a taste of the real thing" (the brief explicitly wants a research-console feel, not training wheels) but it does cost something: diagnostic #4 ("do I hit a word I don't know with no way to find out what it means") fails on the homepage itself, before the visitor has even chosen a path.

**4. The rigor-first course's difficulty label doesn't warn about the actual jump in kind.**
`mathematical-foundations` is tagged `difficulty: "foundational"` (`curriculum.ts:52`) — the *lowest* rung on `DifficultyMark`'s ladder, identical to `what-is-a-qubit`'s own "foundational" tag. Difficulty here is calibrated to "foundational within the curriculum," not "foundational for a reader with only high-school math," so the one at-a-glance instrument the site uses everywhere else to signal "how hard is this" (`DifficultyMark`, `PrerequisiteReadout`) gives this lesson the same badge as the gentle intuition-first lesson, even though the Taylor-series jump above is real. A beginner scanning the two "ways in" on `/learn` has no signal that one of the two "foundational" starting points requires calculus background the other doesn't.

**5. "Pillar" is the site's own organizing word, and it's never defined where it's first used.**
`Hero.tsx`'s `Readouts` prints `{ label: "Pillars", value: PILLARS.length }` (line 126) as the very first use of the word, with no gloss. The nav softens this well (labeled "Tracks" in `src/lib/nav.ts` and `Navbar.tsx`), and `CurriculumExplorer.tsx`'s own comment shows the team is aware of the problem ("grouped by plain-language weight... so the six-pillar progression reads as an order even to someone who doesn't know what a 'pillar' is," line 95) — but the raw word still surfaces first, undefined, in the homepage's own readout strip, and again as "Pillar 01/06" eyebrows throughout `/learn` and every pillar page. This is a persistent low-grade instance of exactly the jargon diagnostic #4 warns about, on a word the persona was told by name not to know.

## Friction — survivable but costly

- **Glossary coverage is skewed toward advanced material, away from the words a true beginner actually stalls on.** `src/lib/content/concepts.ts` and `src/lib/content/glossary.ts` together cover ~90 terms, but a scan of the ids shows heavy weighting toward research-adjacent vocabulary (`qubitization`, `naimark-dilation-theorem`, `oracle-relativization-barrier`, `barren-plateaus`) versus a thin layer at the very bottom (no dedicated entry for "amplitude," "Born rule," or "wavefunction collapse" as such — see Blocker 2). The glossary is excellent for an intermediate-to-advanced reader looking something up; it is not yet built out for the exact three or four words a total beginner hits first. **[Resolved 2026-08-29 — the two files now cover 272 terms (59 derived from `concepts.ts`, 213 authored in `glossary.ts`), every one of the four named gaps has a `foundational`-level entry, and the count is enforced from below rather than above: `glossary.test.ts` fails if the foundational tier drops under 40 entries. The skew this finding names is genuinely reversed, not merely diluted.]**
- **`complex-numbers-for-physics.mdx` line 33** ("Derive Euler's formula from the Taylor series") is stated as a learning objective before the lesson has taught what a Taylor series is anywhere in the corpus that precedes it — the term itself does have a glossary entry (`glossary.ts:367`, `taylor-series`) but, per Blocker 2, nothing links to it.
- **`RabiExplorer` and other Hardware-pillar simulators** (`src/components/simulators/rabi-explorer/RabiExplorer.tsx`) introduce "detuning Δ" and drive strength "V" via control labels and live KaTeX readouts with no inline gloss — reasonable for a reader who arrived via the Hardware pillar's own lessons, but the simulator is also directly linked from the homepage (`HardwareSection.tsx:117`, "Drive real qubit dynamics yourself in the Rabi Explorer") before any lesson has introduced detuning as a concept.
- **`CourseList.tsx:105-110`** prints a course's prerequisite titles as plain text ("Requires Mathematical Foundations for Quantum Mechanics") with no link — a reader who doesn't recognize a listed prerequisite has to go find it manually, unlike `PrerequisiteStatus`/`PrerequisiteReadout` elsewhere, which do link.
- **Lesson-page metadata stack is real but present.** `LessonLayout.tsx` puts breadcrumb → title → lede → difficulty+readouts → progress bar → "Before you start" prerequisite row → objectives list, all before the first prose heading. Each piece is well-justified individually (and the team has visibly already trimmed this once — see the "P2-6"/"Lineage wall" comments throughout `LessonLayout.tsx` and `PrerequisiteReadout.tsx`), but it is still five stacked instruments before "Motivation" — worth watching as more chrome accretes.

## Where it works — protect these

- **`src/components/home/Hero.tsx`**: the single-CTA pattern (`primaryStart`/`alternateStart` derived from real data, not hardcoded) is exactly right, and leads with `what-is-a-qubit` — a genuinely no-math lesson — rather than the graph-root course. This is the correct default and should not drift.
- **`src/content/lessons/quantum-computing/qubits-and-quantum-states/what-is-a-qubit.mdx`** itself is excellent pedagogy: coin-spinning analogy before formalism, an explicit "superposition is not classical uncertainty" callout, `EquationReveal` term chips on the one central equation, a `PredictBeforeReveal` prompt before the Bloch-sphere demo, and a `NextDiscovery` that's honest about what it deferred ("every example above could have used real numbers. That changes fast."). Its only real gap is the missing `<Term>` wraps noted in Blocker 2 — the prose structure itself is a model for the rest of the corpus.
- **`src/components/lessons/PrerequisiteReadout.tsx`**: a genuinely strong answer to "do I have what I need." Above the fold, direct links to each named prerequisite, done/not-done shown by shape (filled vs. hollow ring) and not color alone, and an honest "No prerequisites. This is a starting point." for the true entry points. This is the single best beginner-honesty mechanism on the site and should be the template for anything else that needs to gate a reader.
- **`src/app/courses/[slug]/page.tsx`**: now a real, complete answer to "what is this course, what do I need, where do I start" — prerequisites resolved and linked with live completion state (`PrerequisiteStatus.tsx`), a full module manifest showing authored vs. "Coming soon," and outcomes pulled from real lesson objectives rather than invented copy (`displayedOutcomes`, line 125). Course cards in `CourseList.tsx` are now genuinely whole-card clickable via a stretched `::after` link with correctly-layered per-module links beneath it (verified in code: `after:absolute after:inset-0` on the title link, `relative` on each module row so it paints above the stretched pseudo-element) — confirmed as a real fix, not cosmetic.
- **The simulators** (`src/app/simulators/page.tsx` + individual explorers): every instrument gets a "physics" sentence and a "why" sentence before the controls, e.g. `bloch-sphere`'s "Rotate a real qubit by hand and watch superposition collapse the instant you measure it." Individual explorers go further — `BlochSphereExplorer.tsx` has a live `aria-live` narration string ("Applied the H gate...", "Measured |1⟩ (this was random...)") and a `SimulatorFraming` block (`shows`/`watchFor`/`tryThis`) that answers all three of the task's simulator diagnostics directly, not just "here are some sliders." This is a strong, consistent pattern across the bench and should be preserved as new simulators are added.
- **`ApexHero.tsx` and the Apex/Mastery content**: reads as an actual research-preprint title block (running head, "FIG. 1" caption, a computed "prerequisites assumed" list derived from real course data, not hand-typed copy) rather than a de-fanged "advanced" section. `MasteryPage`'s QFT figure (`computeQftStructure`, `src/app/mastery/page.tsx:57`) is computed live through the platform's own tested `quantumFourierTransform`, and `qma-and-quantum-verification.mdx` runs real Hoeffding/Chernoff-bound arithmetic rather than gesturing at it. Nothing here reads sanded-down.
- **No kid-ification found anywhere.** Targeted searches across `src/content` and `src/components` for emoji (Unicode emoji ranges), mascots, streaks, points/XP, leaderboards, confetti, and "Great job!"/"You've got this!"-style copy all came back empty; the only symbols in the emoji Unicode range are functional ✓/⚠ glyphs. `DailyPuzzle`/`DailyPuzzleClient` ("Problem of the Day") is the closest thing to a habit-loop feature on the site, and it's implemented as a plain instrument readout with no streak counter, no reward animation, and identical content for every visitor on a given date — restrained by design.
- **Recoverability is solid.** `Navbar.tsx` keeps `/learn`, a grouped "Tracks" dropdown, and a persistent "Start learning" button on every page; `/glossary` is one click away from anywhere via the main nav (`src/lib/nav.ts`), not buried.

## Expert-credibility check

Nothing found here would make a graduate student close the tab. The strongest signals: `ApexHero.tsx`'s preprint-styled composition; `MasteryPage`'s live-computed QFT phase structure; problem sets with real Hoeffding-bound numerics (`qma-and-quantum-verification.mdx`); the CHSH Bell test, syndrome-extraction, and period-finding simulators all doing real numerical work rather than canned animation (per their own docstrings, cross-checked against the actual math imports in `RabiExplorer.tsx`/`BlochSphereExplorer.tsx` — `exactTwoLevelTrajectory`, `applySingleQubitGate`, `measure` are real quantum-engine calls, not scripted values). The one soft spot for credibility is the flip side of Blocker 3/5: a grad student skimming the homepage will notice bra-ket notation and the word "pillar" used loosely before any of it is grounded — not damaging, but it reads slightly more like marketing copy than the research-console register the rest of the site earns.

## Verdict

The site is not failing toward the children's-app end — it has essentially zero symptoms of that (see Kid-ification, above), and Apex/Mastery are genuinely research-depth, not sanded down. Where it is currently failing is the beginner side, and specifically in a narrow, fixable way: the *structural* beginner-honesty machinery (`PrerequisiteReadout`, `/courses/<slug>`, the hero's single CTA, the two-honest-paths pattern on `/learn`) is now strong, but the *lexical* layer — the actual words a newcomer doesn't know, at the exact moment they hit them — is not yet caught by the tools built for it. `Term.tsx` exists, is well-designed, and is used nowhere; the glossary is deep on advanced terms and thin on the three or four a total beginner needs first; and the "rigor-first" front door assumes calculus the site's own copy says isn't required. (The first two clauses were fixed after this review — see the resolution note under Blocker 2. The third stands.) None of this requires re-architecture — it's a content-and-wiring gap, not a design one — but as written today, a genuinely math-comfortable-but-quantum-naive 15-year-old following the site's own "prefer rigor first" recommendation would stall inside the first lesson.
